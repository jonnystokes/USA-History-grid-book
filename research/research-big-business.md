## Parked from `marketplace` research (2026-08-08) — chain-store scale
`marketplace` leads the shopping experience; the size, the backlash, and the law are yours.
- **A&P**: partnership formed 1859 (Gilman and Hartford); about 70 stores by 1878, nearly 200 by 1900, 1,600 by 1915 after the 1912 "economy store" format, and **about 15,000 stores with $2.9 billion in sales by 1930**, when it was the largest retailer in the world. Largest U.S. grocery retailer 1915–1975; largest U.S. retailer of any kind until 1965. (A&P company histories; Encyclopedia.com; FundingUniverse.) This scale is the backdrop to the anti-chain-store politics of the 1920s–30s.
- **F. W. Woolworth Co.**: merger agreements signed November 12, 1911 (incorporation 1912) combining four rival chains, opening with **596 stores and $65 million in capital**, then the world's largest merchandising operation. (Library of Congress, *This Month in Business History*.)
- **1962**: Kmart (March 1), Target (May 1), Wal-Mart (July 2), and Woolco all opened their first discount stores in the same year. Three of the four were older store companies remaking themselves — Kresge, Dayton's, and Woolworth. (Forbes; Britannica; corporate histories.)
