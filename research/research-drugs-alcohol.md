### 5. 1750 to 1800
- **The tax:** 1791 federal excise on distilled spirits, proposed by Hamilton to help carry the Revolutionary War debt — **7 cents a gallon on domestic spirits** (10 cents on spirits from foreign materials). Small frontier distillers paid effectively higher rates than big eastern ones and had to pay in scarce cash — the grievance behind the revolt. (EBSCO "Whiskey tax of 1791"; TTB.gov "The Whiskey Rebellion.")
- **The rebellion:** resistance climaxed **July 1794** — an armed crowd besieged and burned tax inspector John Neville's home at Bower Hill (a two-day fight). Washington called up militia from four states — **about 13,000 men** (sources give 12,000–13,000) — and rode out with them part of the way, the only time a sitting president has led an army in the field. The rebels dispersed without a battle; about 150 arrests, 20 leaders taken east; two convicted of treason and pardoned by Washington. (Mount Vernon Digital Encyclopedia "Whiskey Rebellion"; Gilder Lehrman; TTB.gov.)
- **Whiskey as money:** backcountry farmers turned corn into whiskey because a jug traveled where a wagon of corn could not; whiskey served as a medium of exchange in cash-poor western settlements — your lead (also noted in `food-farming`'s 1750–1800 section). `economy` keeps one span on the tax as war-debt revenue; `government-politics` the federal-power proof.
