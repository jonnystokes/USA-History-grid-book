## Parked from `home-family` (2026-08-07) — a real probate inventory for your `probate-inventory-colonist` target (era 03, 1600s)
- **Mary Ring, Plymouth Colony** — died July 1631; will proved October 28, 1633; estate inventory recorded in Plymouth Colony wills vol. 1:4–6 and transcribed by the **Plymouth Colony Archive Project** ("Probate Inventory for Estate of Mary Ring," histarch.illinois.edu/plymouth/Pring.html). The inventory lists her household goods item by item — including textiles and clothing (the transcription carries colors and fabrics; pull the exact clothing lines from the transcription when this chapter is worked — the histarch page was intermittently unreachable during the home-family pass, so the clothing items were NOT quoted there). Also lists books (Bible, psalmbook, named religious titles) and debts owed her by William Bradford and Edward Winslow. (Sources: Pilgrim Hall Museum "Mary Ring" — with downloadable will/inventory PDFs; Plymouth Colony Archive Project.)

## Parked from technology research (2026-08-08) — Bakelite dates for the "made of" thread
`technology` leads on how plastics were made; this chapter has the look and what things were made of (and the wallace-carothers nylon story). Web-verified 2026-08-08:
- **Bakelite:** Leo Baekeland made the first fully synthetic plastic (phenol + formaldehyde) in 1907; process patent filed July 1907, granted December 7, 1909; ACS National Historic Chemical Landmark as the first commercial synthetic plastic; by Baekeland's death (1944) it was in over 15,000 products — radios, phones, jewelry, handles. (Sources: Science History Institute "Leo Hendrik Baekeland"; Britannica "Bakelite"; ACS landmark program.)
