## Parked from `work-workers` (2026-08-07) — Labor Day (era 7, 1850–1900)

- **The first Labor Day parade: Tuesday, September 5, 1882, New York City**, organized by the Central Labor Union — a workers' holiday invented by the labor movement itself, years before any government adopted it. (Sources: U.S. Department of Labor "History of Labor Day"; U.S. House history "The First Labor Day".)
- **Labor Day became a federal holiday on June 28, 1894**, when President Grover Cleveland signed the law making the first Monday in September a national holiday — days into the federal government's breaking of the Pullman strike (troops were in Chicago that same week; about 30 strikers died nationwide). Any telling of why the date exists can note the timing plainly. (Sources: U.S. DOL "History of Labor Day"; Britannica "Labor Day".)
- Who founded it is disputed — credit goes to either Peter J. McGuire (carpenters) or Matthew Maguire (machinists); the DOL history page itself presents both. State the dispute; do not pick one.
