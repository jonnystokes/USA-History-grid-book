### Era 08 (1900–1950)
- **The Cuyahoga River fire of 1912 killed five men** — the deadliest of the river's at least 13 fires between 1868 and 1969; the 1952 fire was the costliest ($1.3 million damage); the famous 1969 fire (which `land-environment` leads on as the pollution-to-law story) lasted only about 20–30 minutes and killed no one. If this chapter wants an industrial-river-fire calamity, 1912 is the one with the dead. (Sources: Case Western Reserve Encyclopedia of Cleveland History "Cuyahoga River Fire"; Smithsonian magazine.)
- **Black Sunday, April 14, 1935** — the worst single dust storm of the Dust Bowl: a black wall of soil moving about 60 mph across the southern Plains, day turned to night. `land-environment` owns the soil/plowing cause; `migration` owns the exodus; the storm as a survived calamity (people caught on roads, in cars, in fields) is this chapter's angle. (Sources: NWS Norman "The Black Sunday Dust Storm of April 14, 1935"; PBS American Experience Dust Bowl timeline.)

### Era 10 (2000–today)
- **The Camp Fire, November 8, 2018** — deadliest U.S. wildfire in a century: 85 people killed; the town of Paradise, California essentially destroyed — about 19,000 structures, roughly 11,000 of them homes; NIST built a minute-by-minute timeline of the fire and evacuation (useful for a rescue-angle telling: clogged evacuation routes, people sheltering in parking lots). `land-environment` tells the fuel-and-fire-policy cause (fire suppression debt + warming); this chapter gets the catastrophe and response. (Sources: Britannica "Camp Fire of 2018"; NIST "New Timeline of Deadliest California Wildfire," 2021; Butte County.)
- **2020 California fire siege** — about 4.2 million acres burned (~4% of the state), 33 deaths, $7–13 billion damage; the August Complex passed 1 million acres (first modern "gigafire"). (Sources: CAL FIRE 2020 incident archive; CalMatters "California's 2020 fire siege: wildfires by the numbers" — journalism.)
- **Santa Barbara oil spill, January–February 1969** — well blowout off the coast blackened California beaches; with the Cuyahoga fire that year it pushed the environmental lawmaking wave (`land-environment` leads on the law story; the spill as disaster-and-cleanup is available here). (Source: covered in EPA/NPS histories of 1969–70.)

## Parked from `energy` (2026-08-08) — Three Mile Island and the Texas freeze
- **Three Mile Island (yours to lead):** March 28, 1979, TMI-2 partial core meltdown, the worst U.S. commercial nuclear accident. Gov. Thornburgh's March 30 advisory: pregnant women and preschool children within 5 miles should leave; ~**140,000** residents self-evacuated amid conflicting official messages. NRC and 12+ epidemiological studies since 1981: no detectable health effects, **no deaths attributed** — the fear and confusion are equally documented. (NRC Backgrounder; World Nuclear Association; Dickinson College TMI archive.) **Film:** *Meltdown: Three Mile Island* (2022, Netflix, 4-part documentary, dir. Kief Davidson). `energy` keeps only the policy effect (new-plant orders stopped; no newly built U.S. reactor until Vogtle 3, 2023).
- **Texas winter storm Uri (Feb 2021):** 4.5M+ households (~10 million people) lost power, some for days; ERCOT said the grid was **4 minutes 37 seconds** from total collapse; official state death toll **246** (Texas DSHS, 2022) with researchers arguing a substantial undercount; damage estimates $195B+. (FERC/NERC final report; UT Energy Institute; Houston Public Media/KUT — journalism.) Coordinate lead with `energy` at prose time: energy = the grid failing as a system; yours = the storm and the deaths.

### 8. 1900 to 1950
- **Quebec Bridge collapse, August 29, 1907:** the cantilever span failed during construction, killing 75 workers, **33 of them Mohawk ironworkers from Kahnawake** — a community catastrophe that reshaped the high-steel tradition (afterward Kahnawake crews spread across many jobs rather than concentrating on one). (NFB *High Steel* materials; standard Quebec Bridge histories — verify the 75/33 split against a primary source when this chapter is worked.)
- **Golden Gate Bridge scaffold failure, February 17, 1937:** a work platform tore loose and ripped through the safety net; ten men died — the bridge's eleven total deaths came almost all in this one event, three months before opening. (Golden Gate Bridge district history; Half Way to Hell Club sources.)
