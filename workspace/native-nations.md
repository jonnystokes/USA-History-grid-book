Jon has ruled this goes in, and ruled HOW. The research bank has nothing on it at all
(no hit for "steriliz"), so a research pass must come first. One agent, on its own.

**What the research must establish, with sources:**
1. What the Indian Health Service did in the 1970s, on what scale, and under what
   consent conditions. The 1976 GAO study is the usual starting point; find what it
   actually examined and what it found, and what later scholarship establishes.
2. **The methods used** — this is the part Jon specifically requires and the bank must
   carry it. If surgical, what was cut, tied or removed. If a drug or device, what it
   did biologically. Sourced, and where the record does not say, recorded as not said.
3. Consent: what women were told, what they signed, what they were not told, and the
   circumstances (during labour, tied to benefits, language barriers).
4. Named women who have spoken on the record, if any exist in published accounts. Under
   the naming rule: named where the record names them, unnamed otherwise, and never a
   composite.

**How the book must then write it — `control/hard-subjects-policy.md` §3b, the
clinical-word rule.** "Sterilization" is a container word a child cannot see inside.
The prose must say, in plain words, that it means the person can never become pregnant
again, and by what method it was done. Honest and non-sexual, described as a doctor's
report would, not lurid and not lingering. Jon's reason: the point of reading the hard
parts is to understand them and be moved by them, so they are remembered and not
repeated — a sanitized version fails at exactly that.
