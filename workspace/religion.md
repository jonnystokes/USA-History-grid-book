## Left unsettled on purpose (eras 5–7) — kept out of the outline rather than hedged
- **The date of the St. George's walkout in Philadelphia.** Richard Allen's own account gives no date. Tradition says 1787; Richard S. Newman, *Freedom's Prophet* (NYU Press, 2008), 63–68, argues 1792–93; the Encyclopedia of Greater Philadelphia says "the early 1790s." The outline names the disagreement instead of picking one. (Bank §5d.)
- **Absalom Jones's ordination as priest: 1802 or 1804.** The Archives of the Episcopal Church say 1802; Delaware's state historical marker says 1804. The outline gives 1802 and names the marker's 1804. (Bank §5e.)
- **The exact day of Massachusetts's 1833 disestablishment.** The state's own published constitution gives Amendment Article XI with no date. The outline says 1833 and no day. **Do not write "11 November 1833."** (Bank §5c.)
- **The 1884 parish-school decree's "within two years" requirement, and the claim that parents were bound to send their children.** The decrees were published in Latin; the Memorial Volume does not contain them and no accessible primary text was found. Only the Pastoral Letter's own wording is used. (Bank §7a.)
- **The staffing claim** — "nuns and brothers teaching for almost no pay, which is what made a second school system affordable." Not sourced. Cut from the outline. (Bank §7a.)
- **Total dead in the 1844 Philadelphia riots.** No source gives a figure for both riots together. The outline gives the counted dead per riot and says no reliable total exists. (Bank §6d.)
- **Andrew Bryan's whipping.** Georgia's state historical marker and the New Georgia Encyclopedia both record that he was jailed and whipped for preaching; neither gives a number of lashes. The outline says so. Sources also disagree on his baptism year (1777, 1781, 1782) and on his birth year, so the outline uses only the ordination date of 20 January 1788, which they agree on. (Bank §5e2.)

## Left unsettled on purpose (eras 9–10) — kept out of the outline rather than hedged
- **Why the *Epperson* justices struck the Arkansas law down.** The Encyclopedia of Arkansas says the ruling was unanimous. The printed opinion shows separate opinions by Black, Harlan and Stewart, and Black saying he could not agree with the Court's reasoning about the voters' motive. The outline says no justice voted to keep the law and that they did not agree on why. (Bank §9d.)
- **Alice Moore's denomination.** e-WV says "the wife of a fundamentalist minister"; West Virginia Public Broadcasting says "the wife of a Church of Christ preacher." Both are recorded in the bank; the outline says only "the wife of a minister." (Bank §9e.)
- **Casualties in the Kanawha County fight of 1974.** The record has gunfire, firebombs and dynamite attacks on schools, and no accessible source gives a count of the injured or says whether anyone was killed. The outline says so in as many words rather than implying nobody was hurt. (Bank §9e.)
- **The Chattanooga rope story about Billy Graham.** The often-repeated account that he pulled down the segregation ropes himself at a 1953 crusade rests on his own memoir, *Just as I Am* (1997), which could not be read from this environment. **It is not in the outline.** The 1957 invocation by Martin Luther King Jr. and King's two letters, which are documented at Stanford's King Institute, are used instead. (Bank §9i.)
- **Whether Louisiana's classroom Ten Commandments law is constitutional.** No court has answered it. The Fifth Circuit sitting en banc vacated the injunction on 20 February 2026 on ripeness alone and said in terms that it was not deciding the constitutional question. The outline says the question is open. (Bank §10e.)
- **One spelling.** The United States' Third Circuit brief in *United States v. Bowers* spells one victim's name "Joyce Feinberg" in one place and "Joyce Fienberg" in another. The outline uses Fienberg and the bank records the disagreement. (Bank §10i.)
