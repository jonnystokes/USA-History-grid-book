## [VERIFY] queue — CLEARED 2026-09-10

**All 24 lines of this queue are resolved.** Eras 3-8 were cleared on 2026-09-09; eras 9 and 10 on
2026-09-10. Every item is written up with per-fact sources in `research/research-government-politics.md`.
Three of them did not resolve the way the queue assumed, and the corrections are recorded here so
nobody re-introduces the old wording:

- **The Elementary and Secondary Education Act, 1965.** The statute itself (79 Stat. 27) says
  **11 April 1965**; the National Center for Education Statistics' own Title I page says 12 April.
  Use 11 April and note the conflict.
- **"States legalising homeschooling one at a time, ending in the early 1990s."** Michigan 1993 is the
  usual answer, and *People v. DeJonge* (25 May 1993) is real, but the same court on the same day ruled
  **against** a family with no religious objection in *People v. Bennett*. Michigan wrote homeschooling
  into its attendance statute separately, effective 1 July 1996. The outline now says this properly.
- **The 2007 pupil-assignment ruling.** The famous sentence in *Parents Involved* was joined by **four**
  justices, not five. Kennedy's separate opinion is the controlling one and it says a district may still
  work to mix its schools by other means. The outline now says this properly.

Two error corrections worth keeping: a fetched case summary listed **Stewart as a dissenter in
*Rodriguez*** (he concurred), and another called ***Espinoza* 6-3 with Kagan in the majority** (it was
5-4; Kagan joined Ginsburg's dissent). Both were caught against the opinions themselves.
