## Era 10 (2000-Today) — finished 2026-09-09. What was resolved, and what was not.

All nine `[VERIFY]` tags in this era are closed and the bank carries the sourcing in **PART F,
§§10a-10k**. `python tools/project_state.py --check education --stage research` now **PASSES**, which
makes `education` the first chapter in the book to clear its research bar.

**The standing rule this era is built on:** every figure carries the year it describes *and* the year
its source was published. Several of the newest federal counts are a few years older than the writing
date, and the outline says so out loud instead of implying they are current.

**Resolved, with the year of each source:**
- No Child Left Behind: Public Law 107-110, approved 8 January 2002 at Hamilton High School,
  Hamilton, Ohio. Replaced by the Every Student Succeeds Act, signed 10 December 2015.
- Charter schools: 7.6 percent of public school pupils in 2022-23 (NCES Digest table 216.90, prepared
  February 2024). No 2023-24 version of that table existed on the NCES site when this pass ran.
- Private and parochial: 4.7 million and 9 percent in autumn 2021 (NCES Condition of Education,
  published May 2024); Catholic 1.7 million, under a third of the 1964-65 peak. The 2023-24 Private
  School Universe Survey was still being collected.
- Vouchers and ESAs: 69 programmes; 488,736 + 349,923 + 217,194 + 106,442 = about 1,162,000 children
  in 2024-25 (EdChoice, which supports these programmes and is labelled that way in the outline).
- Homeschooling: 2.8 percent in 2019 (NCES 2023-101, September 2023); 5.4 percent in spring 2020 and
  11.1 percent in autumn 2020, Black households 3.3 to 16.1 percent (Census Bureau, March 2021);
  5.82 percent in 2022-23 and holding (Smith and Watson, *Journal of School Choice* 18(4), 2024).
- State oversight of homeschooling: 12 states require no notice at all, 29 require no assessment at
  any point, only 3 require every homeschooled child to be assessed, 22 require no records at all
  (Coalition for Responsible Home Education, updated March 2026). Texas and New York are the two ends.
- Calendar and start times: 180 days is the norm in 27 states plus D.C. (Pew, September 2023);
  year-round schooling shrank from 4.4 percent of schools in 2007-08 to 2.5 percent in 2017-18
  (NCES); the four-day week grew to about 2,100 schools in almost 900 districts across 26 states by
  September 2023 (Paul Thompson, Oregon State University). Average public high school start time
  8:07 in 2020-21 (NCES); California SB 328 signed 13 October 2019; Florida's 2023 law repealed by
  SB 296, Senate vote 27 March 2025.
- Discipline: the 2021-22 Civil Rights Data Collection, released January 2025, is the newest federal
  count of corporal punishment, suspension, expulsion, police referrals and preschool discipline.
  About 24,500 children were hit at school that year; 15 states still allow it and 30 states plus
  D.C. ban it outright (NPR analysis, 19 August 2026).
- The 2020 story slot: Tabatha Rosproy, named and sourced.
