## What Jon asked for (2026-09-06) — the widened remit

Jon considered adding a separate chapter about play — children playing outside against children sitting on phones and gaming — then confirmed this chapter already covers it and said: *"just make sure it covers what kids do to entertain themselves and adults, and how it's changed over time, and whatever would make this a good chapter, make it so."*

What that means for this chapter, as applied to the seed outline:

- The chapter carries **two halves**: organized sport (which it already had), and **what children and adults did with their free time** (which it barely had). Both run through all ten eras.
- The through-line the ten cells now set up: what a child's day was → who organized it → what it moved indoors onto → what is measured about the change. Named in order: unstructured outdoor play, the rise of organised youth sport, television, arcades and home consoles, phones and online games.
- The modern change is told **with real numbers and named studies**, not as an impression and not as nostalgia. Every measurable claim in the 2000–today cell is seeded as a research target with a named source to check.
- **The ADHD and attention question is a contested research area.** Ruling recorded in `control/DECISIONS.md`: handle it with named studies and real numbers, never asserted. The outline carries the handling rule inside the `what researchers disagree about` span: report what each study measured, how big the reported effect was, and where researchers disagree; never state, and never imply by arrangement, that screens cause ADHD.

Registry angle now reads: *How Americans and their children entertained themselves and how it changed — unstructured outdoor play, games, teams, what sport came to mean, arcades and consoles, and the shift to phones and online play.*
