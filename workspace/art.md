## Thin eras (honest — reflected as `state` in the outline)
- **The 1500s (`thin`)** — almost no Europeans lived here, so almost no European art was made here. One set of watercolors made for investors in England, and some mission decoration, is the whole of it. Two spans and one story is correct. Native art continued and is named, but it is not there to make the European side of the cell look full.
- **The 1600s (`thin`)** — plain portraits by painters whose names were not recorded, carved gravestones by carvers who are also unnamed, samplers, and the first two books. Most of this cell's makers are anonymous; say so rather than implying a roster exists.
- **Before 1500 (`full`, and no named person)** — rich in objects, empty of names, because these nations kept their records in speech, in song, and in the objects themselves. A full cell with no named maker is ruling D working as intended. Do not resolve it by naming a pre-1500 maker; resolve it, if at all, with a living tradition-bearer.
- **1700–1750 (`full`, and the thinnest of the full cells at 235 words)** — painting as a trade, four named painters, prints and almanacs. It is short because the record is short. Do not pad it.
