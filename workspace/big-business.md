## Thin eras (from the seed — reflected as `state` in the outline)
**This chapter has the clearest empty cells in the book. Say so plainly (Jon's rule, 2026-07-23) rather than reaching.**
- **Before 1500 — genuinely empty from this angle.** There were no companies, because the idea of a company did not exist here. There was trade, but trade is not a business with owners and a charter — that is `economy`'s and `marketplace`'s material. Do not dress Native trade networks up as early corporations. (state="empty")
- **The 1500s — nearly as empty.** What reached this coast were crown expeditions, not businesses. The one permitted addition is a short, clearly-labelled aside that Europeans were inventing the chartered trading company in this period and that ships of such companies would arrive in the next century — a fact about Europe, told as one. (state="empty", aside folded into the era zoom)
- **1700–1750** is thin on scale — the largest business was a family merchant house with a few ships. A real answer, not an empty one. (state="thin")
- Every era from 1600 onward has real material; the honest-empty rule applies only to the first two.
