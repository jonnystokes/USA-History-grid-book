## Before 1500

<!-- hb-zoom level="era" -->
Before 1500, nobody crossed the ocean to settle for good in the land that is now the United States. Hundreds of Native nations already lived here, and their people spoke hundreds of different languages. Every group of newcomers who came later arrived on land where people were already living.

Nobody knows how many people lived north of Mexico in 1492, and scholars give very different numbers. The geographer William Denevan puts the number at about 3.8 million, and many scholars cite his figure. A world population atlas published in 1978 gave only about 1 million. Some later scholars argued for as many as 18 to 20 million. The historian Donald Fixico gives a range from a few million to 15 million. No number is settled.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->

<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="The land bridge and the Norse" -->
The first people to reach this land came on foot from Asia, long ago. They walked across the ice-age land bridge, a stretch of dry ground that joined Asia to North America.

Only one group crossed the ocean to this land before 1500, and it did not stay. Around the year 1000, Norse sailors from northern Europe built a camp at L'Anse aux Meadows, in Newfoundland, in what is now Canada. They lived there for about ten years and then left. They built no settlement that lasted.
