## 1800 to 1850

<!-- hb-zoom level="era" -->
Between 1800 and 1850, Americans founded the country's first lasting science journal and a national institution for science. Benjamin Silliman began teaching chemistry at Yale in 1802 and founded the journal in 1818. The Smithsonian Institution was founded in Washington in 1846, with money left by an Englishman who never visited the country. Its first head, Joseph Henry, set up a network of volunteers who reported the weather.

In Philadelphia in the same years, a doctor named Samuel George Morton collected hundreds of human skulls. Some of the men who supplied him robbed graves to get them. Morton measured the skulls and used the numbers to rank the races of people. The idea that skull size shows the intelligence or worth of a group of people is false.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->

<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="A professor, a fallen stone and a journal" -->
Benjamin Silliman became Yale's professor of chemistry and natural history in 1802. Chemistry is the study of what things are made of and how substances change. Natural history is the study of plants, animals and rocks. Silliman gave his first lectures in 1804.

On December 14, 1807, a meteorite fell at Weston, Connecticut. A meteorite is a rock from space that falls to the ground. Silliman and James Kingsley wrote a careful record of the fall. They also ran chemical tests to learn what the meteorite was made of. It was the first meteorite fall in the Americas that scientists recorded and studied in this way.

In 1818 Silliman founded the *American Journal of Science*. A scientific journal is a magazine in which scientists publish their research. His was the first one in the country that lasted. For decades people called it "Silliman's Journal."
<!-- /hb-zoom -->

<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Joseph Henry, magnets and credit" -->
Joseph Henry discovered self-induction and published it in July 1832, in Silliman's journal. When the electric current in a coil of wire changes, the coil makes its own push of electricity against that change. That effect is self-induction.

Henry was a teacher at the Albany Academy, a school in Albany, New York. His best years of research there ran from 1826 to 1832. He built the strongest electromagnets of his day. An electromagnet is a magnet made by sending electricity through a coil of wire.

Henry also saw a related effect, called electromagnetic induction. It is the making of an electric current with a magnet. Michael Faraday in London published his discovery of it first, in 1831. Henry had found it on his own but published later. Faraday keeps the credit for that discovery, and Henry has the credit for self-induction. The unit for measuring inductance, the strength of this effect in a coil, is called the henry, after him.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->

<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Samuel Morton's skulls" -->
Samuel George Morton was a Philadelphia doctor who collected human skulls and used them to rank the races of people. He lived from 1799 to 1851. He was a member of the Academy of Natural Sciences of Philadelphia, a society of scientists. In 1830 he prepared a lecture on how skulls differ. In the early 1830s he began to collect them. When he died in 1851, he owned 867 human skulls.

He got the skulls through 138 contacts: scientists, merchants, military men and missionaries. Army surgeons at far-off posts were his most useful suppliers. Some of his collectors robbed graves. About a dozen skulls came from a potter's field in Philadelphia, a burial ground for poor people. Poor Black Philadelphians were buried there. More than 50 skulls in his collection were dug out of a burial ground of enslaved Africans in Cuba. Others came from battlefields and from gallows, the frames on which people were hanged. Many of the skulls were those of Native Americans.

Morton filled each skull with peppercorns, and later with lead shot, which is tiny balls of lead. Then he poured them out and measured how much the skull had held. He believed that a bigger skull meant a more intelligent person. He ranked the races by the average size of their skulls, with white Europeans at the top.

His book *Crania Americana*, Latin for "American skulls," came out in 1839. It made him famous in other countries. Defenders of slavery used his rankings to argue for slavery and to claim that some races stood above others. The Penn Museum's own account states that he is sometimes called the "father of scientific racism." Scientific racism means using the look of science to claim that some races are better than others.

Scholars still argue about how carefully Morton measured. In 1978, and again in a 1981 book, Stephen Jay Gould of Harvard wrote that Morton's numbers were biased. That means his mistakes leaned toward the answer he wanted. In 2011 six scholars led by Jason E. Lewis measured 308 of the 670 skulls Morton had published. They found his errors small and random. A 2016 paper disputed parts of their work. The argument is only about whether Morton measured carelessly. His main idea, that skull size ranks the intelligence or worth of groups of people, is false.

The skulls are now in the Penn Museum, at the University of Pennsylvania. In 2021 its director, Christopher Woods, apologized for "the unethical possession of human remains in the Morton Collection." He announced a plan to return them.

### James Smithson

> **Who:** An English chemist who left his fortune to the United States to found the Smithsonian Institution. He never set foot in the country.
> **When and where:** Died in Genoa, Italy, on June 27, 1829. The Smithsonian was founded in Washington on August 10, 1846.

James Smithson paid for the Smithsonian Institution, even though he never set foot in the United States. He was an English chemist. He died in Genoa, Italy, on June 27, 1829.

His will left his money to his nephew. If the nephew died without heirs, meaning children to inherit it, the money was to go "to the United States of America, to found at Washington, under the name of the Smithsonian Institution, an establishment for the increase & diffusion of knowledge." Diffusion means spreading. Smithson never explained why he left his fortune to a country he had never seen, and nobody knows the reason.

The nephew died without heirs in 1835. In September 1838, more than 100,000 gold coins called sovereigns reached the mint in Philadelphia, where the country's coins are made. Workers there turned them into American coins worth more than $500,000. Members of Congress then argued for eight years about what to do with the money. On August 10, 1846, President James K. Polk signed the law that founded the Smithsonian.

### Joseph Henry

> **Who:** A teacher at the Albany Academy who discovered self-induction and became the first Secretary, or head, of the Smithsonian Institution.
> **When and where:** Albany, New York, for his research of 1826 to 1832. Princeton from 1832. Washington from 1846.

Joseph Henry was the first Secretary, or head, of the Smithsonian Institution. He was elected to the job on December 3, 1846.

Before that he had taught at the Albany Academy, where he discovered self-induction. In 1832 he became a professor at Princeton.

At the Smithsonian, from 1849, Henry ran the Smithsonian Meteorological Project. Meteorology is the science of weather. About 150 volunteers wrote down the weather where they lived. They sent their reports by mail, and some by telegraph at no charge. A telegraph sent messages along a wire as bursts of electricity. Henry used the reports to build the first weather maps of the whole country.

A decade later, about 600 observers were sending reports. The project ran from 1849 to 1870. The National Weather Service, the government's weather agency today, began from it.
<!-- hb-story:end slug="joseph-henry" -->

<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Charting the winds at sea" -->
Matthew Fontaine Maury made charts of the winds and currents at sea from thousands of old ships' logs. A log is the daily record a ship's crew keeps of its position, the wind and the sea. A current is a stream of moving water inside the ocean. A chart is a map for sailors.

From 1842 Maury ran the Navy's Depot of Charts and Instruments, where old logs were stored. He went through thousands of them for facts about winds and currents. In 1847 he issued the first Wind and Current Chart of the North Atlantic. He then collected logs from ship captains around the world, all kept in the same format. Captains who used his charts cut their sailing times.

In 1861 Maury resigned from the US Navy and joined the Confederacy. The Confederacy was the group of Southern states that broke away from the United States and fought against it in the Civil War.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->

### Maria Mitchell

> **Who:** A librarian and astronomer on Nantucket who found a comet in 1847. She was the first American and the first woman to win the King of Denmark's gold medal for finding a comet.
> **When and where:** Nantucket, Massachusetts. She found the comet on October 1, 1847.

On October 1, 1847, Maria Mitchell found a comet that no chart of the sky showed. A comet is a frozen body that travels around the sun. She was on the roof of the Pacific National Bank building on Nantucket, an island off Massachusetts. Her father worked at the bank. She was sweeping the sky with a small telescope, moving it slowly across the stars. The comet is known as "Miss Mitchell's Comet."

The King of Denmark offered a gold medal for each new comet found with a telescope. Mitchell won it. She was the first American and the first woman to receive it. An Italian astronomer named de Vico saw the same comet two days after she did.

In 1836 she had become the first librarian of the Nantucket Atheneum, a library on the island. She kept that job for 20 years. She read in the library by day. At night she watched the sky with her father from the roof of the bank, where he was the head officer.

In 1848 Mitchell became the first woman elected to the American Academy of Arts and Sciences, a society of scholars.
