# How This Book Works

This book is a history of the United States in 37 chapters, each about one subject. Every chapter is divided into the same ten eras in the same order: before 1500, the 1500s, the 1600s, 1700 to 1750, 1750 to 1800, 1800 to 1850, 1850 to 1900, 1900 to 1950, 1950 to 2000, and 2000 to today. That makes the book a grid, 37 subjects by 10 eras, and you can read down one subject or across one era.

Each chapter includes these signposts:
- **The card** at the top lists the chapter's point in one sentence, a true story, what to watch for, three questions, a section to read when you have five minutes, and the sections you can skip.
- **The first bold line of each era** states how long the era takes to read.
- **"By [year]" lines**, in the longest eras only, state where things stand partway through.
- **The end of a long era** states the one thing to remember from it, often with a few dates, and what comes next.
- **Boxes with a person's name at the top** contain many of the main stories, each built from the records.
- **The closing** at the end states the answers to the three questions, five dates to keep, one thing still not known, a place to visit and the next chapter.
- **A hard word** is explained the first time it appears in a chapter, and the glossary at the back lists them all.

Every fact in this book comes from a record someone made: a letter, a court paper, a count, a photograph, bones in the ground, or a story a nation kept. "How We Know What Happened," at the back, describes how historians check those records and what happens when two records state different things.
