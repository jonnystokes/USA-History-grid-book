## 1900 to 1950

<!-- hb-zoom level="era" -->
In 1900 Americans who wanted to see actors mostly went to a live stage. By 1950 they watched actors on film and heard them on the radio. From 1905 small theatres charged five cents to see short films, and by 1908 there were about 8,000 of them. The first film studio in Hollywood opened in 1911. By the 1930s eight companies made most American films. Films began to talk in 1927.

The old stage caricatures moved onto the screen and the radio. White actors in blackface or other makeup still played Black, Asian and Native characters. On the radio, two white men used put-on voices to play Black characters in one of the most popular shows of the time. State laws and the rules that studio heads wrote for themselves limited the parts that actors of color could get.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->

<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Story films, nickelodeons and actors with no names" -->
Edwin S. Porter made *The Great Train Robbery* for Thomas Edison's company in November 1903. The film runs about 12 minutes. Porter cut between places to show things happening at the same time. He took the title from a stage play of 1896. Later legends called it the first film with a story, and film historians have shown that the legends are wrong.

On June 19, 1905, Harry Davis and John P. Harris opened a small theatre in a Pittsburgh storefront and charged a nickel to see films. They named it the Nickelodeon, and soon people used that name for all theatres of the kind. By 1908 there were about 8,000 nickelodeons in the United States. Most of them seated fewer than 200 people. William Fox, Carl Laemmle and Louis B. Mayer, who later ran big film studios, all started out as nickelodeon owners.

The Nestor company opened the first permanent film studio in Hollywood on October 27, 1911, at Sunset Boulevard and Gower Street. Hollywood lies in the Los Angeles Basin, the homeland of the Tongva people. The Spanish called them the Gabrielino. In 1994 California officials recognized the Tongva as the original tribe of the Los Angeles Basin. No Tongva group has federal recognition as a tribe.

The first film companies did not publish their actors' names. The owners of the Biograph company refused to name Florence Lawrence, one of their leading actors, so fans called her "the Biograph Girl." Her biographer writes that the owners feared famous actors would ask for more pay. In 1910 the producer Carl Laemmle hired her. He spread a false story that she had been killed by a streetcar. Then he named her in newspaper ads headed "We nail a lie." Lawrence is often called the first film star. Evidence published in 2019 shows that the French actor Max Linder was named first.

In 1915 all nine justices of the Supreme Court ruled that showing films was "a business, pure and simple." That meant films had no protection as free speech. State and city censor boards could cut any film or ban it. In 1952 the justices reversed that ruling.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->

<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="1915: The Birth of a Nation" -->
D. W. Griffith's *The Birth of a Nation* showed the Ku Klux Klan as heroes, and it cast white actors in blackface as most of its Black characters. It opened in Los Angeles on February 8, 1915. It ran about three hours, longer than any film released before it. Griffith based it on a novel by Thomas Dixon called *The Clansman*.

The film shows its Black characters as unintelligent and as a danger to white women. In one scene, Gus, a freed man played by a white actor in blackface, follows a white girl named Flora Cameron into the woods and says he wants to marry her. She runs from him to the edge of a cliff and jumps to her death. Klansmen in the film hunt Gus down, hold their own trial and lynch him. To lynch is for a mob to kill a person with no lawful trial. At the end of the film, armed Klansmen on horseback stop Black men from voting. Griffith said that he had decided to have "no black blood among the principals," the main actors. Black extras, whom the filmmakers housed apart from the white cast, appear in many crowd scenes.

On February 18, 1915, President Woodrow Wilson watched the film in the East Room of the White House, with his family and members of his Cabinet. Dixon and Wilson had been graduate students together. Wilson's secretary later wrote that Wilson had not known what the film contained. In 1937, 22 years later, a magazine printed words that it said Wilson spoke about the film: "It is like writing history with lightning. And my only regret is that it is all so terribly true." No record from 1915 contains those words, and historians dispute them.

In May 1915 Dixon wrote to Wilson's secretary that "the real purpose of my film was to revolutionize Northern sentiments." Sentiments here means opinions.

Members of the NAACP, the National Association for the Advancement of Colored People, protested at showings from the first weeks. In Boston in April 1915, the managers of the Tremont Theatre refused to sell tickets to William Monroe Trotter, the editor of a newspaper called the *Guardian*, and to a group of Black Bostonians. Police arrested Trotter and ten others. Thousands of people then rallied against the film in Boston. Censors in Ohio and in Chicago, Denver, Pittsburgh, St. Louis and Minneapolis refused to let it be shown. The mayor of Cedar Rapids, Iowa, banned it after he met with a group of Black citizens. Most cities and states let it play.

No one knows how much people paid to see the film. The company that made it reported $4.8 million by the end of 1917. The historian Richard Schickel estimates more than $60 million from its first run. No American film earned more until *Gone with the Wind* in 1939.

After the film opened in Atlanta, a preacher named William J. Simmons started the Klan again. He led a cross burning on Stone Mountain, Georgia, in November or December 1915. *Encyclopaedia Britannica* states that Simmons was "directly inspired" by the film. Klan leaders later showed the film to bring in new members. David Duke did so in the 1970s.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->

### Lilian St. Cyr (Red Wing)

> **Who:** A Ho-Chunk actress who played the leading woman in the first Western made in Hollywood.
> **When and where:** Born on the Winnebago Reservation in Nebraska in February 1884. Acted in films from 1908 to 1925. Died in March 1974.

Lilian St. Cyr played a leading part in the first Western made in what is now Hollywood, and she was paid far less than the white man who starred with her. She was Ho-Chunk, born on the Winnebago Reservation in Nebraska. By 1888 both of her parents had died. She spent ten years in Indian boarding schools and graduated from the Carlisle Indian School in Pennsylvania in 1902.

She married James Young Deer in 1906. The couple performed in Wild West shows, and from 1908 they acted in short Western films. She used the name Red Wing. In 1909 the director D. W. Griffith hired the couple as technical advisers.

On February 23, 1914, Cecil B. DeMille's *The Squaw Man* opened. Its title uses an insulting word for a Native woman. St. Cyr played a Ute woman who marries an Englishman and then kills herself. She made her own costumes. The white male lead earned $1,000 a week. Someone told her she should be proud to act with such a star. She answered, "What about him? He's working with a one hundred percent American."

She acted until 1925. Then she made costumes in New York and worked for a national day to honor Native Americans. Sources give her birth date as February 14 or February 23, 1884, and her death as March 12 or March 13, 1974.
<!-- hb-story:end slug="lilian-st-cyr" -->

<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="1913: Wounded Knee acted again" -->
In the fall of 1913, Buffalo Bill Cody made a film that acted out the Wounded Knee killings, on the Pine Ridge Reservation in South Dakota. Cody got $50,000 for it from the Essanay film company and from the owners of two newspapers. Lindley Garrison, the Secretary of War, supplied soldiers of the 12th Cavalry to play the troops. Franklin K. Lane, the Secretary of the Interior, allowed more than 1,000 Lakota people to take part. General Nelson Miles advised on the film, although he had written that such a show was not "for the welfare of the Indians." Short Bull, who had lost 23 relatives at Wounded Knee and had toured with Cody as a prisoner in 1891, acted in it.

The film was called *The Indian Wars*. Only a few minutes of it survive. Ben Black Elk, a Lakota man, later said that "the government put a ban on it" and destroyed it. No record names the official who did so.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->

<!-- hb-story:start slug="bert-williams" name="Bert Williams" movie="" kind="famous" status="verified" -->
### Bert Williams

> **Who:** A Black comedian who wore blackface on stage and starred in an otherwise all-white Broadway show.
> **When and where:** Born in Nassau, in the Bahamas, on November 12, 1874. Performed in the United States and Britain from 1895. Died in New York City on March 4, 1922.

Bert Williams was a Black comedian who blacked his face to work, even as a star on Broadway. He moved to the United States at ten. From 1895 he worked with another Black comedian, George Walker. The two men wore burnt-cork blackface, as white minstrels did. They billed themselves as "Two Real Coons," using a slur of the time, to set themselves apart from the many white performers in blackface. On stage Williams played the slow-witted, shuffling Black man that was then a standard part in vaudeville.

In August 1900, during a riot in New York, a white mob pulled Walker off a streetcar and beat him. In 1903 the two men starred in *In Dahomey*, a musical with an all-Black cast. It played on Broadway and then at Buckingham Palace in London.

In 1909 a group of white vaudeville performers called the White Rats made theatre managers cut Williams's billing, the size and place of his name on the posters. Managers on the big vaudeville circuits allowed only one Black act on each program. Williams had to travel, eat and sleep apart from the white performers.

In 1910 Florenz Ziegfeld hired Williams for his Broadway revue, the *Follies*. A revue is a show of songs and comic scenes. Several white cast members demanded that Ziegfeld fire him. Ziegfeld answered, "I can replace every one of you," except Williams. Williams starred in the *Follies* until 1919. On one three-year contract he was paid $62,400 a year. That August, the rest of the cast went on strike, and no one told him. He said, "I don't belong to either side. Nobody wants me." He collapsed on stage in Detroit in February 1922 and died in New York at 47. More than 5,000 people filed past his coffin.
<!-- hb-story:end slug="bert-williams" -->

<!-- hb-story:start slug="charlie-chaplin" name="Charlie Chaplin" movie="Chaplin (1992)" kind="famous" status="verified" -->
### Charlie Chaplin

> **Who:** A London comedian who became a film star around the world by acting without speaking.
> **When and where:** Born in London on April 16, 1889. Made films in Los Angeles from 1914 to 1952. Died in Switzerland on December 25, 1977.
> **Movie:** *Chaplin* (1992), a feature film about his life, directed by Richard Attenborough.

Charlie Chaplin became famous around the world for acting with his face and body, with no spoken words. He grew up poor in London. Before he was nine, he was sent twice to a workhouse, a place where poor people lived and worked in return for food and a bed.

Chaplin came to the United States with a touring comedy company. In September 1913 he signed with the Keystone studio for $150 a week. His character the Tramp first appeared on screen on February 7, 1914. In 1916 the Mutual company paid him $670,000 for one year of work.

On February 5, 1919, Chaplin formed a company called United Artists with the actors Mary Pickford and Douglas Fairbanks and the director D. W. Griffith. The four could then pay for their own films and control them. Chaplin kept making films with no spoken words after sound arrived, including *City Lights* (1931) and *Modern Times* (1936). His first talking film, *The Great Dictator* (1940), made fun of the German dictator Adolf Hitler.

For years, FBI agents under J. Edgar Hoover investigated Chaplin. On September 18, 1952, he sailed for London. The next day Attorney General James McGranery canceled his permit to come back into the United States. Chaplin settled in Switzerland. He came back once, in April 1972, to accept an honorary Academy Award, and the audience stood and applauded for 12 minutes.
<!-- hb-story:end slug="charlie-chaplin" -->

<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Sound, and the Production Code" -->
*The Jazz Singer* opened in New York on October 6, 1927. It was the first full-length film with songs and speech recorded to match the actors' lips, in a few scenes. Its star, Al Jolson, was a Jewish performer born in Lithuania who worked in blackface. At the end of the film he sings in blackface.

In 1930 the heads of the big studios agreed to follow a set of rules called the Production Code. Martin Quigley, a Catholic publisher, and Daniel Lord, a Catholic priest, wrote it. From July 1, 1934, no film from those studios could open without a seal of approval from an office run by Joseph Breen. No government wrote the Code or made anyone follow it. The studio heads used it to keep government censors away.

One rule in the Code read: "Miscegenation (sex relationships between the white and black races) is forbidden." Miscegenation is an old word for marriage or romance between people of different races. The studio heads rewrote that rule in 1956. On November 1, 1968, they replaced the whole Code with movie ratings.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->

<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="The studios and their contracts" -->
By the 1930s eight companies made most American films. Five of them, MGM, Paramount, Warner Bros., 20th Century-Fox and RKO, also owned chains of theatres. By 1939 there were 15,000 movie theatres in the country.

Actors signed contracts with a studio for up to seven years. Studio heads did not count any time when an actor was not working, so a contract could run much longer than seven years. The actress Olivia de Havilland sued Warner Bros. over this in 1943. On December 8, 1944, California judges ruled that seven years meant seven years on the calendar.

On May 4, 1948, the justices of the Supreme Court ruled against the five studios that owned theatres, in a case called the Paramount case. By 1954 the studios had sold their theatres.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->

### Oscar Micheaux

> **Who:** A Black homesteader and novelist who made more than 40 films for Black audiences outside the Hollywood studios.
> **When and where:** Born in Metropolis, Illinois, on January 2, 1884. Made films from 1919 to 1948. Died in Charlotte, North Carolina, on March 25, 1951.
> **Movie:** *Midnight Ramble: Oscar Micheaux and the Story of Race Movies* (1994), a PBS documentary about him and about films made for Black audiences.

Oscar Micheaux made more than 40 films for Black audiences outside the Hollywood studios. He worked as a Pullman porter, an attendant on railroad sleeping cars. Then he farmed a homestead in Gregory County, South Dakota, until he lost it in 1911. He wrote novels about that life and sold them door to door.

In 1918 the Lincoln Motion Picture Company, a Black-owned film company, offered to film his novel *The Homesteader*. Micheaux wanted to make the film himself. He started his own company and sold shares in it for $75 to $100 each. The film came out in 1919.

His next film, *Within Our Gates* (1920), shows a white mob lynching a Black couple and burning their bodies. Micheaux gave it to Chicago's censor board in December 1919, and the board first rejected it. Five months earlier, gangs of white youths in Chicago had attacked Black residents during a riot in which 38 people died, 23 of them Black. The censors feared the film would set off more violence. Officials in Omaha and New Orleans also blocked it or demanded cuts.

Micheaux's films played in about 700 theatres. He paid for each new film by touring with the last one and raising money from investors along the way, often carrying the prints by hand. The only known copy of *Within Our Gates* was found in Spain in 1979.
<!-- hb-story:end slug="oscar-micheaux" -->

<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Plays on the radio" -->
Radio drama was acting for listeners who could not see the actors. Two white men, Freeman Gosden and Charles Correll, started *Amos 'n' Andy* on a Chicago radio station on March 19, 1928. They knew the traditions of the minstrel show, and they voiced Amos, Andy and more than 170 other characters as Black men, in the dialect of minstrel shows. One storyline in 1930 and 1931 drew about 40 million listeners. In 1930 Gosden and Correll played the parts in blackface in a film.

In 1931 the *Pittsburgh Courier*, a Black newspaper, collected petitions from more than 700,000 Black Americans. The petitions asked the Federal Radio Commission to take the show off the air. The *Chicago Defender*, another Black newspaper, praised the show that same year.

On October 30, 1938, Orson Welles, who was 23, and his Mercury Theatre on the Air performed H. G. Wells's novel *The War of the Worlds* on the CBS radio network. They acted it as a string of fake news bulletins about Martians landing in New Jersey. The announcement that it was a play came almost 40 minutes in.

The next morning, newspapers across the country reported that the broadcast had set off a national panic. Later researchers found a much smaller event. That night a ratings company telephoned 5,000 homes, and 2 percent said they were listening to the Welles program. Some listeners were frightened and called police stations and newspapers. Six weeks later, researchers checked six New York City hospitals and found no one brought in because of the broadcast. No death has ever been traced to it.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->

<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="The Federal Theatre Project, 1935 to 1939" -->
During the Great Depression, many theatre workers lost their jobs. On August 27, 1935, federal officials set up the Federal Theatre Project to pay them to put on plays. Harry Hopkins, the head of the Works Progress Administration, a federal program that gave people jobs, chose Hallie Flanagan, a theatre professor at Vassar College, to lead it. Within a year the project employed 15,000 people at $23.86 a week. About 30 million people saw its roughly 1,200 productions, and most of the shows were free.

The project set up 17 Negro Theatre Units, companies of Black actors, in cities from Seattle to Birmingham. On April 14, 1936, the New York unit opened *Macbeth* at the Lafayette Theatre in Harlem. It had an all-Black cast of about 150 and was set on an island like Haiti in the 1800s. Orson Welles, then 20, directed it. A free show on April 20 drew 3,000 more people than the theatre could seat.

In 1938 a committee of the House of Representatives, led by Martin Dies Jr., held hearings that accused the project of spreading communism. The committee's members argued that "racial equality forms a vital part of the Communist dictatorship." On December 6, 1938, Flanagan testified before the committee. Members of Congress then cut off the project's money in June 1939, and 8,000 people lost their jobs.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->

### Anna May Wong

> **Who:** A film actress often called the first Chinese American film star, who spoke out against the parts that film studios gave her.
> **When and where:** Born Wong Liu Tsong in Los Angeles on January 3, 1905. Acted in the United States and Europe from 1919. Died in Santa Monica, California, on February 3, 1961.

Anna May Wong became a film star while producers kept her out of leading parts written for Chinese women. Her parents were Chinese Americans. She got her first film job as an extra in 1919. She starred in *The Toll of the Sea* (1922), one of the first films made in color.

State laws against marriage between races barred her from kissing a white actor on screen. That held even when the white actor was playing an Asian man. Studio producers cast her as villains and as a scheming "Dragon Lady." In 1933 she asked a magazine writer, "Why is it that the screen Chinese is always the villain?"

In 1935 the producers at MGM refused to consider her for O-Lan, the Chinese heroine of *The Good Earth* (1937). They gave the part to Luise Rainer, a white actress made up to play a Chinese woman. Rainer won the Academy Award for Best Actress. MGM's producers tested Wong for Lotus, a dishonest singer. She turned the part down. She told the producer Irving Thalberg that he was asking her, "with Chinese blood," to play "the only unsympathetic role" in a film where white actors played the Chinese characters.

In 1951 she starred in *The Gallery of Madame Liu-Tsong*, the first American television series with an Asian American star. In 2022 her picture went on the quarter, which made her the first Asian American shown on United States coins.
<!-- hb-story:end slug="anna-may-wong" -->

<!-- hb-story:start slug="hattie-mcdaniel" name="Hattie McDaniel" movie="" kind="famous" status="verified" -->
### Hattie McDaniel

> **Who:** The first Black person to win an Academy Award.
> **When and where:** Born in Wichita, Kansas, on June 10, 1893. Acted in Los Angeles from the 1930s. Won the award on February 29, 1940. Died on October 26, 1952.

Hattie McDaniel won the Academy Award in 1940 and had to sit apart from the white stars of her film to receive it. She was the youngest of 13 children, and her parents had been enslaved.

In *Gone with the Wind* she played Mammy, an enslaved house servant, for $450 a week. The film opened in Atlanta on December 15, 1939, at a theatre for white people only. The producer, David O. Selznick, asked that she attend. Officials at MGM advised against it because of Georgia's segregation laws, the laws that kept Black and white people apart. She did not go.

On February 29, 1940, she won the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress. The ceremony took place at the Cocoanut Grove in the Ambassador Hotel in Los Angeles. The hotel had a strict rule against Black guests, and Selznick had to ask a special favor to get her into the building. She sat at a small table with her escort and her white agent, away from the film's white stars. Accounts differ on where the table stood. *The Hollywood Reporter* places it against a far wall. A 2008 book places it at the back of the room. A 2019 article places it in a separate back room used to store the trophies. In her speech she said she hoped to "always be a credit to my race."

At least 74 of her film parts were maids and other servants. Walter White of the NAACP and others criticized her for taking those parts. She often answered, "I'd rather play a maid than be a maid."

In 1945 white neighbors in West Adams Heights, her Los Angeles neighborhood, sued her and 30 other Black homeowners. They wanted to force them out under a rule that only white people could live there. Judge Thurmond Clarke threw the case out. McDaniel died of breast cancer in 1952. She had asked to be buried in Hollywood Cemetery, but it buried only white people. She was buried at Rosedale Cemetery.
<!-- hb-story:end slug="hattie-mcdaniel" -->

<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="The first television plays" -->
On September 11, 1928, the radio station WGY in Schenectady, New York, broadcast a short spy play called *The Queen's Messenger* by television. It was the first television drama. Two actors, Izetta Jewell and Maurice Randall, performed it. Viewers watched on screens three inches wide.

Twenty years later, on June 8, 1948, the NBC network began broadcasting a comedy show called *Texaco Star Theater*. The comedian Milton Berle became its host that fall, and people called him "Mr. Television."
<!-- /hb-zoom -->

## 1950 to 2000

<!-- hb-zoom level="era" -->
After 1950, most Americans watched actors on television. In the early 1950s, actors performed plays live on television, with no second takes. From 1947, members of a House committee questioned film workers about communism, and studio executives agreed not to hire people who would not answer. Hundreds of writers, directors and actors lost work, some for more than ten years.

White actors still played Asian and Native characters in makeup. Actors of color such as Rita Moreno and Sidney Poitier won Academy Awards, and they were still offered a narrow set of parts. From 1975, studios opened big films in hundreds of theatres on the same day. At home, families could record shows from 1975, and in 1984 the Supreme Court justices ruled that taping a broadcast to watch later was legal. Video games began in 1958, and by the 1990s game makers were hiring actors.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->

<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="The blacklist" -->
In the late 1940s and 1950s, hundreds of people in film, radio and television lost their jobs because of their politics, or because they would not answer questions about it. They were put on a blacklist, a list of people whom employers would not hire.

It began in September 1947. The House Un-American Activities Committee, a committee of members of the House of Representatives, called 42 people from the film industry to answer questions about communism. Walt Disney, the studio head Jack Warner, and the actors Gary Cooper and Ronald Reagan answered the committee's questions. Disney named former employees of his who, he said, were probably Communists.

Ten writers and directors refused to say whether they belonged to the Communist Party. They became known as the Hollywood Ten. On November 24, 1947, members of the House voted 346 to 17 to charge them with contempt of Congress, the crime of refusing to cooperate with Congress. The next day, nearly 50 film executives met at the Waldorf-Astoria hotel in New York. Eric Johnston, who led the film producers' trade group, then announced that the ten would be fired or suspended without pay. He said the studios would not "knowingly" hire any Communist. That promise started the Hollywood blacklist.

The ten were convicted in 1948 and went to prison in 1950. Others lost their jobs after witnesses named them, or after they refused to answer the committee's questions. The list grew to hundreds of names, and no one kept a complete count. Some writers kept working under false names. In 1957 the Academy Award for the story of *The Brave One* went to "Robert Rich." That was a false name for the blacklisted writer Dalton Trumbo. The real Robert Rich was the nephew of the film's producer. In 1975 Academy officials reissued the award in Trumbo's name.

Some people named others so they could keep working. The actor Sterling Hayden said in 1963, "I was a rat, a stoolie, and the names I named of those close friends were blacklisted and deprived of their livelihood." A stoolie is a person who informs on others. The blacklist began to break in 1960, when the director Otto Preminger and the actor Kirk Douglas gave Trumbo credit on screen for the films *Exodus* and *Spartacus*.

Paul Robeson was a Black singer and actor. In 1943 he became the first Black actor to play Othello on Broadway. In 1950 officials of the State Department took away his passport, so he could not travel outside the country. His yearly income fell from $100,000 to $6,000 between 1947 and 1952. In 1956 he told the House committee, "I am being tried for fighting for the rights of my people, who are still second-class citizens in these United States of America."
<!-- /hb-zoom -->

<!-- hb-story:start slug="lee-grant" name="Lee Grant" movie="" kind="famous" status="verified" -->
### Lee Grant

> **Who:** An actress who was blacklisted for twelve years at the start of her film career, and who later won an Academy Award for acting and another for a documentary she directed.
> **When and where:** Born Lyova Haskell Rosenthal in New York City on October 31, in the mid-1920s. The year is disputed. Acted in New York and Los Angeles from 1948.

Lee Grant lost twelve years of work in film and television to the blacklist. In 1949 she played a shoplifter in the play *Detective Story* on Broadway, and she played the part again in the 1951 film. It earned her an Academy Award nomination and the Best Actress prize at the Cannes Film Festival in France.

In 1951 the actor J. Edward Bromberg died soon after the House committee questioned him. At his memorial service, Grant gave a speech that suggested the committee's pressure had caused his death. The director Edward Dmytryk had already named the writer Arnold Manoff as a Communist. Grant married Manoff in 1952. The sources give two reasons why she was blacklisted. One is her speech for Bromberg. The other, as the actor Kirk Douglas remembered it, is that she refused to testify about her husband.

For twelve years she could get little work in film or television. She taught acting and took small parts under other names. In her 2014 memoir she wrote of "my rage at those who had taken twelve working, acting years from my life."

She came back in the television series *Peyton Place* and won an Emmy award in 1966. She won the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress for *Shampoo* (1975). Her documentary *Down and Out in America* (1986) won the Academy Award for Documentary Feature.
<!-- hb-story:end slug="lee-grant" -->

<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="The Actors Studio, and plays on live television" -->
In 1947 the directors Elia Kazan, Cheryl Crawford and Robert Lewis opened the Actors Studio in New York, a place where working actors trained. About 700 actors tried out in the first year, and 50 were chosen, including Marlon Brando. The studio taught "method acting." It was built on the teaching of Konstantin Stanislavsky, a Russian director at the Moscow Art Theatre. Lee Strasberg ran the Actors Studio from 1952 until he died in 1982.

On December 3, 1947, Brando played Stanley Kowalski in Tennessee Williams's play *A Streetcar Named Desire* on Broadway. Kazan directed it, and he directed the film version in 1951.

From 1947 the television networks broadcast plays performed live each week, such as *Kraft Television Theatre*, from May 7, 1947, and *Studio One*, from November 7, 1948. The actors had one chance, with no second takes. The first practical videotape came in 1957. By 1961 about 80 percent of evening programs were filmed ahead of time.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->

<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="I Love Lucy: a stage comedy on film" -->
*I Love Lucy* was filmed like a stage play, in front of a live audience. It ran on CBS from October 15, 1951, to May 6, 1957. Lucille Ball and Desi Arnaz acted each episode in order, like a play, before an audience of about 300 people. Three film cameras recorded it at the same time.

The three-camera method was not new. Jerry Fairbanks had used it on other shows before. The *Lucy* crew, led by the cameraman Karl Freund, lit the set so that all three cameras got a clear picture.

The sponsor, Philip Morris, wanted the show performed live in New York. Ball and Arnaz wanted to stay in Los Angeles, because Ball was pregnant, and to make the show on film. They took a pay cut of $1,000 a week to cover the cost of the film. In return they got 80 percent ownership of the films. Because they owned the films, they could sell the episodes again as reruns.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->

<!-- hb-story:start slug="lucille-ball" name="Lucille Ball" movie="Being the Ricardos (2021)" kind="famous" status="verified" -->
### Lucille Ball

> **Who:** A film actress who became television's biggest star and then the first woman to run a major television studio.
> **When and where:** Born in Jamestown, New York, on August 6, 1911. Worked in Los Angeles from the 1930s. Died on April 26, 1989.
> **Movie:** *Being the Ricardos* (2021), a feature film about Ball and Arnaz, written and directed by Aaron Sorkin.

In 1962 Lucille Ball became the first woman to run a major television studio. Through the 1930s she acted in films under contract to the RKO studio. People called her "Queen of the B's," because she made so many low-budget films, called B movies.

In 1948 she starred in a radio comedy, *My Favorite Husband*, on CBS. CBS executives asked her to move it to television. She insisted that her real husband, the Cuban bandleader Desi Arnaz, play her husband on the show. At first the executives did not want Arnaz, a Cuban, in the part.

*I Love Lucy* began on October 15, 1951. The episode in which Lucy has a baby aired on January 19, 1953, and about 44 million people watched it. In 1957 Desilu, the company Ball and Arnaz owned, bought the old RKO studios for more than $6 million. Ball had once worked there as a contract actress.

Ball and Arnaz divorced in 1960. In November 1962 she bought his share of Desilu and became its president. Her company developed the television series *Star Trek* and *Mission: Impossible*. She agreed in 1967 to sell Desilu for $17 million, and the sale closed in 1968.
<!-- hb-story:end slug="lucille-ball" -->

<!-- hb-story:start slug="lorraine-hansberry" name="Lorraine Hansberry" movie="Lorraine Hansberry: Sighted Eyes/Feeling Heart (2017)" kind="famous" status="verified" -->
### Lorraine Hansberry

> **Who:** The first Black woman to have a play produced on Broadway.
> **When and where:** Born in Chicago on May 19, 1930. *A Raisin in the Sun* opened in New York on March 11, 1959. Died in New York City on January 12, 1965, at 34.
> **Movie:** *Lorraine Hansberry: Sighted Eyes/Feeling Heart* (2017), a documentary about her life and work.

Lorraine Hansberry wrote *A Raisin in the Sun*, the first play by a Black woman to be produced on Broadway. Her father, Carl Hansberry, sold real estate on Chicago's South Side. When she was about seven, the family bought a house in Woodlawn, a white neighborhood. Owners there had signed an agreement not to sell to Black people. Such an agreement is called a restrictive covenant.

Anna M. Lee, a homeowner in the neighborhood, sued to cancel the sale. A violent mob harassed the family. The judges of the Illinois Supreme Court ruled against the Hansberrys, and the family had to leave. In 1940, in the case *Hansberry v. Lee*, the justices of the US Supreme Court reversed that ruling on a legal point.

*A Raisin in the Sun* tells of a Black family in Chicago who plan to move into a white neighborhood. A white man from that neighborhood offers to pay them to stay away. The producer, Philip Rose, spent 18 months raising money for a play in which all but one character is Black. Lloyd Richards directed it, the first Black director of a Broadway play. Sidney Poitier and Ruby Dee starred. The play ran for 530 performances. Richards said it was the first play on Broadway that large numbers of Black people came to see. Hansberry died of pancreatic cancer in 1965.
<!-- hb-story:end slug="lorraine-hansberry" -->

<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Who was allowed to play whom" -->
White actors kept playing Asian characters in makeup. In *Breakfast at Tiffany's* (1961), Mickey Rooney, a white actor, played a Japanese landlord named I. Y. Yunioshi. The part later drew wide criticism as racist.

Espera de Corti was the son of Italian immigrants in Louisiana. Under the name Iron Eyes Cody, he played Native Americans in more than 200 films, and he said his parents were Cherokee and Cree. In 1971 he appeared in a well-known television advertisement against litter, with a tear rolling down his face. In 1996 a New Orleans newspaper reported, from his half-sister and his baptism record, that he was Italian American. He denied it.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->

<!-- hb-story:start slug="rita-moreno" name="Rita Moreno" movie="Rita Moreno: Just a Girl Who Decided to Go for It (2021)" kind="famous" status="verified" -->
### Rita Moreno

> **Who:** A Puerto Rican actress, dancer and singer who became the first Hispanic woman to win an Academy Award.
> **When and where:** Born Rosa Dolores Alverío Marcano in Humacao, Puerto Rico, on December 11, 1931. Moved to New York in 1936. Acted in Hollywood from 1950. Won the award on April 9, 1962.
> **Movie:** *Rita Moreno: Just a Girl Who Decided to Go for It* (2021), a documentary about her.

On April 9, 1962, Rita Moreno became the first Hispanic woman to win an Academy Award, and the studios went on offering her the same narrow parts. Her mother brought her from Puerto Rico to New York City in 1936 and left her younger brother behind. Moreno was acting on Broadway at 13, and as a teenager she signed a contract with MGM.

Studio producers cast her again and again in the same kind of part. She later said, "I was always offered the stereotypical Latina roles. The Conchitas and Lolitas in westerns. I was always barefoot. It was humiliating, embarrassing stuff. But I did it because there was nothing else." A stereotype is a fixed, simple idea of what a whole group of people is like.

In the film *West Side Story* (1961) she played Anita, a Puerto Rican woman in New York. The part won her the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress. She said the offers that followed were "pretty much the same thing. A lot of gang stories." She did not make another film for seven years. She went on to win a Grammy award for recorded music in 1972, a Tony award for theatre in 1975, and Emmy awards for television in 1977 and 1978.
<!-- hb-story:end slug="rita-moreno" -->

<!-- hb-story:start slug="sidney-poitier" name="Sidney Poitier" movie="Sidney (2022)" kind="famous" status="verified" -->
### Sidney Poitier

> **Who:** The first Black man to win the Academy Award for Best Actor.
> **When and where:** Born in Miami on February 20, 1927. Raised in the Bahamas. Acted in New York and Los Angeles from the 1940s. Won the award on April 13, 1964. Died on January 6, 2022.
> **Movie:** *Sidney* (2022), a documentary about him, directed by Reginald Hudlin.

On April 13, 1964, Sidney Poitier became the first Black man to win the Academy Award for Best Actor. His parents were tomato farmers on Cat Island in the Bahamas. He was born two months early in Miami, while they were there on business, and that made him a US citizen. He grew up on Cat Island and in Nassau. He went to Miami at 15 and moved to New York at 16, where he worked washing dishes.

Poitier failed his first audition at the American Negro Theatre, a Black theatre company in Harlem, because he could not read the script well. An older waiter sat with him every night for several months and helped him read the newspaper. After a short time in the Army, Poitier tried out again and won a part.

In 1958 he became the first Black actor nominated for Best Actor, for *The Defiant Ones*. In 1959 he starred on Broadway in *A Raisin in the Sun*. He won Best Actor for *Lilies of the Field* in 1964. In 1967 more people bought tickets to his films than to any other star's. Critics complained that his characters were too perfect. At the time, he was the only major Black actor being cast in leading parts.
<!-- hb-story:end slug="sidney-poitier" -->

<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="March 27, 1973: a refusal at the Academy Awards" -->
On March 27, 1973, Marlon Brando won the Academy Award for Best Actor for *The Godfather*, and he stayed away from the ceremony. He sent Sacheen Littlefeather, who said she was Apache, to refuse the award for him. She spoke for about 60 seconds. She said Brando was protesting "the treatment of American Indians today by the film industry," and the armed standoff then going on at Wounded Knee, South Dakota. Some people in the audience booed, and some applauded. In 2022 she said that some people mocked her with the "tomahawk chop," a chopping arm motion that makes fun of Native people, as she was led off the stage. In June 2022 the leaders of the Academy sent her a letter of apology.

Littlefeather died on October 2, 2022. After her death, her two sisters told the Navajo writer Jacqueline Keeler that the family was not Native American. They said their father was of Spanish and Mexican descent.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->

<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Big openings and home video" -->
On June 20, 1975, executives at Universal opened *Jaws* on 409 screens in the United States at once, with advertisements on national television. Columbia had done something like it a month earlier with a film called *Breakout*. *Jaws* earned more than any film before it. *Star Wars* opened on May 25, 1977, in only a small number of theatres, because few theatre owners wanted it. Crowds grew, the film spread, and it passed *Jaws* in earnings.

Sony's Betamax video recorder went on sale in the United States in November 1975. The first VHS recorder in the United States, made by RCA, followed on August 23, 1977. In 1976 Universal and Walt Disney Productions sued Sony to stop people from taping at home. On January 17, 1984, the justices of the Supreme Court ruled 5 to 4, in *Sony v. Universal*, that recording a broadcast at home to watch later was legal. From then on, a family could keep a copy of a performance and watch it again.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->

<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Games, and the first actors in them" -->
In 1958 the physicist William Higinbotham built *Tennis for Two* for a public exhibition at Brookhaven National Laboratory in New York. Visitors lined up to play it on a small round screen. In 1972 the Atari company sold *Pong* to arcades. It was the first video game that made money.

The early games had no actors. From 1983 some arcade games played filmed scenes stored on laser discs. In the early 1990s, games on compact discs used filmed actors, often from low-budget films and television. *Night Trap* (1992) and *The 7th Guest* (1993) were two of them. In 1994 the makers of *Wing Commander III*, published by Electronic Arts, cast Hollywood actors in it, including Mark Hamill.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->

<!-- hb-time:start id="2000-today" order="10" chapter="storytelling-evolution" label="2000 to Today" state="full" progress="written" -->
## 2000 to Today

<!-- hb-zoom level="era" -->
Since 2000, actors have performed in suits covered with markers while computer artists turned their movements into creatures and game characters. Game makers now hire actors to perform whole stories. Streaming services brought films and series into homes, and fewer people went to movie theatres. In 2023 film and television actors and writers went on strike together for the first time since 1960. From July 2024 to July 2025, video game performers struck too. Both strikes were partly about who controls a computer copy of a performer's face, body and voice.

In 2023 Michelle Yeoh and Ke Huy Quan won Academy Awards for acting, and in 2024 Lily Gladstone became the first Native American nominated for Best Actress.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->

<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Acting in a marker suit" -->
In motion capture, cameras record the movements of an actor's body, and a computer uses those movements to move a character that artists have drawn. When the cameras also record the actor's face, the method is often called performance capture.

In the *Lord of the Rings* films (2001 to 2003), the English actor Andy Serkis played the creature Gollum on set, beside the other actors. Artists at a company called Weta Digital used the recorded movements of his body and face. They painted Serkis out of each shot, one frame at a time, and put the computer-made Gollum in his place. For *The Two Towers* (2002), the crew fed Serkis's movements into the Gollum model while he performed. It was the first feature film to use capture as the actor acted.

Other actors followed. Tom Hanks played several characters this way in *The Polar Express* (2004). The actors who played the Na'vi people in *Avatar* (2009) did too, and so did Mark Ruffalo as the Hulk in *The Avengers* (2012).
<!-- /hb-zoom -->

<!-- hb-story:start slug="andy-serkis" name="Andy Serkis" movie="" kind="famous" status="verified" -->
### Andy Serkis

> **Who:** An English actor whose performance-capture work for American studios started an argument over whether a performance turned into a computer-made body still counts as acting.
> **When and where:** Born April 20, 1964. Played Gollum in films released from 2001 to 2003. Played the ape Caesar in the *Planet of the Apes* films from 2011 to 2017.

Andy Serkis's work as Gollum started a public argument over whether an actor whose body is replaced on screen by a computer image is still acting. He played Gollum in *The Lord of the Rings* on set with the other actors, and he also gave Gollum his voice. For the second and third films, the artists redesigned Gollum's face to look more like Serkis's.

The films' producer, Barrie M. Osborne, argued that Serkis should be nominated for an Academy Award as an actor, because his voice, his body movements and his facial expressions were on screen. In 2011 staff at the studio 20th Century Fox campaigned for a nomination for his work as the ape Caesar in *Rise of the Planet of the Apes*. The actor James Franco called Serkis "the undisputed master of the newest kind of acting called 'motion capture.'" Serkis said the apes were "infused with the heart and soul of an actor." He has never been nominated for an acting Oscar. *War for the Planet of the Apes* (2017) was nominated for its visual effects instead.

In 2014 Serkis described the animators' work as "painting digital makeup" onto an actor's performance. Animators objected, because they build much of what appears on screen. The director Matt Reeves said the phrase had come from Weta's own artists. In 2011 Serkis opened a capture studio in London, called the Imaginarium.
<!-- hb-story:end slug="andy-serkis" -->

<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Games as acted stories" -->
The game company Naughty Dog released *The Last of Us* on June 14, 2013. Troy Baker played Joel, and Ashley Johnson played Ellie, with their voices and their bodies. About 85 percent of the movement in the game was recorded on a motion-capture stage. Some lines were recorded later in a sound studio. The two actors changed their characters as they worked.

The HBO television version began on January 15, 2023, with Pedro Pascal as Joel and Bella Ramsey as Ellie. Ramsey was told not to play the game, so as not to copy Johnson's performance.

Game actors went on strike for the first time from October 2016 to September 2017, for 340 days. They asked for residuals, payments that grow when a game sells well, and they did not get them. In 2016 the voice actor Jennifer Hale told NPR that game actors were paid $825 for a four-hour session, with nothing more however many copies sold.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->

<!-- hb-story:start slug="ashley-johnson" name="Ashley Johnson" movie="" kind="famous" status="verified" -->
### Ashley Johnson

> **Who:** A child television actor who later played Ellie in the video game *The Last of Us* with her voice and her body, and won the British Academy's game award for performance twice.
> **When and where:** Born in Camarillo, California, on August 9, 1983. Acting in Los Angeles from 1990.

Ashley Johnson played Ellie in *The Last of Us*, and her acting changed who Ellie is. She began acting at six, as Chrissy Seaver on the comedy *Growing Pains*, from 1990 to 1992. By the time she was 12 she had been in the casts of eight television series.

In *The Last of Us*, she acted most of the part on a motion-capture stage opposite Troy Baker. She persuaded the director, Neil Druckmann, to rewrite Ellie as tougher and more independent. She said, "There were some days when we would shoot things that even at my age made me feel a little uncomfortable."

Her own face shaped the character. The actor Elliot Page complained that the first design of Ellie looked like Page. The studio's artists then redrew Ellie to look younger and more like Johnson.

Johnson won the British Academy Games Award for Performer for the 2013 game. She won it again on March 12, 2015, for the added chapter *Left Behind*. She is the only person to have won it twice. She played Ellie again in *The Last of Us Part II* (2020). In the 2023 television series, she played Ellie's mother, Anna.
<!-- hb-story:end slug="ashley-johnson" -->

<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Streaming, and fewer people in movie theatres" -->
Streaming means watching a film or show sent over the internet as you watch it, instead of on a disc or at a set broadcast time. On January 16, 2007, the managers of Netflix, which then mailed DVDs to renters, started a streaming service with 1,000 titles. On February 8, 2012, they released every episode of *Lilyhammer*, a Norwegian series they had bought, on the same day. On February 1, 2013, they released *House of Cards*, the first series Netflix had ordered made for itself. By 2026, Netflix's managers reported 325 million paid memberships in more than 190 countries.

In March 2020 the COVID-19 pandemic closed movie theatres. The weekend of March 13 to 15, 2020, brought the lowest ticket sales in North America since 1998. Studio executives sent new films straight to home viewing. On July 28, 2020, the managers of the AMC theatre chain agreed that Universal could sell a film for home viewing after only 17 days in AMC theatres.

In 2025, according to the research company Comscore, moviegoers in the United States and Canada spent $8.87 billion on tickets. That was 22 percent less than the $11.4 billion they spent in 2019. They bought about 780 million tickets in 2025, about 5 percent fewer than the more than 820 million of 2024.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->

<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Broadway dark for eighteen months" -->
On March 12, 2020, New York Governor Andrew Cuomo ordered that gatherings of 500 people or more be canceled. His order closed the Broadway theatres at 5 p.m. that day, a day earlier than other large events. According to the theatre magazine *Playbill*, all 41 Broadway theatres stayed dark for 18 months.

The first play back on Broadway was *Pass Over*, by the Black playwright Antoinette Nwandu. It began performances at the August Wilson Theatre on August 4, 2021. Nwandu had started writing it in 2012, after the killing of the Black teenager Trayvon Martin. Full productions of other shows returned that fall.

The Broadway League, which represents Broadway's theatre owners and producers, reported in May 2026 that in the season ending May 24, 2026, audiences bought 14,577,322 tickets and paid $1.91 billion. That was the most money in any Broadway season on record.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->

<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Anyone can perform" -->
Three former workers at the payment company PayPal started YouTube in February 2005, and the first video went up on April 23, 2005. From May 2007 YouTube's managers shared the advertising money from some videos with the people who made them. The usual split was 55 percent to the maker and 45 percent to YouTube. YouTube's managers set the rules for who gets paid, and they have changed those rules several times. Since November 2020 they have been able to put advertisements on any video in the United States and keep all of that money when the maker is not in the program.

On August 2, 2018, managers at the Chinese company ByteDance merged a lip-sync app called Musical.ly into their app TikTok. That October, TikTok was the most downloaded app in the United States. A person with a phone can now write, act, film, edit and publish a performance with no studio and no audition, under rules that the platform's managers write and change.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->

<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Who is on screen, and who votes on the awards" -->
On January 15, 2015, all 20 actors nominated for Academy Awards were white. That day April Reign started the Twitter hashtag #OscarsSoWhite. On January 14, 2016, all 20 were white again. The director Spike Lee and the actress Jada Pinkett Smith said they would stay away from the ceremony.

The Oscars are voted on by members of the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences. In 2015 its members were 92 percent white and 75 percent men. Its president, Cheryl Boone Isaacs, promised in 2016 to double the number of women and people of color by 2020. The Academy had about 6,000 members in 2016. The Associated Press reported in June 2026 that after that year's invitations, the Academy could have 11,319 members, 75 percent of them white and 64 percent men.

Researchers at the USC Annenberg Inclusion Initiative count the speaking characters in each year's 100 most popular films. In the films of 2024, they reported in August 2025, 13.5 percent of speaking characters were Asian, 3.1 percent were Hispanic or Latino, and fewer than 1 percent were American Indian or Alaska Native. Twenty-nine of the 100 films had no Asian speaking characters at all. Ninety-eight had no Native American girls or women.

*Reservation Dogs* ran on the Hulu streaming service from August 9, 2021, to September 27, 2023. Sterlin Harjo, a Native filmmaker from Oklahoma, created it with Taika Waititi. It is about four Native teenagers in a small town in the Muscogee Nation. It was the first American series with all Native writers and directors and an almost all Native cast.

*Killers of the Flower Moon* opened on October 20, 2023. It is about the murders of Osage people for their oil money in the 1920s. The director, Martin Scorsese, met with Osage Principal Chief Geoffrey Standing Bear. The film's makers held a meeting with about 200 Osage people, cast Osage actors in at least 40 parts, and hired Christopher Côté to teach the actors the Osage language. At the premiere, Côté said the film told the story mostly through the white husband who helped kill his wife's family. He said, "when somebody conspires to murder your entire family, that's not love." In 2024 he said, "I still stand by what I said."

Lily Gladstone, who grew up on the Blackfeet Reservation in Montana, played Mollie Kyle, the Osage wife. In January 2024 she became the first Native American to win a Golden Globe for acting, and the first Native American nominated for the Academy Award for Best Actress.

*Everything Everywhere All at Once* is about a Chinese immigrant woman who runs a laundromat. On March 12, 2023, it won seven Academy Awards. Michelle Yeoh won Best Actress, and Ke Huy Quan won Best Supporting Actor.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->

<!-- hb-story:start slug="ke-huy-quan" name="Ke Huy Quan" movie="" kind="famous" status="verified" -->
### Ke Huy Quan

> **Who:** A child actor from a refugee family who quit acting when he could not get parts as an adult Asian American actor, and came back decades later to win an Academy Award.
> **When and where:** Born in Saigon, South Vietnam, in 1970. Came to the United States in 1979. Acted in Los Angeles from 1984. Won the award on March 12, 2023.

Ke Huy Quan stopped acting for 19 years because he could not get parts, and then he came back to win an Academy Award. His family was ethnic Chinese. They left Vietnam three years after the war there ended in 1975. Quan, his father and five of his brothers and sisters went to a refugee camp in Hong Kong. His mother and three of the other children went to Malaysia. In 1979 the whole family came to the United States as refugees and settled in Los Angeles.

At 13 Quan played Short Round, Harrison Ford's young sidekick, in *Indiana Jones and the Temple of Doom* (1984). In 1985 he played Data in *The Goonies*. As he grew up, he said, "the opportunities got fewer, and the wait time longer. And the roles got smaller."

In 1993 he tried out for a small part and never heard back. "I remember sitting at the edge of my bed, completely frozen," he said in 2023. He blamed himself: "I thought I wasn't tall enough, I wasn't good-looking enough, I was not a good enough actor." He quit acting and studied film at the University of Southern California. He arranged fights for the film *X-Men* (2000) and worked as an assistant director.

In 2018 he watched *Crazy Rich Asians*, a hit film with an almost all-Asian cast. "I was sitting in the theatre, fantasising, wishing I was up there with them," he said. He went back to acting and played Waymond Wang in *Everything Everywhere All at Once*. On March 12, 2023, he won the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor. He was the first actor born in Vietnam to win an Oscar. He began his speech, "My journey started on a boat."
<!-- hb-story:end slug="ke-huy-quan" -->

<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="#MeToo in Hollywood: Harvey Weinstein" -->
In October 2017 reporters at *The New York Times* and *The New Yorker* published accounts from more than a dozen women who said the film producer Harvey Weinstein had sexually harassed them, sexually assaulted them or raped them. Rape means forcing a person into sex. By October 31, 2017, more than 80 women had made such accusations. Leaders of his own company fired him, and the leaders of the Academy expelled him. Weinstein said he had never had sex with anyone against her will.

In November 2017 the reporter Ronan Farrow showed that Weinstein had hired a private spy company called Black Cube. Its investigators used false names to follow reporters and actresses. One of them was the actress Rose McGowan, who had said Weinstein raped her.

On February 24, 2020, a New York jury convicted Weinstein of a criminal sexual act and of rape, and on March 11, 2020, a judge sentenced him to 23 years in prison. On December 19, 2022, a Los Angeles jury convicted him of rape and other sexual assaults he committed in 2013, and a judge sentenced him to 16 years. On April 25, 2024, the judges of New York's highest court threw out the New York convictions. They found that the trial judge had made "egregious errors," meaning very serious mistakes, and they ordered a new trial. Weinstein stayed in prison because of the California conviction.

At the new trial, on June 11, 2025, a jury convicted him of forcing oral sex on Miriam Haley, a former production assistant, in his Manhattan apartment in 2006. The jury found him not guilty on the charge that involved the model Kaja Sokola. Two juries in a row could not agree on the charge that he raped the actress Jessica Mann. On June 25, 2026, Manhattan District Attorney Alvin Bragg announced that there would be no further trial on that charge.

On September 23, 2026, Justice Curtis Farber sentenced Weinstein to 15 years in prison. He told him, "You took what you wanted by force, and you have never accepted responsibility." Haley told the court that the assault was "a life sentence for me." In June 2026, California judges had upheld his Los Angeles conviction but ordered a new sentence. As of late September 2026, no date had been set for it.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->

<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="The 2023 strikes, and scanning" -->
Members of the Writers Guild of America, the union of 11,500 screenwriters, struck against the studios from May 2 to September 27, 2023, for 148 days. Their new contract forbids the studios to use writers' work to train computer programs that write. It also forbids them to use such programs to cut writers or writers' pay.

Members of SAG-AFTRA, the actors' union, with about 160,000 members, struck from July 14 to November 9, 2023. It was the longest actors' strike against the film and television studios, and the first time since 1960 that actors and writers had struck at the same time.

On July 13, 2023, the union's chief negotiator, Duncan Crabtree-Ireland, said the studios' negotiators had proposed scanning background actors for one day's pay, with the studio owning the scans. Background actors are extras with no lines. The studios' negotiators answered that a scan would be used only in the film the actor was hired for. In August 2023 NPR interviewed five background actors who had been scanned. Most had signed agreements not to talk about their work. They said they feared that if they refused a scan, they would be called difficult and not hired again. No public count exists of how many background actors were scanned.

The union's members approved the contract that ended the strike on December 5, 2023. It requires a studio to get clear consent from an actor before it makes or uses a digital replica, a computer copy of the actor's face, body or voice. A studio that wants to use a replica in a different production must ask again and bargain over pay. The consent lasts after the actor dies unless the contract says otherwise. Studios may not use replicas to avoid hiring background actors.

The writers approved a new four-year contract in April 2026. SAG-AFTRA's members approved theirs on June 4, 2026, with 91 percent voting yes. According to *Los Angeles Magazine*, it lets producers use a performer made entirely by computer only when that performer adds "significant additional value" over a live actor.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->

<!-- hb-story:start slug="alexandria-rubalcaba" name="Alexandria Rubalcaba" movie="" kind="ordinary" status="verified" -->
### Alexandria Rubalcaba

> **Who:** A background actor whose face and body were scanned into a digital copy, with no word to her on how anyone would use it.
> **When and where:** The Los Angeles area, on the Disney+ series *WandaVision* during the COVID-19 pandemic. She told her story to NPR in 2023.

Alexandria Rubalcaba was scanned into a digital copy at work, and no one told her how or whether the copy would be used. She worked for four weeks as a background actor on the series *WandaVision*. Then the crew sent her to a truck trailer. Inside, dozens of background actors stood one at a time in front of cameras mounted on metal frames. She remembered the instructions: "Have your hands out. Have your hands in. Look this way. Look that way. Let us see your scared face. Let us see your surprised face."

Each scan took about 15 minutes, and a digital copy of each actor was made from it. Rubalcaba earned the union rate for background actors, $187 a day. She would not be paid for any use of the scan, and she did not give permission for her copy to appear in any scene. "What if I don't want to be on MarioVision, or SarahVision?" she asked. "I fear that AI is eventually going to weed out background actors. They won't have any use for us anymore."

Disney did not answer NPR's questions, and no record shows whether her scan was ever used. Her account came out in August 2023, during the actors' strike. The contract that ended the strike requires consent before a studio makes or uses a replica. It also forbids studios to use replicas in place of hiring background actors.
<!-- hb-story:end slug="alexandria-rubalcaba" -->

<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="The video game strike, 2024 to 2025" -->
About 2,600 game performers who work in voice and motion capture struck from July 26, 2024, to July 9, 2025. They struck against companies including Activision, Electronic Arts, Epic Games, Take-Two and Warner Bros. Games. Their old contract had run out in November 2022, and union negotiators had bargained with the companies for about a year and a half. The one issue left unsettled was consent and pay for AI copies of the performers' voices and bodies. AI, or artificial intelligence, here means computer programs that can make new images, voices and text by copying patterns from recordings of real people.

On May 16, 2025, staff at Epic Games added Darth Vader to the game *Fortnite*. The character talked with players in a computer-made copy of the voice of James Earl Jones, who had died on September 9, 2024. In 2022 Jones had signed a deal that allowed his recorded voice to be used this way, and his family worked with Epic and Disney on it. On May 19, 2025, union officials filed a charge with the National Labor Relations Board, the federal agency that hears complaints about unfair treatment of workers. The charge was against Llama Productions, a company owned by Epic Games. It said the company's staff had replaced actors' work with AI without telling the union.

The strike ended when 95 percent of the members who voted approved a new contract on July 9, 2025. Before a company makes a digital replica of a performer, it must now get written consent that names what the replica will be used for. It must pay for the replica's use and tell the performer how it is used. During a future strike, performers can stop companies from using their replicas to make new material.

In *Rogue One* (December 16, 2016), the actor Guy Henry played the character Grand Moff Tarkin. Effects artists then placed a digital copy of the face of Peter Cushing, an actor who had died in 1994, over Henry's face. Lucasfilm staff first got permission from Cushing's estate, the people in charge of his property after his death.

On March 21, 2024, Tennessee Governor Bill Lee signed the ELVIS Act. Under it, copying a person's voice with AI without permission is against the law in Tennessee. On September 17, 2024, California Governor Gavin Newsom signed two laws at the actors' union headquarters. The first, AB 2602, requires a contract to describe how a digital replica of a living performer will be used. The second, AB 1836, forbids using a digital replica of a dead performer in films, games and recordings without the estate's consent. As of September 2026, members of Congress had not passed a national law, the NO FAKES Act.

In September 2025 Eline Van der Velden, whose company made a computer-generated "actress" called Tilly Norwood, said talent agents wanted to represent it. The leaders of SAG-AFTRA answered in a statement that Tilly Norwood "is not an actor." They said it was made by a computer program "trained on the work of countless professional performers," without their permission or pay.

The company OpenAI released its Sora 2 video app on September 30, 2025. Users soon made clips with the face and voice of the actor Bryan Cranston, without his consent. Cranston and the union complained. On October 20, 2025, OpenAI's managers agreed to require a person's permission before the app copies that person. Cranston said he was "deeply concerned not just for myself, but for all performers whose work and identity can be misused in this way."
