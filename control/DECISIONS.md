## Part 1 — the six tone rulings

### These are NOT "whether" questions. That is already settled.

`control/AGENT-BRIEF.md:70` and `control/project-notes.md:124` already rule:

> where leaving out the injustice, cruelty, or hard truth of a story would make the
> telling dishonest, **it goes in** — plainly, at a young reader's level, in your
> chapter.

**every chapter owns its own hard parts.**

`control/writing-style-guide.md` has no age-appropriateness rule at all, but it does

reader how to feel about it"), no staged scenes (§1.10), no hedging (§1.5), no
dramatizing language (§1.8), unanchored comparatives get numbers (§1.9). So the
guide already forecloses both softening *and* sensationalizing.

**So the real question is one question, asked six times: how much documented physical
detail?** And the book already has a house answer, in its most-researched chapter.

### The existing standard — `outlines/native-nations.md:183`

The boarding-school passage quotes Pratt's own words, then lists punishments
documented by the federal investigation — beatings, solitary confinement, withheld
food, mouths washed with soap — then the 2024 count: 973 children died, 74 burial
sites, and the report itself says the count is low. Then it closes on survival:
children kept their languages in secret.

That is: **named source · documented specifics including bodily harm · numbers with
their uncertainty · no adjectives · ends on what people did, not on the wound.**
