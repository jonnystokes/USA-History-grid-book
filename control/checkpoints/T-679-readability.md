**Spine:** Fires, floods, storms and quakes strike suddenly, but people's choices decided how many died and who got help, and most safety rules came only after the deaths.

**Three questions:**
1. What rules did Americans make after disasters, and did they work?
2. Was anyone punished when people's choices killed others?
3. Did everyone get the same help after a disaster?

**Anchor:** the South Fork Dam above Johnstown, 1889: a club of rich Pittsburgh men lowered its top by as much as 3 feet for a carriage road and screened the spillway to keep their fish in; it broke on May 31, 1889, and 2,208 people died; the club's lawyers called it an "act of God" and the survivors lost (era 7).
