**Spine (bold, first sentence of the card):** Many of the people who built America's landmarks were
enslaved, forced to work or killed on the job, and Americans still argue about whose land the
landmarks stand on and which people they honor.

**Three questions** (repeated word for word in the closing):
1. Who built the White House and the Capitol, and who was paid for their work?
2. How many workers died building Hoover Dam?
3. What has happened to the Confederate monuments since 2015?

**Anchor:** the block of Aquia Creek sandstone in Emancipation Hall, Capitol Visitor Center, taken
from the Capitol's East Front (built 1824 to 1826), unveiled by members of Congress on
February 28, 2012, with its plaque line "was quarried by laborers, including enslaved African
Americans" (era 10), tied to the 1790s rented men whose owners collected the pay (era 5).

**Turn:** the chapter goes era by era from earthworks more than 3,000 years old to memorials
opened in 2024. Watch who did the building (Native men drafted for the Castillo, enslaved men
rented out for the capital, immigrants killed on the Brooklyn Bridge) and what people argued about
afterward (a golf course on the Octagon, Mount Rushmore on land taken from the Sioux, Confederate
monuments coming down and some going back up).

**Five minutes?** "Enslaved men who built the capital" in 1750 to 1800.
**Can skip:** "The Woolworth Building", "The largest private house", "The Space Needle". None of
them feeds the spine or a question.
