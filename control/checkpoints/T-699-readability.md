**Spine:** American food began with crops that Native farmers bred, and since then much of it has been grown on taken land by people who were enslaved or paid little.

**Three questions:**
1. How did Native farmers turn a wild grass into corn?
2. Who did the hardest farm work, and what did they get for it?
3. How did Americans come to eat food grown far away by people they never met?

**Anchor:** Cornhill, Cape Cod, November 1620 (era 3): the *Mayflower* men dug up about ten bushels of corn and a bag of beans that Nauset farmers had buried in baskets as seed for the spring, named the place Cornhill, wrote they would "satisfy them for their corn". The first corn the Plymouth colonists ate. Corn itself bred from teosinte by Native farmers (era 1). All in the chapter.

**Turn:** two things to watch: whose land and whose work the food came from (Native farmers, enslaved Africans in rice and cotton, sharecroppers, braceros, pickers today), and the food moving farther from the farm (nine in ten working people farmed when the country began; cans, pond ice, cold railroad cars, frozen dinners, fast food; in 2022 the farm count fell below 2 million for the first time since before the Civil War).
