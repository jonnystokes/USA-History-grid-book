**Spine:** Most American money since 1690 has been a written promise to pay, and when the people who made the promise could not keep it, the people holding the money took the loss.

**Anchor:** the Massachusetts bills of credit of 1690. Soldiers home from the failed Quebec attack, too few coins in the treasury, lawmakers began voting in December 1690 to pay them in paper; each bill stated the treasurer would accept it "in all Public payments" and that it could later be exchanged for coins; the first paper money printed by any government in Europe or the lands Europeans settled; people passed the bills from hand to hand. All in era 3 ("1690: the first government paper money").
