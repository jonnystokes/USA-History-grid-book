## SUBJECT NOTES (decided before writing)

**Spine (bold, one sentence):** Native nations were governments long before the United States existed, and through every war, treaty and law aimed at ending them, 574 of them still govern themselves today.

**Anchor:** the Two Row Wampum (Guswenta): two purple rows of beads on white, two boats on one river, neither steering the other; Haudenosaunee tradition dates the agreement with the Dutch to 1613 (era 3, already in the chapter).

**Three questions (word for word in the closing):**
1. How did Native nations govern themselves and deal with other nations before the United States existed?
2. What did United States officials and soldiers do to take the nations' land and break the nations up?
3. How did Native nations hold on to their governments, their languages and some of their land?

**Five minutes?** "Removal and the Trail of Tears" in 1800 to 1850 (renamed from "Removal").

**Can skip:** "Comanche power" (1700 to 1750), "Treaty councils" (1700 to 1750), "Ironworkers from Kahnawake" (1900 to 1950, renamed from "High steel").
