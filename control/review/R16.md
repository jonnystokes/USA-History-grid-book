# R16 reader review: Chapters 26 and 27

## Chapter 26: Health, Disease, and Medicine

- [Era 4, 1700 to 1750] "The minister Cotton Mather led the" : The Onesimus and Mather story is told four times (opening hook, Era 4 intro, this paragraph, the Onesimus box), the hook and the box print the same quoted words, and the 2-in-100 against 14-or-15-in-100 figures appear twice, so the reader keeps meeting the same story : Tell it in full once, in the box, give the section one line that points to it, and cut the Era 4 intro's retelling.
- [Era 3, The 1600s] "In general, about 3 of every" : This gives 3 in 10 as the usual death rate from smallpox, but the chapter opens with 14 or 15 in 100 dying of ordinary smallpox in Boston and repeats that in the Boston section, so the reader sees two rival rates and no reason for the gap : Say why the rates differ or use one figure.
- [Era 4, 1700 to 1750] "By the end of the epidemic," : This says about 287 people in Boston were inoculated and 6 died, while the Boylston box says between 242 and 248 were inoculated and 6 died, so the reader cannot tell whether these are two counts of one group or two groups : Say how many were Boylston's and who did the rest, or give one count.
- [Era 8, 1900 to 1950] "The ambulance brought four sick men" : The hook promises an ambulance bringing four sick men and taking away four dead, but no ambulance appears anywhere in the box, so the reader waits for a scene that never comes : Change the hook to a detail the box does give (the dying man on the floor, the nosebleeds, the bubbles under the skin) or add the scene.
- [Era 5, 1750 to 1800] "They buried several hundred people and" : The hook says the two men never asked for pay, but the box never says so (it mentions the five men they hired, a nurse's wage, and the charge that Black nurses overcharged), so the claim has no support in the text and sits oddly beside that charge : Add the sentence that supports it or change the hook to something the box shows.
- [Era 5, 1750 to 1800] "Jones and Allen's account does not" : The reader has not been told who Jones and Allen are (the Rush box names them with only a note that they knew how to bleed a patient, and their own box comes later), and this paragraph also packs the mayor's order, the committee of 26 and the freed prisoners into one block : Introduce them where first named and split the paragraph.
- [Era 6, 1800 to 1850] "Over the next two months he" : It is unclear what he did again and to which baby, whether Ann's baby was cut repeatedly, and what became of Ann's baby, yet the paragraph goes on to blame Ann : Say which baby each sentence is about, say what became of Ann's baby (or that the records do not say), then give the blame.
- [Era 6, 1800 to 1850] "One count gives twelve women and" : The numbers of women do not add up for the reader (twelve in all, six or seven unnamed, three named by Sims, four more named by Bozeman), so it is unclear how many were operated on : Give one plain total, say which sources differ, or say the number is not known.
- [Before 1500] "Their results support the idea that" : This sentence is hard to follow because it strings together every bacterium studied, old and modern, and it uses ancient DNA and non-venereal with no plain gloss, so the reader cannot tell what was found : Split it into two short sentences, say plainly that the syphilis-family germs may have started in the Americas, and gloss DNA in a few words.
- [Era 6, 1800 to 1850] "The operations went on for about" : The Anarcha box repeats the section above it (about thirty operations, knees and elbows, no drug for the pain, the enslaver who sent her, the small hospital, the closing in 1849) within twenty lines : Keep only what is new (the accounts of her age, the second hole, the unknown fate) and cut the rest.
- [Era 2, The 1500s] "Only 15 of the 80 Spaniards" : A paragraph about five Spaniards eating their dead, with five names in a row, arrives in a chapter about disease with no bridge, and the islanders' uproar is the only link, so the reader cannot see why it is here : Add one sentence on why it matters (the islanders then blamed the Spaniards for the deaths) or trim the names.
- [Era 8, 1900 to 1950] "Accounts differ on when penicillin became" : Penicillin is used in the Era 8 intro and here, in the card's recommended read, before the 1942 section says what it is, so the reader meets the cure with no idea what it is : Add a short gloss at the first use (a medicine made from a mold that kills bacteria) or move the 1942 section earlier.
- [Opening] "Cotton Mather, a Boston minister, wrote" : Smallpox, the disease the opening story and much of the chapter turn on, is not described until the 1634 section in Era 3, and Era 1 only lists it among a dozen names : Add a plain clause at first use (a deadly disease that brings fever and blisters).
- [Era 5, 1750 to 1800] "When Caesar died, he owned an" : The box ends on a surprise (a freed man who owned another person) with no word on who she was or how it came about, so the reader may take away a wrong idea or be left confused : Add what the records say, or say the records do not say who she was.
- [Era 9, 1950 to 2000] "Another doctor, Chester Southam, injected HeLa" : The chapter's third question is about people used without agreeing, but this sentence does not say whether the patients, prisoners and healthy people agreed : Add what the accounts say about consent, or say they do not say.
- [Era 8, 1900 to 1950] "By 1920: Army doctors had proved" : The recap lines that start with By sit directly under unrelated headings (here Radium, again at 1955 and 1965), so they read as part of that section, and the 1955 one sits inside a section the card says to skip, so a skipping reader loses the recap : Put each recap before its heading or set it apart, and keep it out of skippable sections.
- [Opening] "Five minutes? Read" : The five-minute read is the Tuskegee section, which stops in 1972 and says little of what changed, though that is half of the third question and the answer sits in Era 9 and the closer : Name the Era 9 section too, or add a pointer at the end of the Tuskegee section.
- [Era 8, 1900 to 1950] "Officials took her back to the" : No reason is given for taking Mary Mallon back to the island in 1915, and nothing says when she left in between, so the reader may think she was locked up twice for nothing : Give the reason the records give and when she was released.
- [Era 9, 1950 to 2000] "In the Relf sisters' case, a" : The sisters are mentioned before they are introduced (their box comes after the Serena and Puerto Rico paragraphs), and it is unclear which two practices the judge barred until the box : Move the Relf box up beside this paragraph, or introduce the sisters in a clause and name the two practices.
- [Era 9, 1950 to 2000] "They awarded her $17,000. The same" : The reader cannot tell what the $17,000 was for (the jurors faulted the welfare workers about her children), and the last sentence, that the jurors found she agreed to the operation, sits oddly beside her statement that no doctor told her : Say what the award was for and set out the consent finding as its own sentence.
- [Before 1500] "Smallpox, measles, influenza, mumps, chickenpox, cholera," : A run of twelve disease names, several of them hard (diphtheria, typhus), with only the group explained, so the reader skims past them : Name the three or four the chapter returns to and say the rest are met later.
- [Era 8, 1900 to 1950] "Only 16 of every 100 infected" : The Tuskegee section, the card's recommended read, packs its report findings into paragraphs of percentages and counts (16 and 61 in 100, twice as many deaths, a fifth, 30 in 100, 28, 100, 40, 19), more than a reader can hold : Keep the two or three figures that carry the point.
- [Era 7, 1850 to 1900] "When President James Garfield was shot" : The intro says as plain fact that he died with his body full of infection, but the section reports a 2013 paper arguing that a burst artery killed him : Say in the intro that doctors found infection, or carry the doubt there.
- [Era 3, The 1600s] "Surgeons worked in the town too." : This section is a run of names, dates and rules (1638, 1652, 1657, 1658, a 1957 article) with no sentence saying why it matters, and the later Pennsylvania Hospital paragraph leans on the Dutch hospital described only here, though the card says to skip this section : Open with the point, cut the rules, and restate the hospital where it is used later.
- [Era 9, 1950 to 2000] "Inspectors drove at high speed to" : Klan appears with no plain explanation anywhere in the chapter, and a reader who opens at random will not know the word : Gloss it in a few words at first use.

Flow: uneven. Sections open on the point and explain hard words quickly, but the same stories come back in intros, sections and boxes, some hooks promise things the box never says, and a few passages are walls of figures.

## Chapter 27: Disasters and Rescue

- [Era 3, The 1600s] "Another wave swept all four off" : It follows a list of four of Thacher's children who died, so the reader takes all four to be those children, but the four on the rock were Thacher, Mary, Avery and Avery's son, and the other children were still in the boat : Name the four who were swept off.
- [Era 2, The 1500s] "The colonists he led landed in" : The Luna box retells the section above it (the landing, the hurricane, the seven ships, the 200 men sent inland, Nanipacana, Villafañe's arrival), and the two tellings differ on what the 200 men did, so the reader is unsure which is right : Cut the box to what is new (Coosa, the refused march) and make the 200-men account match.
- [Era 6, 1800 to 1850] "What became of Charles. Charles Lamar" : The Rebecca Lamar box breaks off to a slave-smuggling voyage of 1858 that is not a disaster and is tied to the chapter in no stated way, then goes back to Rebecca's later life, so the reader loses the thread : Cut it, or add one sentence tying it to the chapter's question about punishment, and end the box on Rebecca.
- [Era 7, 1850 to 1900] "Who got the food. Red Cross" : The Sea Islands section poses the question and never says whether the Red Cross gave in to the pressure (it ends on a governor's collection for white farmers), while the Barton box says her relief reached about 30,000 people, most of them Black, so the reader cannot tell who got the food : State what the Red Cross did and make the section and the box agree.
- [Closer] "After many of these disasters someone" : The second half of the first question, did the rules work, gets two short sentences and one failure (the 1838 steamboat law), though the chapter shows others (the lifeboat law and the Eastland, locked exits at the Cocoanut Grove, the Katrina floodwalls) : Give a plain verdict by rule, grouped as worked, failed and still open.
- [Era 4, 1700 to 1750] "New York officials said that enslaved" : The section reports the officials' claim of a plot, the trials and the burnings, but never says what the evidence was or whether anyone believes the plot was real, so the reader cannot tell whether those put to death were guilty (the 1776 fire section does give historians' views) : Add one plain sentence on what historians conclude.
- [Era 10, 2000 to Today] "In an October 2024 report, federal" : It reads as a contradiction, since the same section says power lines started the fire and Hawaiian Electric agreed to pay 4 billion dollars, and the two sentences after it (the old capital, inherited homes) have no stated link to the fire : Explain what the report's finding means and tie the last two sentences to the loss.
- [Era 9, 1950 to 2000] "A later study counted 692 deaths" : The heat wave is first given as 739 extra deaths in one week, then this later study gives 692 for the whole summer, which reads as a smaller summer than week : Explain the difference or give one figure.
- [Era 8, 1900 to 1950] "Isaac Cline took charge of the" : It says he headed Galveston's Weather Bureau office as early as 1889, but Era 7 says the Bureau began with the 1890 law and weather work before that was in the Army : Say weather office for 1889 and note when it joined the Bureau.
- [Era 8, 1900 to 1950] "They put that blame on three" : The coroner's jury is said to blame the public as one of three groups, with no reason given, which reads as blaming the victims : Give the jury's reason in a clause or leave the public out.
- [Era 10, 2000 to Today] "At Memorial Medical Center, 45 bodies" : The section goes straight from 45 bodies to a murder arrest with no word on the hospital's situation after the storm, so the reader lacks the context to understand the accusation : Add the setting before the arrests.
- [Era 10, 2000 to Today] "A 15-million-dollar repair had been approved" : The Surfside section stops here with no word on help for families, a rule that followed, or any punishment, though the chapter promised these three for every era, and why it matters that each family owned its apartment is never said : End on what followed or say the records do not yet say, and explain the ownership point or cut it.
- [Era 8, 1900 to 1950] "Between 6,000 and 8,000 people died" : Galveston's paragraph holds five figures (the city toll, the island toll, a final list of 4,263, a third of the city, 2,636 to 3,600 homes) and the list being lower than the toll is unexplained : Keep the toll range and one line on the list, drop the rest.
- [Era 7, 1850 to 1900] "The count of the dead was" : The Johnstown paragraph stacks nine figures (2,209, 2,208, 99 families, 396 children, 777 unidentified, 124 widows, 198 widowers, 98 orphans, 568 children who lost a parent) : Keep the toll, the families and the unidentified, and drop the rest.
- [Era 2, The 1500s] "The Stahle team writes that the" : The sentence gives the Croatan's hunger as part of the reason for the shooting and leaves out the link (they had come to the empty town for food, so they were there when White's men attacked), so it reads as blaming the victims : Say that the Croatan were in the empty town looking for food when they were attacked.
- [Era 10, 2000 to Today] "Inspectors of Tennessee's workplace safety agency" : The agency is said to find the deaths not work-related though the dead were at work, and no reason is given, so the finding reads as a puzzle : Add the agency's reason in a clause.
- [Era 10, 2000 to Today] "In the week before July 18," : The section's last sentence says officials used three of six new sirens, with no sign of when or what for, so it dangles : Say what the sirens were used for or cut it.
- [Era 10, 2000 to Today] "He gave no evidence." : The Maria section closes on a short verdict about a living person in the book's own voice, which reads as performed and as taking a side : State neutrally what the tweet said and what the studies counted, and end on the count.
- [Closer] "Over ten eras, help after a" : The closer says help moved to a national office and warnings to sirens, but Era 10 shows the national office failing at Katrina and Maria and under threat, and sirens missing or silent at Kerr County and Maui, so the closer reads as progress the last era undercuts : Add the Era 10 turn.
- [Era 9, 1950 to 2000] "Six people were holding on to" : The Windsor box repeats the Air Florida section (six people on the wreck, the helicopter at 4:20, the man who passed the line, the Carnegie medals), so the reader reads the rescue twice : Keep Windsor's own part and point back.
- [Era 10, 2000 to Today] "Rinderknecht's federal trial ended on June" : Two paragraphs of legal steps and dates for a case still open (June 26, August 19, October 22, 26 and 29, November 2) read as a date wall : Keep one sentence on where the case stands.
- [Era 7, 1850 to 1900] "Clara Barton founded the American Red" : The 1881 founding and the Michigan fires as first relief appear in the era intro, the Red Cross section and this box, and Chapter 26's Barton box already gave the founding date and the 1904 resignation in nearly the same words : Keep the Johnstown and Sea Islands details and point to Chapter 26.
- [Era 3, The 1600s] "Tree rings show that the colonists" : Chapter 26 already told Jamestown's first years (the drought of 1606 to 1612, the salty river, the Starving Time), and the two chapters frame the numbers differently (half of 104 dead by September 1607 there, 38 alive after a year here) : Point back to Chapter 26 and keep what is new here (the relief fleet, the hurricane, the killing).
- [Era 9, 1950 to 2000] "Forecasters warned the whole Louisiana coast" : The Audrey warning is told three times (era intro, first paragraph, and here), each time with the day-before timing : Tell it once.
- [Era 8, 1900 to 1950] "By the end of 1911: Thousands" : Recap lines sit under unrelated headings (here Ships, later Floods) and the 1929 one sits inside the Black Sunday section the card says to skip, so it reads as part of the wrong section and a skipper loses it : Put each before its heading.

Flow: uneven. Most sections run cleanly from the choice to the help to the rule, but the late eras pile up legal dates, counts and loose ends, and several boxes repeat the section above them.
