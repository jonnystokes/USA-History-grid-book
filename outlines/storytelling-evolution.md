## Before 1500
<!-- hb-zoom level="era" -->
People in this land told stories out loud and acted them out. There was no building called a theatre, no ticket, and no job called actor. Acting was part of prayer, healing, teaching and law. Nations north of Mexico kept their stories in memory, not in writing, so every written description of these traditions dates from after 1500. What survives from before 1500 is the ground people built to gather on, a few painted walls, and a few masks.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Playing someone who is not you" -->
- **Hopi and Zuni katsinam.** Katsinam are more than 500 spirit beings in Pueblo religion (Britannica). Pueblo men wear katsina masks and regalia in dances, and the spirit shown on the mask is believed to be present in the dancer (Britannica). The oldest clear pictures of masked katsina figures are on kiva walls and pots in Hopi country and at Homol'ovi, from about 1300 to 1350 (Adams, *Kiva* 1989, abstract).
- **Yup'ik masks.** Yup'ik dancers in what is now Alaska wore masks in the winter ceremony and took on the spirits of the animals the masks showed (Fienup-Riordan, in *Archaeology*, 2015). After a dance the masks were usually burned, broken or left on the tundra. Masks from Nunalleq, a village near Quinhagak lived in from about 1300 to the 1650s, are the oldest complete Yup'ik masks known (*Archaeology*, 2015). Some may date from before 1500. The dig dates the village, not each mask.
- **Haudenosaunee medicine masks.** Haudenosaunee medicine societies use wooden masks and cornhusk masks in healing. In 1995 the Grand Council of the Haudenosaunee stated that all such masks are sacred, that only society members may use them, and that the public showing of the masks, or of pictures of them, is forbidden (Akwesasne Notes, 1995). No date for when the practice began is sourced.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Rules about who tells what, and when" -->
- **Winter only.** Ojibwe people tell their sacred stories, the aadizookaanag, only in winter. James Vukelich Kaagegaabaw (Turtle Mountain Ojibwe) says tellers wait until the lakes freeze, the bears and frogs are asleep and snow covers the ground (MPR News, 2024). The spirits in the stories are then thought to be asleep, and telling the stories at another season is disrespect toward them (MPR News, 2024).
- **Stories as property.** Tlingit clans on the Northwest Coast own their crests, and the stories, songs and names that go with them, as sacred clan property called at.óow (Sealaska Heritage Institute). The clan holds these because an ancestor met a spirit being at a certain place, and the clan keeps exclusive rights to them through the generations (Sealaska Heritage Institute).
- These are statements by the nations themselves, made in the 1990s to 2020s. They describe living rules. The sources do not date the rules to before 1500.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Places built to hold an audience" -->
- **Cahokia**, near present-day St. Louis in Illinois. People there built a Grand Plaza about 1,600 feet long and 900 feet wide. Published figures give its area as 40, 47 or about 50 acres (UNF Cahokia lidar site. Wikipedia, "Cahokia"). Builders cut down a sand ridge and filled low ground by hand to make it level (Holley, Dalan and Smith, 1993, via UNF). Archaeologists describe the plazas as the center of the city's ritual and political gatherings (UNF).
- **Chaco Canyon**, New Mexico. Casa Rinconada is a great kiva, a large round room partly underground, about 63 feet across inside. It had a bench around the wall and 34 wall niches. A tunnel 39 feet long let people enter the kiva floor unseen and then appear (Chaco Research Archive). It was built about the middle 1000s and used into the early 1100s (Chaco Research Archive). No seat count is sourced.
- **Hopi plazas.** Katsina dances move out of the kivas into the village plazas in spring and summer, and the Niman dance in July opens in the plaza at sunrise (Kachina House calendar, a trader's page) (unconfirmed: search summary only).
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="What the record does not have" -->
No person from before 1500 in this land who acted, danced or told stories is named in any surviving record. The traditions continued through every era below, and named performers working in them are documented from the 1800s onward.

## The 1500s
<!-- hb-zoom level="era" -->
Almost no Europeans lived in this land in the 1500s, so almost no European plays were performed here. Native nations went on with their own ceremonies. The first European play on record in what is now the United States came at the very end of the century, in 1598. Spanish soldiers and priests acted it on the bank of the Rio Grande, as part of an expedition that had come to take New Mexico for the king of Spain.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="April 30, 1598: the play at the river" -->
- Juan de Oñate's colonizing expedition reached the Rio Grande near present-day San Elizario, Texas, on April 20, 1598 (TSHA, "San Elizario"). On April 30 the expedition held a Mass of thanksgiving, and Oñate read "La Toma," a formal claim of all the land the river drained for King Philip II (TSHA. El Paso Mission Trail).
- That afternoon the expedition put on a play written by Captain Marcos Farfán de los Godos. The stage was bare ground. A canvas curtain hung between two cottonwood trunks was the backdrop. Soldiers and friars (Catholic priests) played the main parts (Marc Simmons, historian, *Santa Fe New Mexican* "Trail Dust" column).
- Simmons writes that Manso people found near the camp were "pressed into service" and played the Pueblo people who lived farther north (Simmons). An officer who watched described the plot: friars arrive in New Mexico, and Native people kneel and ask to be baptized (Simmons, quoting a witness).
- The soldiers cheered and fired their guns in the air at the end. No script survives, and the title is uncertain (Simmons). One later account gives it as a "comedia" about the church's welcome in New Mexico (Villagrá, Canto XIV) (unconfirmed: search summary only).
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="The same expedition at Acoma" -->
- In early December 1598, Oñate's nephew Juan de Zaldívar and his soldiers climbed the mesa at Acoma Pueblo and demanded food. Acoma men killed Zaldívar and between 11 and 14 of his men (sources differ). The All Pueblo Council of Governors says the soldiers had also assaulted an Acoma woman (bank, native-nations §Acoma).
- In January 1599 Oñate sent Zaldívar's brother Vicente with about 70 soldiers. They stormed the mesa and burned much of the pueblo. No one counted the Acoma dead. Published estimates run from about 300 to about 1,500. About 800 is the figure given most often, and it is the figure the Pueblos themselves use (native-nations bank).
- In February 1599 Oñate sentenced every Acoma man over 25 to have a foot cut off and to twenty years of forced service as a servant. He sentenced younger men and women over 12 to twenty years of forced service, and he had children under 12 taken from their families. The number 24 is reported for the men whose feet were cut. Historians disagree over whether the cutting was carried out in full, and over whether the whole foot or the toes were meant (native-nations bank).
- In 1614 a Spanish court in Mexico convicted Oñate of using excessive force at Acoma, among 12 charges. He was fined and banished from New Mexico for life (native-nations bank). `native-nations` leads on Acoma.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Los Moros y Cristianos" -->
- Oñate's soldiers also performed *Los Moros y Cristianos* (The Moors and the Christians) in 1598, after the expedition reached the pueblo the Spanish renamed San Juan, now Ohkay Owingeh (Marc Simmons, *Santa Fe New Mexican*, 2016). The play acts out old wars in Spain between Muslim armies and Christian armies, with long speeches between the leaders and battle scenes with clanging weapons (Simmons).
- New Mexicans still perform it. A troupe from Chimayó has staged it in recent times, and it was performed at the University of New Mexico in 1998 for the 400th anniversary of Oñate's colony (Simmons, 2016).
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-story:start slug="marcos-farfan-de-los-godos" name="Marcos Farfán de los Godos" movie="" kind="ordinary" status="verified" -->
### Marcos Farfán de los Godos
> **Who:** A captain in Juan de Oñate's expedition who wrote the first European play on record in what is now the United States · **When and where:** the Rio Grande near present-day El Paso, April 30, 1598
He was born in Seville, Spain. When the expedition's officers were counted before it left Mexico, he brought two servants, 16 horses, a tent, weapons and armor (Simmons, from the muster roll). He opened his play himself with a speech, calling himself unworthy to have written it and offering it to his fellow soldiers and to Oñate (Simmons). The script does not survive. In November 1598 Oñate sent him west past the Hopi villages to look for mines. His party reached the Verde Valley in what is now Arizona and staked 14 or 15 mining claims near present-day Jerome (NAU, "Captain Farfán and His Forgotten Trail"). The date of his death is not known (Wikipedia, "Marcos Farfán de los Godos").

## The 1600s
<!-- hb-zoom level="era" -->
The English colonies had no theatre buildings in the 1600s, and nobody in them made a living by acting. The first play in English on record here is known only because a neighbor reported the actors to a Virginia court. In New Mexico, Spanish officials and Franciscan priests (Catholic missionaries) raided Pueblo ceremonial rooms and burned the masks used in dances. In 1680 the Pueblos drove the Spanish out and went back to their own ceremonies.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Plays banned in England, and no stage in New England" -->
- On September 2, 1642, as the English Civil War began, the Lords and Commons in Parliament ordered that "Public Stage Plays shall cease." Their order called plays "Spectacles of Pleasure, too commonly expressing lascivious Mirth and Levity" (Acts and Ordinances of the Interregnum, British History Online). A 1648 act went further: actors could be punished as rogues, and theatre seating was to be torn down (EBSCO) (unconfirmed: search summary only). London theatres opened again in August 1660, after Charles II became king (unconfirmed: search summary only).
- In 1687 the Boston minister Increase Mather printed an attack on Christmas customs that listed "interludes," short plays, among the sins of the holiday (Mather, *A Testimony against several Prophane and Superstitious Customs*, 1687) (unconfirmed: search summary only). `holidays` leads on the Christmas ban.
- In 1690 students at Harvard College acted *Gustavus Vasa*, a tragedy by Benjamin Colman, and local officials disapproved (Encyclopedia.com, "1600-1754: Theater". Colman named by Historic Interpreter blog, citing Hornblow 1919). Theatre historian Arthur Hornblow (1919) called it the first play written by an American to be acted in America (via Historic Interpreter blog).
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="August 27, 1665: a play, a complaint, and a court" -->
- On August 27, 1665, Cornelius Watkinson, Philip Howard and William Darby acted a play called *Ye Bare and Ye Cubb* at a tavern near Pungoteague in Accomack County, Virginia. Later writers give the tavern's name as Fowkes, Cowle's or Cole's. A man named Edward Martin reported the play to John Fawsett, the king's attorney for the county (Accomack County court record, printed in Campbell's *History of Virginia*).
- On November 16, 1665, Fawsett accused the three men in the county court. The justices ordered them to come to the next court "in those habilements that they then acted in," meaning their costumes, and to bring a written copy of the verses and speeches. The sheriff held Watkinson and Howard until they paid a bond, and arrested Darby (court record).
- At a court in January 1666 the justices found the three men "not guilty of fault." They ordered Martin, who had made the complaint, to pay all the costs of the case (court record). The record does not say whether the men acted the play in front of the justices.
- No script survives. Everything known about the first play in English on record in what is now the United States comes from these court orders.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Pueblo ceremonies attacked, and brought back" -->
- From 1656 to 1665, the Franciscan leader Alonso de Posada forbade Pueblo ceremonies. He ordered missionaries to go into kivas, the Pueblos' sacred ceremonial rooms, burn the masks there, and take or destroy sacred objects. Armed Spanish soldiers protected the missionaries (Dennis Zotigh, National Museum of the American Indian, 2026).
- In 1675 Governor Juan Francisco Treviño had 47 Pueblo religious leaders arrested and charged with sorcery. He sentenced four to hang. Spanish officials hanged three, and the fourth killed himself. The rest were whipped in public and jailed. Pueblo men came to Santa Fe in force and demanded their release, and Treviño let them go. One of the men freed was Po'pay of Ohkay Owingeh (Zotigh, NMAI).
- Po'pay planned a revolt of the Pueblos. It began on August 10, 1680. Pueblo fighters killed about 400 people, including 21 of the 33 Spanish priests in New Mexico. About 2,000 surviving settlers fled south to El Paso (Zotigh, NMAI). After the Spanish left, the Pueblos destroyed the churches and restored their own religious practice (Zotigh, NMAI). The Spanish came back 12 years later. `native-nations` leads on the revolt.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="What the record does not show" -->
Africans were first brought to Virginia by force in 1619 and sold there. Storytelling, music and dance mattered in the lives of enslaved people (American Battlefield Trust). No record from the 1600s describes a performance by an enslaved person in the English colonies. The earliest picture of one is a painting from the late 1700s. `music` leads on the songs.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-story:start slug="william-darby-1665" name="William Darby" movie="" kind="ordinary" status="verified" -->
### William Darby
> **Who:** One of three men who acted *Ye Bare and Ye Cubb* in Virginia in 1665, and the one the sheriff was ordered to arrest · **When and where:** Accomack County, Virginia, August 27, 1665, and the county court, November 1665 to January 1666
The court record calls him an "actour" of the play. Later writers name him as its author, and an academic book about the play credits it to "W. Darby" (Mellen Press) (unconfirmed: search summary only). In November 1665 the justices ordered the sheriff to arrest him "to answere at his maties suit," meaning a case brought in the king's name. In January 1666 the justices found all three men not guilty (Accomack County court record). The court record does not state his trade, his birth or his death.

## 1700 to 1750
<!-- hb-zoom level="era" -->
Between 1700 and 1750 a few actors from England worked in the colonies, and the first buildings for plays went up in Williamsburg, Virginia and Charleston, South Carolina. Two of the first actors were servants bound by contract to the man who built the Williamsburg playhouse. In Pennsylvania and New England, lawmakers and ministers worked to keep plays out. The biggest crowds of these fifty years came to hear a preacher, George Whitefield, who had acted in plays as a schoolboy.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="The first actors and the first playhouses" -->
- **Charleston, about 1703.** The English actor Anthony Aston wrote that he arrived in Charleston poor and hungry, became a "Player and Poet," and wrote a play about the colony. No copy of the play survives (Aston's preface to *The Fool's Opera*, quoted by NCpedia). Sources date his arrival 1701, 1702 or 1703.
- **Boston, 1714.** Judge Samuel Sewall wrote a letter against a plan to act a play in the Council Chamber, the room where the colony's council met. He wrote: "Let not Christian Boston goe beyond Heathen Rome in the practice of Shamefull vanities." No record shows the play was performed (Historic Interpreter blog, citing Daly 1896).
- **Williamsburg, 1716 to 1745.** In 1716 William Levingston, a merchant, signed a contract with Charles Stagg and his wife Mary Stagg, who were his indentured servants. An indentured servant was bound by contract to work for a master for a set number of years. Levingston agreed to build a playhouse, and the Staggs agreed to act and dance in it (Encyclopedia Virginia. Colonial Williamsburg research, via search). Sources give the contract date as July 11 or November 19, 1716. The first play on record there was acted in 1718, for the king's birthday, and Governor Alexander Spotswood sponsored it (Encyclopedia Virginia). No list of the plays Levingston staged survives. College of William and Mary students acted plays in the building in 1735 and 1736 (Historic Interpreter blog, citing Hornblow 1919).
- **Charleston, 1735 and 1736.** On January 24, 1735, a company acted Thomas Otway's tragedy *The Orphan* in the "Court-room" of Charles Shepheard's tavern on Broad Street, a room the colony's government rented for trials. Six ticketed shows followed through March, at forty shillings in South Carolina money. Most of the actors' names were not printed. A dancer named Henry Holt probably led them (Nic Butler, Charleston County Public Library, 2024). The "New Theatre in Dock Street" opened in February 1736, built for plays (Butler). Its first play was George Farquhar's comedy *The Recruiting Officer* on February 12, 1736 (Charleston Stage) (unconfirmed: search summary only).
- **Philadelphia, 1749.** Walter Murray and Thomas Kean led a company of actors that opened Joseph Addison's tragedy *Cato* on August 22, 1749, (Encyclopedia.com. Holy Cross classics site). They played in a warehouse owned by the merchant William Plumsted (unconfirmed: search summary only). In January 1750 the city's Common Council condemned the players (unconfirmed: search summary only).
- **Whose land.** Williamsburg stands on land the Paspahegh and Chiskiack peoples used before the English, both part of the Powhatan paramount chiefdom, Tsenacomoco (Encyclopedia Virginia). The Etiwan people lived on the Charleston peninsula until about 1672, when English colonial officials paid them trade goods to leave so the town could be laid out there (Nic Butler, Charleston County Public Library, 2022).
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Laws against plays" -->
- Pennsylvania's lawmakers passed laws against stage plays in 1700, 1706 and 1713. Officials in London overturned them. Queen Anne refused one because it kept her subjects from "Innocent Sports and Diversions" (unconfirmed: search summary only). The 1750 Massachusetts ban on plays belongs to the next era.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Preaching as performance" -->
- George Whitefield, the English preacher, wrote that as a schoolboy of about twelve in Gloucester, England, he "was very fond of reading plays," and stayed away from school "for days together to prepare myself for acting them" (Whitefield's *Journals*, via Banner of Truth).
- He toured the colonies from 1739 and preached outdoors. Benjamin Franklin heard him preach from the courthouse steps on Market Street in Philadelphia. Franklin walked backward down the street until he could no longer hear him, then worked out the size of a half circle of that radius at two square feet per listener. He "computed that he might well be heard by more than thirty thousand" (Franklin, *Autobiography*).
- Franklin also noticed that Whitefield got better at sermons he repeated on tour: "every accent, every emphasis, every modulation of voice, was so perfectly well turn'd and well plac'd" (Franklin, *Autobiography*). Franklin compared the preacher's advantage to rehearsal. `religion` leads on Whitefield as a preacher, and on his campaign to bring slavery into Georgia.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Pinkster and Black election days" -->
- Pinkster, the Dutch name for the Christian feast of Pentecost, came to New Netherland with Dutch settlers in the 1620s. The oldest written account of it is in the *New-York Weekly Journal* of March 1737, which describes a gathering in fields outside New York City with African-style musical instruments (Historic Hudson Valley). Enslavers gave enslaved people several days off, and many used them to travel to see family (Historic Hudson Valley).
- Black communities in Connecticut held their own elections for a governor, "possibly as early as 1749." A man named Hercules served as governor at New London in 1749 (Connecticut State Library). Voters dressed in their best clothes, some borrowed from their enslavers, and paraded. The enslaver of the man elected was expected to pay for the food and drink (Connecticut State Library).
- These were festivals with parades, music, dancing and a crowned leader. They were staged by Black people, enslaved and free, who were kept off every stage in the colonies. `holidays` leads on them as festivals.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-story:start slug="william-levingston" name="William Levingston" movie="" kind="ordinary" status="verified" -->
### William Levingston
> **Who:** A merchant and dancing master who built the first playhouse in the English colonies, with two indentured servants as his actors · **When and where:** New Kent County and Williamsburg, Virginia, 1716 to about 1727
Before the playhouse, Levingston ran a traveling dancing school in New Kent County, and Charles and Mary Stagg were its star dancers (Tyler 1907, via Historic Interpreter blog). His 1716 contract freed the Staggs from their earlier indenture to him in exchange for performing on his stage and teaching others to act. Levingston promised to build "one good substentiall House commodious for acting such Plays as shall be thought fitt to be acted there" at his own cost (contract text, Colonial Williamsburg research report, via search) (unconfirmed: search summary only). On land by the Palace Green he built the playhouse, a house, a kitchen, a stable and a bowling green. Archaeologists found its foundations: 30 feet wide and 86.5 feet long (Colonial Williamsburg, via search) (unconfirmed: search summary only). The business did not pay. He mortgaged the property to Dr. Archibald Blair in 1721, and Blair foreclosed, meaning he took the property for the unpaid debt, in 1723. Levingston appears to have been out of business by 1727 (Colonial Williamsburg, via search. Historic Interpreter blog). In 1745 a group of Virginia men bought the empty building and gave it to Williamsburg as a town hall (Hornblow 1919, via Historic Interpreter blog).
<!-- hb-story:end slug="william-levingston" -->
<!-- hb-story:start slug="anthony-aston" name="Anthony Aston" movie="" kind="ordinary" status="verified" -->
### Anthony Aston
> **Who:** An English lawyer turned actor, usually named as the first professional actor to work in the English colonies · **When and where:** Charleston, the Albemarle region of North Carolina, New York and Virginia, about 1701 to 1703
Aston told his own story in the preface to his play *The Fool's Opera*, printed in London around 1730. He listed himself as "a gentleman, lawyer, poet, actor, soldier, sailer, exciseman, publican." He wrote that he reached Charleston "full of Lice, Shame, Poverty, Nakedness and Hunger," and that he "turned Player and Poet, and wrote one Play on the subject of the Country" (quoted in NCpedia and Historic Interpreter blog). He then spent about a month in North Carolina's Albemarle region, housed by two settlers, Allen and Abraham Waights (NCpedia, William S. Powell, 2006). A storm blew his ship to the Virginia capes on the way to New York. In New York he spent a winter "acting, writing, courting, fighting," then sailed to Virginia and later went back to England (Aston's preface, via Historic Interpreter blog). His play about the colony does not survive.

## 1750 to 1800
<!-- hb-zoom level="era" -->
In 1752 a family company of actors from London began touring the colonies, and from then on some people in America earned their living by acting. Lawmakers in Massachusetts, Pennsylvania and Rhode Island passed laws against plays. In 1774 the Continental Congress asked Americans to give up plays during the quarrel with Britain, and the leading company of actors left for the West Indies. British officers put on plays in Boston while the city was under siege, and American officers acted *Cato* at Valley Forge. After the war the company came back. The first plays by American writers were acted in these years, and one of them put a new kind of character on the stage, the plain-spoken country Yankee.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Laws against plays" -->
- In 1750 the Massachusetts General Court, the colony's lawmakers, passed "An Act for preventing Stage-Plays, and other Theatrical Entertainments." The act said that plays "tend to increase immorality, impiety, and a contempt of religion" (Forgotten Chapters of Boston's Literary History). It set a fine of twenty pounds for each day a person rented out a room for a play, and five pounds for each actor or audience member at a play with more than twenty people present (unconfirmed: search summary only). Earlier that year two English actors and some local volunteers had acted Thomas Otway's *The Orphan* in a coffee house on State Street in Boston (unconfirmed: search summary only).
- Pennsylvania's lawmakers passed a law against acting plays on May 31, 1759, with a penalty of five hundred pounds. Rhode Island passed "an act to Prevent Stage Plays and other Theatrical Entertainments" in 1761. In 1762 New Hampshire's lawmakers refused to let a company of actors into Portsmouth (Historic Interpreter blog, citing Hornblow 1919).
- On May 5, 1766, after news came that Parliament had repealed the Stamp Act, a crowd in New York tore down the theatre in Chapel Street, where a comedy was advertised that night (Daly, *First Theater in America*, 1896, quoting the playbill and Gabriel Furman). One modern account says the crowd, led by the Sons of Liberty, beat an actor and killed a child (David Malinsky, *Journal of the American Revolution*, 2013). The older accounts do not mention anyone killed, and no source names the child.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="1752: the first touring company" -->
- The London actor Lewis Hallam led a company of actors to Virginia in 1752. The company included his wife and their children. It opened in Williamsburg with Shakespeare's *The Merchant of Venice* on September 15, 1752 (Historic Interpreter blog. Colonial Williamsburg) (Daly quotes William Dunlap giving September 5). The company acted the plays then popular in London: Shakespeare and Joseph Addison, and comedies by William Congreve and Richard Steele (Encyclopedia.com, "Lewis Hallam Sr. and Jr.").
- The company played Williamsburg, Annapolis, Philadelphia and New York, where Hallam built a theatre on Nassau Street in 1754 (Historic Interpreter blog). Lewis Hallam died in Jamaica, where the company had gone to perform. His widow, Sarah Hallam, married the actor-manager David Douglass, and in 1758 they formed the American Company (Historic Interpreter blog. Encyclopedia.com). Douglass built theatres wherever he found a town that would allow one: Philadelphia in 1759, a brick theatre in Annapolis that held five to six hundred people in 1760, and New York in 1761 (Daly).
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="The first American plays" -->
- *The Prince of Parthia*, a tragedy by Thomas Godfrey of Philadelphia, was acted by the American Company at the Southwark Theatre in Philadelphia on April 24, 1767. It was the first play by an American writer acted by professional actors. Godfrey had died of yellow fever in 1763 and never saw it staged. Lewis Hallam the Younger played the hero, Arsaces (Wikipedia, "The Prince of Parthia". Encyclopedia.com).
- Mercy Otis Warren of Massachusetts wrote plays that attacked the royal governor and his supporters: *The Adulateur* (1772), *The Defeat* (1773), *The Group* (1775), *The Blockheads* (1776) and *The Motley Assembly* (1779). She published all of them without her name (National Women's History Museum). They reached people in print. Massachusetts law had banned acting plays since 1750.
- *The Contrast*, a comedy by Royall Tyler, opened at the John Street Theatre in New York on April 16, 1787. It was the first comedy by an American acted by professionals (Wikipedia, "The Contrast". Britannica). Tyler wrote it after meeting Thomas Wignell, the American Company's leading comic actor (Britannica). Its servant character Jonathan is a New England country man, plain-spoken, honest and new to the city. Britannica calls him "the predecessor of many such in years to follow," and later playwrights kept writing Yankee characters like him.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Congress against plays, and armies acting them" -->
- On October 20, 1774, the delegates to the First Continental Congress signed the Continental Association. In its eighth article they promised to "discountenance and discourage every species of extravagance and dissipation, especially all horse-racing, and all kinds of games, cock fighting, exhibitions of shews, plays, and other expensive diversions and entertainments" (Journals of the Continental Congress, Avalon Project). The American Company left for the West Indies (Encyclopedia.com).
- British officers acted plays in Boston while American troops held the city under siege. On December 2, 1775, officers staged Aaron Hill's tragedy *Zara* in Faneuil Hall, with a prologue by General John Burgoyne, and gave the money to soldiers' widows and children (Massachusetts Historical Society).
- American officers at Valley Forge acted Joseph Addison's *Cato* in spring 1778. Lieutenant Colonel William Bradford Jr. wrote to his sister that "Cato was performed before a very numerous & splendid audience." He named George and Martha Washington among the audience (Mount Vernon. Shawn David McGhee, *Journal of the American Revolution*, 2024). Sources date the performance May 4 or May 11, 1778. The audience was officers and their guests. McGhee argues it may have been a reading rather than a full staging.
- In October 1778 Congress asked the states to suppress "theatrical entertainments." On October 16 the delegates resolved that any official who would "act, promote, encourage or attend such plays, shall be deemed unworthy to hold such office" (Malinsky, *Journal of the American Revolution*, 2013).
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="After the war" -->
- Lewis Hallam the Younger brought the American Company back in 1784 and 1785 and reopened theatres in Philadelphia and New York (Encyclopedia.com. Historic Interpreter blog).
- Boston still banned plays. In 1792 a group of Boston men built the "New Exhibition Room" on Board Alley and advertised its plays as "moral lectures." Governor John Hancock forced it to close in June 1793 (Wikipedia, "Board Alley Theatre").
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-story:start slug="lewis-hallam-younger" name="Lewis Hallam the Younger" movie="" kind="famous" status="verified" -->
### Lewis Hallam the Younger
> **Who:** A boy in the first touring company of actors in the colonies, who became the leading actor and manager of the American Company · **When and where:** born about 1740 in England, came to Virginia in 1752, died in Philadelphia on November 1, 1808
He was about twelve when his father's company landed in Virginia (Daly, citing Dunlap, who got the story from Hallam himself). In 1758, at eighteen, he became the leading man of the American Company, run by his mother and stepfather (Encyclopedia.com). He played Arsaces in *The Prince of Parthia* in 1767, the first professional staging of a play by an American writer. When Congress turned against plays before the Revolution, he went to the West Indies. He brought the company back in 1784 and 1785 and managed it until 1796. He kept acting until shortly before his death, about fifty years on the stage (Encyclopedia.com. Britannica). Britannica describes him as a skilled actor and a "quarrelsome and financially inept manager."
<!-- hb-story:end slug="lewis-hallam-younger" -->
<!-- hb-story:start slug="mercy-otis-warren" name="Mercy Otis Warren" movie="" kind="famous" status="verified" -->
### Mercy Otis Warren
> **Who:** A Massachusetts writer who used plays to attack the royal government when Massachusetts law banned acting them · **When and where:** born in Barnstable, Massachusetts, September 14, 1728, plays from 1772 to 1779, died 1814
Her first play, *The Adulateur*, was printed in 1772 in the *Massachusetts Spy*, a Boston newspaper, and attacked the royal governor's policies. Four more followed through 1779. She signed none of them. The National Women's History Museum says she hid her name to avoid punishment and to have her work taken seriously as a woman writer. In 1805 she published a three-volume history of the Revolution (National Women's History Museum). `art` leads on her as a writer.
<!-- hb-story:end slug="mercy-otis-warren" -->
<!-- hb-story:start slug="nancy-hallam" name="Nancy Hallam" movie="" kind="ordinary" status="verified" -->
### Nancy Hallam
> **Who:** An actress and singer in the American Company, and the subject of an early painting of an actress in a role in America · **When and where:** Annapolis, Maryland, 1770 and 1771
She was the niece of Sarah Hallam Douglass. She trained as a singer in England from 1760 to 1765 and was with the American Company by 1766. She moved from young women's parts to leading roles in 1769 (Historic Annapolis). In 1770 in Annapolis she played Imogen in Shakespeare's *Cymbeline*, a princess who disguises herself as a young man named Fidele. On September 6, 1770, the *Maryland Gazette* printed an unsigned letter praising "the Musick of her Tongue" (Historic Annapolis). Charles Willson Peale painted her in the role, dressed as Fidele, and the painting was on view by September 1771. It now belongs to Colonial Williamsburg (Historic Annapolis). She went back to Britain in 1773. Her later life and the date of her death are not known (Wikipedia, "Nancy Hallam").

## 1800 to 1850
<!-- hb-zoom level="era" -->
Between 1800 and 1850, actors in the United States played Shakespeare in city theatres, on river boats and in rooms over taverns. The first Black theatre company opened in New York in 1821. Police arrested its actors in the middle of a play, and its best young actor, Ira Aldridge, left for England, where he could get work. In 1828 a white actor named Thomas Dartmouth Rice blacked his face with burnt cork and acted a Black man as a fool, and by 1843 white men in the same makeup were filling whole evenings with the act. In 1849 the fans of two rival actors fought outside a New York theatre. A city judge, a sheriff and a militia general gave soldiers the order to fire into the crowd, and historians count between 22 and 31 dead.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="The African Theatre, New York, 1821 to 1823" -->
- William Brown, a free Black man from the West Indies who had worked as a ship's steward, ran a tea garden behind his house at 38 Thomas Street in New York, with music on Sunday afternoons (BlackPast. Village Preservation). Sources give his middle name as Alexander or Henry.
- In 1821 he opened a theatre of about 300 seats on the upper floor of a house at Mercer and Bleecker Streets, with an all-Black company, the first known in the United States. It opened with Shakespeare's *Richard III*. Sources date the first night September 17, 21 or 24, 1821 (*New York Times*, 2021. BlackPast. MacDonald, Folger 2024). An enslaved man played the king in a robe made from a window curtain. James Hewlett took over the part (*New York Times*, 2021).
- The company also played *Othello*, comedies and pantomimes, and white New Yorkers began to come as well as Black ones (BlackPast. *New York Times*). In 1823 it staged Brown's own play *The Drama of King Shotaway*, about the Black Carib war against English and French settlers on St. Vincent in 1796. No copy of the play survives (BlackPast. Village Preservation).
- White men attacked the company. A group including a man named George Bellmont broke into the theatre, beat the actors and wrecked the costumes and scenery. Police arrested Bellmont and several others (Wikipedia, "African Theatre").
- Brown moved his company next door to the Park Theatre, run by the white manager Stephen Price, and played *Richard III* there at the same time as Price. White audience members heckled and disrupted the show. Police arrested Brown's actors during a performance and jailed them. The police let them go only after they promised to stop acting Shakespeare (Joyce Green MacDonald, Folger 2024, citing Marvin McAllister. Shakespeare Theatre Company). No surviving record gives the date or names the officers.
- Mordecai Noah, editor of the *National Advocate*, mocked the company in his paper and warned that free Black New Yorkers might one day "outvote the whites" (MacDonald, quoting McAllister).
- In October 1822 the *National Advocate* announced that the theatre was closing because of a yellow fever outbreak. The last known playbill is from June 1823 (*New York Times*, 2021).
- In London the English comedian Charles Mathews mocked the company in his show *A Trip to America*, although he had never been to the theatre. In May 1824 Hewlett answered him in the *National Advocate*: "Was this well for a brother actor?" (Shakespeare Theatre Company. Wikipedia, "Ira Aldridge").
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Blackface minstrelsy: the act and the makeup" -->
- In blackface minstrelsy, white actors darkened their faces with burnt cork and played Black people as lazy, stupid and happy to be enslaved, for white audiences who paid to laugh (Jim Crow Museum). `music` tells the songs and the money made from them. This span tells the acting.
- Thomas Dartmouth Rice was a white actor who did short comic acts between the scenes of plays. In 1828 he first went on stage as "Jim Crow," a Black character acted as a fool, with his skin blacked with burnt cork. He said he copied a Black man he had seen singing. Some accounts say the man was old, enslaved and walked with difficulty. Others say he was a ragged stable boy. No record names him (Jim Crow Museum, Ferris State University).
- Rice took the act from Louisville to Cincinnati, Pittsburgh and Philadelphia, and reached New York in 1832. By 1838 white Americans were using "Jim Crow" as an insult for Black people. By the end of the 1800s the words named the laws and customs that kept Black Americans separate from white Americans and took away their rights (Jim Crow Museum).
- In 1843 four white men in New York acted a whole evening in blackface as the Virginia Minstrels (`music` bank).
- William Henry Lane, a Black dancer, worked in minstrel companies in the same blackface makeup as the white men who were copying Black dancers (Wikipedia, "Master Juba"). His story is below.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Shakespeare on the rivers" -->
- In 1816 the actor Noah Ludlow and eleven others floated down the Allegheny, Ohio and Mississippi rivers on a 25-foot flatboat and stopped to perform wherever they could (e-WV, West Virginia Encyclopedia).
- In 1831 the English-born actor William Chapman built the Floating Theatre at Pittsburgh, a boat 14 feet wide and 100 feet long. His family acted plays such as Shakespeare's *The Taming of the Shrew* at each landing, with songs and dances added. At New Orleans they broke up the boat and went back to Pittsburgh by steamboat to build another. From 1836 Chapman used a small steamboat to tow the theatre back upriver (Britannica, "Showboat". e-WV).
- A night on the boat began with a play, then a speech and a set of short scenes and songs. Tickets cost fifty cents, and people who had no cash paid with fruit, potatoes or bacon (Yale Historical Review, citing Philip Graham). Minstrel shows and burlesques in the same years acted comic versions of Shakespeare with titles such as "Julius Sneezer" (Yale Historical Review, citing Teague).
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="May 10, 1849: the Astor Place riot" -->
- On May 10, 1849, militia officers in New York ordered soldiers to fire into a crowd outside the Astor Place Opera House. The crowd had come to stop the English actor William Charles Macready from playing *Macbeth*. Historians count between 22 and 31 dead.
- The quarrel began between two actors. Edwin Forrest, the American star, and Macready accused each other of plotting against their careers. Forrest stood up in a theatre box in Edinburgh in 1846 and hissed Macready (Wikipedia, "Edwin Forrest"). Forrest's fans were mostly working men. The Astor Place Opera House had a dress code of kid gloves and white vests (Wikipedia, "Astor Place Riot", citing Nigel Cliff).
- On May 7, Forrest's supporters bought tickets for the upper seats and stopped Macready's *Macbeth* by throwing rotten eggs, potatoes, shoes and pieces of ripped-up seats (Wikipedia, citing Cliff). Forty-seven New Yorkers, including the writers Herman Melville and Washington Irving, signed a letter asking Macready to play again.
- Isaiah Rynders, a Tammany Hall political boss, and the writer Ned Buntline put up posters that read "WORKINGMEN! SHALL AMERICANS OR ENGLISH RULE IN THIS CITY?" and handed out free tickets (Headley, 1873. Wikipedia).
- On May 10 a crowd of about 10,000 gathered outside and threw paving stones at the building and at the soldiers. Mayor Caleb Woodhull had called out the militia. General Charles Sandford brought about 350 soldiers of the Seventh Regiment (Wikipedia. *Smithsonian*, 2017).
- A city judge, Recorder Frederick Talmadge, told General William Hall, "You must order your men to fire." The sheriff then said, "you have permission to fire," and Sandford gave the order (Headley, *Pen and Pencil Sketches of the Great Riots*, 1873). The soldiers fired over the crowd's heads, then lowered their guns and fired into it (*Smithsonian*).
- *Smithsonian* counts 18 dead that night and five more in the next days, 23 in all. Other historians count between 22 and 31. *Smithsonian* writes that many of those shot were bystanders. Seven were Irish immigrants. The dead included carpenters, clerks, printers and laborers, men and women (*Smithsonian*, citing the *New York Herald*. Wikipedia, citing Cliff). About 48 people in the crowd, 50 to 70 police and 141 soldiers were hurt. The next night, in more fighting near Astor Place, a young boy was killed (Wikipedia).
- A jury found that the circumstances "justified the authorities in giving the order to fire." Buntline and nine others were convicted of starting the riot, fined and jailed (*Smithsonian*).
- It was the largest loss of civilian life in New York since the Revolution (*Smithsonian*).
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-story:start slug="william-alexander-brown" name="William Alexander Brown" movie="" kind="famous" status="verified" -->
### William Alexander Brown
> **Who:** A Black ship's steward from the West Indies who started the first known Black theatre company in the United States, and wrote the first known play by a Black writer to be acted there · **When and where:** New York City, 1816 to 1823
Sources give his name as William Alexander Brown and as William Henry Brown (BlackPast. Wikipedia). From 1816 he ran a tea garden behind his house on Thomas Street, with music and recitations on Sunday afternoons. He used the garden to find performers. In September 1821 he opened a theatre of about 300 seats with *Richard III*, acted by a Black cast for Black audiences. White New Yorkers soon came too (BlackPast. *New York Times*, 2021). Neighbours complained, so he moved the company again and again. White men broke in and beat his actors, and police arrested his actors during a performance and released them only when they promised to stop acting Shakespeare (Wikipedia. MacDonald, Folger 2024). In 1823 he staged his own play, *The Drama of King Shotaway*, about a Black uprising on St. Vincent. The last known playbill of his company dates from June 1823. What happened to him afterwards is not known (*New York Times*, 2021).
<!-- hb-story:end slug="william-alexander-brown" -->
<!-- hb-story:start slug="ira-aldridge" name="Ira Aldridge" movie="" kind="famous" status="verified" -->
### Ira Aldridge
> **Who:** A Black actor from New York who left the United States in 1824 and spent more than forty years as a leading Shakespearean actor in Britain and Europe · **When and where:** born in New York about July 24, 1807, to England in 1824, died on tour in Łódź, Poland, on August 7, 1867
Aldridge went to the African Free School in New York and acted as a teenager with Brown's African Company (Wikipedia. Britannica). In 1824, facing discrimination against Black actors in America, he sailed to England (Wikipedia). In October 1825 he played the enslaved prince Oroonoko in a London theatre. *The Times* of London mocked the shape of his lips and wrote that his skin was not dark enough for Othello (Wikipedia, quoting *The Times*, October 11, 1825). He played Othello, Lear and Macbeth, and he played white characters such as Shylock and Richard III in white greasepaint and a wig. On closing nights he spoke to his audiences against slavery (Wikipedia). In 1828 he became manager of the theatre in Coventry, England (Wikipedia). Rulers in Prussia, Austria and Russia honoured him, and he became a British citizen in 1863 (Britannica. Wikipedia). He had agreed to act in New York in September 1867, but he died in Poland that August. He never acted on an American stage again (Britannica. *New York Times* obituary, 1867).
<!-- hb-story:end slug="ira-aldridge" -->
<!-- hb-story:start slug="william-henry-lane" name="William Henry Lane (Master Juba)" movie="" kind="famous" status="verified" -->
### William Henry Lane (Master Juba)
> **Who:** A young Black dancer, known on stage as Master Juba, who had to dance in blackface to work, and the only Black performer of his time to tour with a white minstrel company · **When and where:** New York and London, 1840s, born about 1825, died about 1854
Lane grew up in the Five Points, a poor New York neighbourhood where Irish immigrants and free Black people lived side by side, and by the early 1840s he was dancing in the street for food and coins (Wikipedia, "Master Juba"). A letter printed in the *Sunday Flash* newspaper in 1841 or 1842 said that the showman P. T. Barnum blacked the boy's face with burnt cork, billed him as the white dancer John Diamond, and staged fake dance contests with him (Wikipedia, quoting the letter). The historian Eric Lott described the act as "a black man imitating a white man imitating a black man." Lane later beat Diamond in public dance contests. In 1848 he went to London with the Ethiopian Serenaders, a white minstrel company. *The Times* of London reported that the crowd made him repeat his dance three times (Wikipedia, quoting *The Times*, August 22, 1848). He died about 1854, at about 29. Historians think overwork and poor food killed him, but no record states the cause (Wikipedia).
<!-- hb-story:end slug="william-henry-lane" -->
<!-- hb-story:start slug="edwin-forrest" name="Edwin Forrest" movie="" kind="famous" status="verified" -->
### Edwin Forrest
> **Who:** An American-born star actor, called "the first real American theatrical star" by some historians, whose feud with an English actor ended in the Astor Place riot · **When and where:** born in Philadelphia on March 9, 1806, acted from about 1817 to 1872, died December 12, 1872
Forrest first acted at eleven, in a woman's part, at a Philadelphia theatre (Wikipedia). He became a star as Othello at New York's Bowery Theatre in 1826. From 1828 he paid prizes for new American plays with a lead part written for him. The first winner was *Metamora*, by John Augustus Stone. Forrest, a white actor, played its hero, the Wampanoag leader Metacom, from December 15, 1829. The theatres made record profits, and Stone got very little of them (Wikipedia, "Metamora"). An English visitor called Forrest "a coarse and vulgar actor" but wrote that his audiences "were enraptured" (Thomas Hamilton, quoted in Wikipedia). In 1846 he hissed the English actor William Charles Macready from a theatre box in Edinburgh. Forrest's fans in New York rioted against Macready in May 1849 (Wikipedia). In 1850 Forrest beat the writer Nathaniel Parker Willis with a whip in Washington Square. A court granted Forrest's wife, Catherine, a divorce in 1852 and ordered him to pay her money every year (Wikipedia). He kept acting almost until he died.

## 1850 to 1900
<!-- hb-zoom level="era" -->
From 1852 white actors in blackface toured the country in stage versions of *Uncle Tom's Cabin*, a novel written against slavery. In 1865 a working actor, John Wilkes Booth, shot President Abraham Lincoln in a theatre where he often performed. From 1881 managers in New York and Boston built vaudeville, programmes of short acts played to family audiences, some of them all day long. Buffalo Bill's Wild West opened in 1883, and by 1890 it paid Lakota men $25 a month to act out attacks on settlers that Cody and his cowboys beat off. Black minstrels such as Billy Kersands performed in the same burnt-cork makeup white minstrels wore. In 1894 the first paying customers watched films in New York, and most of those films showed stage and vaudeville performers doing their acts.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="The Tom shows" -->
- Harriet Beecher Stowe published *Uncle Tom's Cabin*, a novel against slavery, in March 1852. `art` tells the novel. Stowe refused to let it be made into a play and never earned a cent from any stage version (Moody, *American Heritage*, 1955).
- Theatre managers staged it anyway. The version that lasted opened in Troy, New York, on September 27, 1852. The manager, G. C. Howard, paid his 22-year-old cousin George L. Aiken $40 and a gold watch to write it in a week (Moody). Its New York run from July 1853 lasted about 300 performances.
- White actors in blackface played the enslaved characters. Green Germon, cast as Uncle Tom in Troy, at first refused to black up because he said audiences would expect minstrel dances and "Jim Crow" songs from him. Mrs. Howard played Topsy and danced a minstrel "breakdown" (Moody). In January 1854 Thomas Dartmouth Rice, the white actor who had created "Jim Crow," played Tom at New York's Bowery Theatre (Wikipedia, "Tom show").
- P. T. Barnum's museum put on its own version and advertised that it "does not foolishly and unjustly elevate the Negro above the white man in intellect or morals" (Moody).
- In the 1890s between 400 and 500 touring companies played the story. In 1893 an agent in Chicago offered to hire out Uncle Toms at $40 to $60 a week. A dozen companies were still touring in 1927 (Moody). By 1902, one report counted a quarter of a million performances (Wikipedia, citing John W. Frick).
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="April 14, 1865: an actor at Ford's Theatre" -->
- John Wilkes Booth came from a family of actors and often performed at Ford's Theatre in Washington (Ford's Theatre. Britannica). On the morning of April 14, 1865, he learned that President Abraham Lincoln would come that night to see the comedy *Our American Cousin* (Ford's Theatre).
- He found the president's box almost unguarded and barred its outer door from inside. At a line he knew would bring a big laugh, he stepped in, shot Lincoln once in the back of the head with a small pistol called a derringer, and cut Major Henry Rathbone with a knife (Britannica). The play stopped at the line "you sockdologizing old man-trap" (LincolnConspirators).
- Booth jumped from the box to the stage and ran out a side door to a waiting horse. Audience members said he shouted "Sic semper tyrannis," Latin for "Thus always to tyrants," or "The South is avenged!", or both (Britannica). Lincoln died the next morning. `government-politics` and `war` tell the killing and what followed.
- The federal government bought the theatre in 1866. Secretary of War Edwin Stanton had it turned into offices, and in 1893 part of the inside collapsed and killed 22 workers. The next play in the building was in 1968 (Ford's Theatre).
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Vaudeville begins" -->
- In 1881 Tony Pastor, a singer and theatre manager, took over a theatre on 14th Street in New York. He sold no alcohol and kept the acts clean so that women and families would come (Wikipedia, "Tony Pastor"). Britannica credits Pastor with the first show of the kind later called vaudeville.
- In 1883 Benjamin Franklin Keith opened a museum of oddities in a Boston shop. Its first exhibit was a very small three-month-old baby shown as "Baby Alice." He added a 123-seat theatre and ran a "continuous" show from 10 in the morning to 11 at night, so customers could walk in at any time (Wikipedia, "B. F. Keith").
- From 1885 Keith and Edward F. Albee ran the Bijou Theatre in Boston together and built a chain of vaudeville theatres (Wikipedia, "E. F. Albee").
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="The Wild West: Lakota performers" -->
- William F. Cody, called Buffalo Bill, started Buffalo Bill's Wild West in 1883 near North Platte, Nebraska. In the arena, Native performers attacked stagecoaches, wagon trains and a settler's cabin, and Cody and his cowboys drove them off (Wikipedia, "Buffalo Bill").
- The Hunkpapa Lakota leader Sitting Bull signed a contract on June 6, 1885, at the Standing Rock Agency, to tour with the show for four months at $50 a week. The contract also let him sell his own photographs and autographs. The federal Indian agent James McLaughlin signed as a witness (Fine Books Magazine, 2026). `native-nations` tells his life.
- Cody paid most Lakota men $25 a month. "Chiefs" got $30 to $60, and women got $10 (Cody's letter of 1891, in Maddra, 2002). The show needed permits from federal officials to hire Lakota people from their reservations (Maddra).
- Several performers died on tour in Europe. Chief Hawick died of typhoid fever on January 1, 1890, and Featherman of smallpox, both in hospital at Marseilles. Wounds One Another died after he fell from a train in Germany (Maddra). Surrounded by the Enemy, an Oglala Lakota man, died of a lung infection in London in 1887 and was buried there (Wikipedia, "Buffalo Bill").
- In 1890, after reports of deaths and mistreatment, the Secretary of the Interior, John Noble, ordered an investigation and stopped new permits for Native performers (Maddra). The Lakota performer No Neck said the men were paid $25 a month and would not go again for less than $50. He also told reporters that Cody gave him "a suit of clothes, paid my wages, and gave me $100 besides," and told him to give "a good report of the treatment of the Indians" (*New York Herald* and *New York Journal*, July 30, 1890, in Maddra).
- On December 29, 1890, soldiers of the 7th Cavalry killed between 250 and 300 Lakota people at Wounded Knee, South Dakota, most of them women and children (`native-nations` bank). `native-nations` tells Wounded Knee. After the killings the army held Lakota Ghost Dancers as prisoners at Fort Sheridan, Illinois. In March 1891 federal officials released 23 of them to Cody, on the condition that they tour Europe with his show. They included Short Bull and Kicking Bear (Maddra). Short Bull had said of Wounded Knee, "twenty-three of my own relations were killed in this fight, men, women and children" (Short Bull, as told to George Crager, in Maddra). In the arena the prisoners played "hostiles," the Indians who attacked.
- The historian Sam Maddra concludes that the Lakota who joined the show got wages, travel and a measure of freedom from government control, and earned more than farming on the reservation paid.
- In the same years officials and missionaries worked to stop Native ceremonies and dances. In 1883 Hiram Price, the Commissioner of Indian Affairs, issued rules that made the Sun Dance and other dances "Indian offenses," punished by cutting off a family's food rations (`religion` and `music` tell this). In 1885 Moravian missionaries arrived at Quinhagak, Alaska, and called Yup'ik masked dances "heathen rites." No traditional dancing took place in Quinhagak for more than a century, until 2013 (*Archaeology*, 2015).
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="1893 to 1896: the first films show stage acts" -->
- Thomas Edison's staff began building a film studio at West Orange, New Jersey, in December 1892. They called it the Black Maria. It was a shed lined with tar paper, with a roof that opened for sunlight, and the whole building turned on a track to follow the sun (Wikipedia, "Kinetoscope"). `technology` tells the machines.
- On April 14, 1894, the Holland brothers opened a Kinetoscope parlour at 1155 Broadway in New York. Each machine showed one short film to one person looking into it. The ten films, each 15 to 20 seconds long, were shot in the Black Maria. They showed the strongman Eugen Sandow, a contortionist, wrestlers, trapeze artists, a Highland dancer, blacksmiths and a barber shop (Wikipedia, "Kinetoscope").
- On April 23, 1896, films were projected on a screen for a seated audience at Koster and Bial's Music Hall, a New York vaudeville theatre (Wikipedia, "Vitascope").
- The actors May Irwin and John Rice kissed at the end of the 1895 Broadway musical *The Widow Jones*. In April 1896 they acted the same kiss again for Edison's camera. The film, *The Kiss*, runs about 18 seconds (Wikipedia, "The Kiss").
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-story:start slug="edwin-booth" name="Edwin Booth" movie="Prince of Players (1955)" kind="famous" status="verified" -->
### Edwin Booth
> **Who:** The leading American Shakespearean actor of his time, and the older brother of John Wilkes Booth · **When and where:** born in Bel Air, Maryland, on November 13, 1833, acted mostly in New York and on tour, died in New York on June 7, 1893 · **Movie:** *Prince of Players* (1955), a feature film about him
Booth was the son of the actor Junius Brutus Booth and supported the Union, while his brother John Wilkes supported the Confederacy (Wikipedia). Late in 1863 or early in 1864, on a crowded railway platform in Jersey City, a young man slipped into the gap between the platform and a moving train. Booth grabbed his coat collar and pulled him out. The young man was Robert Lincoln, the president's son, who described the rescue in a letter in 1909 (Wikipedia, quoting Robert Lincoln's letter). In 1864 and 1865 Booth played Hamlet about a hundred nights in a row in New York, a record that lasted until 1922. After his brother killed President Lincoln, Edwin Booth stayed off the stage for about nine months. He came back as Hamlet in January 1866 (Wikipedia). His own theatre in New York opened in 1869 and went bankrupt in 1874. In 1879 a salesman named Mark Gray fired two shots at him on a Chicago stage and missed (Wikipedia). In 1888 he founded The Players, a club for actors and artists, and he died there.

### Charlotte Cushman
> **Who:** A Boston-born actress whom Britannica calls "the first native-born star on the American stage," and who played men's parts, including Romeo and Hamlet, as well as women's · **When and where:** born in Boston on July 23, 1816, acted in the United States and Britain from 1835 to 1875, lived in Rome from 1852, died in Boston on February 18, 1876
Cushman trained to sing opera, but her voice failed after she sang parts that were too high for it. She turned to acting and played Lady Macbeth in 1836 (Britannica). In London in 1845, and again in 1854 and 1855, she played Romeo while her sister Susan played Juliet (Britannica). From 1848 she lived with the writer Matilda Hays, and people in Europe knew them as a couple. In 1852 Cushman settled in Rome among American women artists, and she later lived with the sculptor Emma Stebbins (Wikipedia). She promoted the work of the Black and Native American sculptor Edmonia Lewis (Wikipedia). During the Civil War she acted to raise money for the care of Union soldiers. In 1869 doctors began treating her for breast cancer. She kept acting and reading on stage, and she announced her farewell at least seven times before her last performance in Boston on May 15, 1875 (Wikipedia).
<!-- hb-story:end slug="charlotte-cushman" -->
<!-- hb-story:start slug="billy-kersands" name="Billy Kersands" movie="" kind="famous" status="verified" -->
### Billy Kersands
> **Who:** A Black minstrel comedian and dancer, the most popular Black comedian of his day, who performed in burnt-cork blackface · **When and where:** born about 1842 in Baton Rouge, Louisiana, toured the United States and Britain from the 1860s, died on June 30, 1915, in Artesia, New Mexico
Kersands began touring with minstrel companies in the early 1860s (Wikipedia). On stage he played the slow, foolish Black man that white minstrels had invented, and he filled his wide mouth with billiard balls or a cup and saucer to get laughs. He sang "Old Aunt Jemima," a song whose name was later used for a pancake brand (Wikipedia, citing Robert Toll). In 1872 a tavern owner, Charles Callender, bought the Black company Kersands worked for. When Kersands and other stars asked for more pay, Callender fired them, and most later came back to him (Wikipedia). Kersands earned about $15 a week in 1879, about $80 by 1882, and up to $250 a week on tours of Europe. White and Black audiences in the South came to see him, and some theatre owners relaxed the rules that kept Black customers in separate seats (Wikipedia). When people asked why he never moved to vaudeville, he answered, "All of my money came from the people of the South, the white and the colored, while playing down there" (Tom Fletcher, 1954, quoted by Mel Watkins). He died shortly after a show, at about 73.

## 1900 to 1950
<!-- hb-zoom level="era" -->
Between 1900 and 1950 most Americans moved from watching acted stories on a live stage to watching them on film and hearing them on the radio. From 1905 storefront theatres charged five cents to see short films, and by 1908 the country had about 8,000 of them. Film makers opened the first studio in Hollywood in 1911. By the 1930s eight companies made most American films, and five of them also owned chains of theatres. Films began to talk in 1927. On the radio, two white men used made-up voices to play Black characters in one of the most popular shows of the time. White actors in blackface or other makeup still played Black, Asian and Native characters. Studio rules and state laws limited the parts that actors of color could get.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Story films, nickelodeons and actors with no names" -->
- Edwin S. Porter made *The Great Train Robbery* for Thomas Edison's company in November 1903. It runs about 12 minutes. Porter cut between places to show things happening at the same time. Later legends called it the first film with a story, and film historians say that is wrong (Wikipedia).
- Storefront theatres that charged a nickel, called nickelodeons, spread after June 19, 1905, when Harry Davis and John P. Harris opened one in Pittsburgh. By 1908 there were about 8,000 of them, and most seated fewer than 200 people (Wikipedia).
- The Nestor company opened the first permanent film studio in Hollywood on October 27, 1911, at Sunset Boulevard and Gower Street (Wikipedia). Hollywood lies in the Los Angeles Basin, the homeland of the Tongva people. In 1994 California officials recognized the Tongva, whom the Spanish called Gabrielino, as the original tribe of the Los Angeles Basin. No Tongva group has federal recognition as a tribe (Wikipedia, "Tongva").
- The early studios did not publish actors' names. The owners of the Biograph company refused to name Florence Lawrence, so fans called her "the Biograph Girl." Her biographers say the owners feared that famous actors would ask for more pay. In 1910 the producer Carl Laemmle hired her, spread a false story that she had been killed, and then named her in newspaper ads headed "We nail a lie" (Wikipedia). She is often called the first film star, but evidence published in 2019 shows that the French actor Max Linder was named first.
- In 1915 the nine Supreme Court justices ruled that showing films was "a business, pure and simple," with no free-speech protection. State and city censor boards could cut or ban any film (*Mutual Film Corp. v. Industrial Commission of Ohio*, Wikipedia). The justices reversed that ruling in 1952.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="1915: The Birth of a Nation" -->
- D. W. Griffith's *The Birth of a Nation* opened in Los Angeles on February 8, 1915. It runs about three hours and was the longest American film yet made. Griffith based it on Thomas Dixon's novel *The Clansman* (Britannica, Wikipedia).
- White actors in blackface played most of the Black characters. In one scene Gus, a freed man played by a white actor, chases Flora Cameron, a white girl, until she jumps from a cliff to her death. Klansmen in the film catch Gus, hold their own trial and lynch him, which means a mob kills him with no lawful trial. At the end, armed Klansmen stop Black men from voting. The film shows the Ku Klux Klan as heroes (Wikipedia, Britannica).
- On February 18, 1915, President Woodrow Wilson watched it in the East Room of the White House with his family and members of his Cabinet. Dixon had been a graduate student with Wilson. Wilson's secretary later wrote that Wilson had not known what the film contained. In 1937, 22 years later, a magazine printed words it said Wilson spoke about the film: "It is like writing history with lightning. And my only regret is that it is all so terribly true." No record from 1915 contains those words, and historians dispute them (Wikipedia).
- Dixon wrote to Wilson's secretary in May 1915 that "the real purpose of my film was to revolutionize Northern sentiments" (Wikipedia).
- NAACP members protested at showings from the first weeks. In Boston in April 1915, managers of the Tremont Theatre refused to sell tickets to William Monroe Trotter, editor of the *Guardian*, and a group of Black Bostonians. Police arrested Trotter and ten others. Thousands of people rallied against the film in Boston that spring (Wikipedia, Britannica).
- Censors in Ohio and in Chicago, Denver, Pittsburgh, St. Louis and Minneapolis refused to allow it, and the mayor of Cedar Rapids, Iowa, banned it after meeting a group of Black citizens. Most cities and states showed it (Wikipedia, Britannica).
- No one knows how much money it took in. The company reported $4.8 million in receipts by the end of 1917. The historian Richard Schickel estimates more than $60 million in ticket sales. No American film earned more until *Gone with the Wind* in 1939 (Wikipedia).
- After the film opened in Atlanta, William J. Simmons, a preacher, started the Klan again with a cross burning on Stone Mountain, Georgia. Britannica dates this to December 1915 and Wikipedia to November 1915. Britannica says Simmons was "directly inspired" by the film. Klan leaders later showed it to recruit members, including David Duke in the 1970s (Wikipedia). `slavery-freedom` and `rights-movements` tell what happened during Reconstruction and what the Klan did.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Sound, and the Production Code" -->
- *The Jazz Singer* opened in New York on October 6, 1927. It was the first feature-length film with recorded songs and speech matched to the actors' lips, in a few scenes. Its star, Al Jolson, sings in blackface (Wikipedia).
- In 1930 the heads of the big studios agreed to a set of rules called the Production Code. Martin Quigley, a Catholic publisher, and Daniel Lord, a Catholic priest, wrote it. From July 1, 1934, no film from those studios could open without a seal of approval from Joseph Breen's office. No government wrote or enforced the Code. The studio heads used it to keep government censors away (Wikipedia).
- One rule read: "Miscegenation (sex relationships between the white and black races) is forbidden." *Miscegenation* is an old word for marriage or romance between people of different races. The rule named only white and Black people (text of the 1930 Code).
- The studio heads rewrote that rule in 1956. They replaced the whole Code with movie ratings on November 1, 1968 (Wikipedia).
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="The studios and their contracts" -->
- By the 1930s eight companies made most American films. Five of them, MGM, Paramount, Warner Bros., 20th Century-Fox and RKO, also owned chains of theatres. Columbia, Universal and United Artists owned few theatres or none. By 1939 the country had 15,000 movie theatres (Wikipedia, "Studio system"). `big-business` tells the business.
- Actors signed contracts for up to seven years. Studio lawyers stopped the clock on any day the actor was not working, so a contract could last much longer than seven years. Olivia de Havilland sued Warner Bros. in 1943. On December 8, 1944, California judges ruled that seven years meant seven years on the calendar (Wikipedia).
- On May 4, 1948, the Supreme Court justices ruled against the five studios that owned theatres, in the Paramount case. By 1954 the studios had sold their theatres (Wikipedia).
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Plays on the radio" -->
- Two white men, Freeman Gosden and Charles Correll, started *Amos 'n' Andy* on a Chicago station on March 19, 1928. They voiced Amos, Andy and more than 170 other characters as Black men, in the dialect of minstrel shows. One storyline in 1930 and 1931 drew about 40 million listeners (Wikipedia).
- In 1931 the *Pittsburgh Courier*, a Black newspaper, collected petitions from more than 700,000 Black Americans asking the Federal Radio Commission to take the show off the air. The *Chicago Defender*, another Black newspaper, praised the show the same year. In 1930 Gosden and Correll played the parts in blackface in a film (Wikipedia).
- On October 30, 1938, Orson Welles, 23, and his Mercury Theatre on the Air performed H. G. Wells's novel *The War of the Worlds* on CBS as a string of fake news bulletins about Martians landing in New Jersey. The notice that it was a play came almost 40 minutes in (*Smithsonian*, 2015, in the `news-communication` bank).
- The next morning, newspapers across the country reported a national panic. That night a ratings company had telephoned 5,000 homes, and 2 percent said they were listening to the Welles program. Some listeners were frightened and phoned police stations and newspapers. Researchers checked six New York City hospitals six weeks later and found no cases caused by the broadcast. No death has ever been traced to it (Pooley and Socolow, *Slate*, 2013). `news-communication` tells radio as a way of spreading news.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="The first television plays" -->
- On September 11, 1928, the station WGY in Schenectady, New York, broadcast a short spy play, *The Queen's Messenger*, with two actors, Izetta Jewell and Maurice Randall. It was the first television drama. Viewers watched on screens three inches wide (Wikipedia).
- NBC broadcast the comedy show *Texaco Star Theater* from June 8, 1948. The comedian Milton Berle became its host that fall, and people called him "Mr. Television" (Wikipedia). `technology` tells the machines.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="The Federal Theatre Project, 1935 to 1939" -->
- On August 27, 1935, federal officials set up the Federal Theatre Project to pay theatre workers who had lost their jobs in the Depression. Hallie Flanagan, a theatre professor at Vassar College, ran it. Within a year it employed 15,000 people at $23.86 a week (Wikipedia). `economy` and `work-workers` tell the relief programs.
- About 30 million people saw its roughly 1,200 productions, and most shows were free (Wikipedia).
- The project set up 17 Negro Theatre Units, companies of Black actors, in cities from Seattle to Birmingham. On April 14, 1936, the New York unit opened *Macbeth* at the Lafayette Theatre in Harlem, with an all-Black cast of about 150, set on an island like 19th-century Haiti. Orson Welles, then 20, directed it. A free show on April 20 drew 3,000 more people than the theatre could seat (Wikipedia).
- In 1938 a House committee led by Martin Dies Jr. held hearings that accused the project of spreading communism. The committee said that "racial equality forms a vital part of the Communist dictatorship." Members of Congress cut off its money on June 30, 1939, and 8,000 people lost their jobs (Wikipedia). `government-politics` tells the committee.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Native performers in early film" -->
- Native actors worked in films from the start, often as advisers as well as actors. Lilian St. Cyr, whose story follows, played the lead woman in the first Western made in Hollywood (National Women's History Museum).
- In the fall of 1913 Buffalo Bill Cody filmed a re-enactment of the Wounded Knee killings on the Pine Ridge Reservation in South Dakota. Interior Secretary Franklin K. Lane allowed more than 1,000 Lakota people to take part, and soldiers of the 12th Cavalry played the troops (*Cowboys & Indians*, 2023). Short Bull, one of the Ghost Dancers Cody had hired in 1891 and a man who had lost 23 relatives at Wounded Knee, acted in it (Maddra, 2002).
- Only a few minutes of the film, *The Indian Wars*, survive. Ben Black Elk later said, "The government put a ban on it" (*Cowboys & Indians*, 2023, *Nebraska History*, 1990).
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-story:start slug="charlie-chaplin" name="Charlie Chaplin" movie="Chaplin (1992)" kind="famous" status="verified" -->
### Charlie Chaplin
> **Who:** A London music-hall comedian who became the first worldwide film star by acting without speaking · **When and where:** born in London on April 16, 1889, made films in Los Angeles from 1914 to 1952, died in Switzerland on December 25, 1977 · **Movie:** *Chaplin* (1992), a feature film about him
Chaplin grew up poor in London and was sent to a workhouse twice before he was nine. He came to the United States with a touring comedy company and signed with the Keystone studio in September 1913 for $150 a week. His character the Tramp first appeared on screen on February 7, 1914. In 1916 the Mutual company paid him $670,000 for one year. On February 5, 1919, he formed United Artists with Mary Pickford, Douglas Fairbanks and D. W. Griffith, so that the four could pay for and control their own films. He kept making films with no spoken dialogue after sound arrived, including *City Lights* (1931) and *Modern Times* (1936). His first talking film, *The Great Dictator* (1940), made fun of Adolf Hitler. FBI agents under J. Edgar Hoover investigated him for years. On September 18, 1952, he sailed for London, and the next day Attorney General James McGranery cancelled his permit to come back. Chaplin settled in Switzerland. He returned once, in April 1972, to accept an honorary Academy Award, and the audience stood and applauded for 12 minutes (Wikipedia).
<!-- hb-story:end slug="charlie-chaplin" -->
<!-- hb-story:start slug="oscar-micheaux" name="Oscar Micheaux" movie="Midnight Ramble (1994)" kind="famous" status="verified" -->
### Oscar Micheaux
> **Who:** A Black homesteader and novelist who made more than 40 films for Black audiences outside the Hollywood studios · **When and where:** born in Metropolis, Illinois, on January 2, 1884, made films from 1919 to 1948, died in Charlotte, North Carolina, on March 25, 1951 · **Movie:** *Midnight Ramble: Oscar Micheaux and the Story of Race Movies* (1994), a PBS documentary about him and about films made for Black audiences
Micheaux's father was born into slavery in Kentucky. Micheaux worked as a Pullman porter, an attendant on railroad sleeping cars, and then farmed a homestead in Gregory County, South Dakota, until he lost it in 1911. He wrote novels about that life and sold them door to door (Britannica). In 1918 the Black-owned Lincoln Motion Picture Company offered to film his novel *The Homesteader*. Micheaux wanted a hand in making it, so he started his own company and sold stock in it at $75 to $100 a share. The film came out in 1919 (Wikipedia). His next film, *Within Our Gates* (1920), shows a white mob lynching a Black couple and burning their bodies. Chicago's censor board first rejected it in December 1919. Five months earlier, white gangs in Chicago had attacked Black residents in a riot in which 38 people died, 23 of them Black (Chicago Race Riot of 1919 Commemoration Project, in the `rights-movements` bank). Officials in Omaha and New Orleans blocked it or demanded cuts (Wikipedia). Between 1919 and 1948 he made more than 40 films for Black audiences, shown in about 700 theatres. He paid for each new film by touring with the last one and raising money from investors along the way (Britannica). The only known copy of *Within Our Gates* was found in Spain in 1979 (Wikipedia).
<!-- hb-story:end slug="oscar-micheaux" -->
<!-- hb-story:start slug="anna-may-wong" name="Anna May Wong" movie="" kind="famous" status="verified" -->
### Anna May Wong
> **Who:** The first Chinese American film star, who spoke out against the parts Hollywood gave her · **When and where:** born Wong Liu Tsong in Los Angeles on January 3, 1905, acted in the United States and Europe from 1919, died in Santa Monica, California, on February 3, 1961
Wong's parents were Chinese Americans. She got her first film job as an extra in 1919 and starred in *The Toll of the Sea* (1922), one of the first films made in color. Because of state laws against marriage between races, she could not kiss a white actor on screen, even one playing an Asian man. Studios cast her as villains and as the "Dragon Lady" (Wikipedia). In 1933 she asked a magazine writer, "Why is it that the screen Chinese is always the villain?" In 1935 MGM's producers refused to consider her for O-Lan, the Chinese heroine of *The Good Earth* (1937). They gave the part to Luise Rainer, a white actress made up to look Chinese, and Rainer won the Academy Award for Best Actress. MGM tested Wong for Lotus, a scheming singer. She turned it down and told the producer Irving Thalberg that he was asking her, "with Chinese blood," to play "the only unsympathetic role" in a film where white actors played the Chinese characters (Wikipedia). In 1951 she starred in *The Gallery of Madame Liu-Tsong*, the first American television series with an Asian American star. In 2022 her picture went on the quarter, the first Asian American on US coins (Wikipedia).
<!-- hb-story:end slug="anna-may-wong" -->
<!-- hb-story:start slug="hattie-mcdaniel" name="Hattie McDaniel" movie="" kind="famous" status="verified" -->
### Hattie McDaniel
> **Who:** The first Black person to win an Academy Award · **When and where:** born in Wichita, Kansas, on June 10, 1893, acted in Los Angeles from the 1930s, won the award on February 29, 1940, died on October 26, 1952
McDaniel was the youngest of 13 children of parents who had been enslaved (Wikipedia). In *Gone with the Wind* she played Mammy, an enslaved house servant, for $450 a week (*The Hollywood Reporter*, 2015). The film opened in Atlanta on December 15, 1939, in a theatre for white people only, and Georgia's segregation laws kept her out (Wikipedia). On February 29, 1940, she won the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress. The ceremony took place at the Cocoanut Grove in the Ambassador Hotel, which did not admit Black guests, and the producer, David O. Selznick, had to ask a special favor to get her in. She sat with her escort and her white agent at a small table away from the film's white stars. Accounts differ on where the table stood. *The Hollywood Reporter* places it against a far wall, a 2008 book at the back of the room, and a 2019 article in a separate back room used to store the trophies. In her speech she said she hoped to "always be a credit to my race" (*The Hollywood Reporter*). At least 74 of her roles were maids and other servants. NAACP leaders criticized her for taking those parts, and she often answered, "I'd rather play a maid than be a maid" (*The Hollywood Reporter*). In 1945 white neighbours in Los Angeles sued her and 30 other Black homeowners to force them out under a rule that only white people could live there, and Judge Thurmond Clarke threw the case out. She died of breast cancer in 1952. She had asked to be buried in Hollywood Cemetery, but it took only white burials, so she was buried at Rosedale Cemetery (Wikipedia).
<!-- hb-story:end slug="hattie-mcdaniel" -->
<!-- hb-story:start slug="bert-williams" name="Bert Williams" movie="" kind="famous" status="verified" -->
### Bert Williams
> **Who:** A Black comedian who wore blackface on stage and became the best-paid Black performer in vaudeville and the star of an otherwise all-white Broadway revue · **When and where:** born in Nassau, the Bahamas, on November 12, 1874, performed in the United States and Britain from 1895, died in New York City on March 4, 1922
Williams moved to the United States at ten. From 1895 he worked with the comedian George Walker. The two Black men wore burnt-cork blackface, as white minstrels did, and billed themselves as "Two Real Coons," a slur of the time, to set themselves apart from white performers (Wikipedia). In August 1900, during a riot in New York, a white mob pulled Walker off a streetcar and beat him. Their musical *In Dahomey*, with an all-Black cast, played Broadway in 1903 and Buckingham Palace in London that June (Britannica). On the vaudeville circuits, managers allowed only one Black act on each program, and Williams had to travel, eat and sleep apart from white performers. In 1910 Florenz Ziegfeld hired him for the *Follies*. Several white cast members demanded that Ziegfeld fire him, and Ziegfeld refused. Williams starred in the *Follies* until 1919 (Britannica, Wikipedia). In August 1919 the rest of the cast walked out on strike, and no one had told him. He said, "I don't belong to either side. Nobody wants me." He collapsed on stage in Detroit in February 1922 and died in New York at 47. More than 5,000 people filed past his coffin (Wikipedia).
<!-- hb-story:end slug="bert-williams" -->
<!-- hb-story:start slug="lilian-st-cyr" name="Lilian St. Cyr (Red Wing)" movie="" kind="famous" status="verified" -->
### Lilian St. Cyr (Red Wing)
> **Who:** A Ho-Chunk actress who played the lead woman in the first Western made in Hollywood · **When and where:** born on the Winnebago Reservation in Nebraska in February 1884, acted in films from 1908 to 1925, died in March 1974
St. Cyr's parents had both died by 1888. She spent ten years in Indian boarding schools and graduated from the Carlisle Indian School in Pennsylvania in 1902. With her husband, James Young Deer, she performed in Wild West shows and then, from 1908, in short Western films, using the name Red Wing (National Women's History Museum). On February 23, 1914, Cecil B. DeMille's *The Squaw Man* opened. It was the first Western made in what is now Hollywood, and its title uses an insulting word for a Native woman. St. Cyr played a Ute woman who marries an Englishman and kills herself (*Indian Country Today*, 2014). She made her own costumes. She was paid far less than the white male lead. When someone told her she should be proud to act with a star who earned $1,000 a week, she answered, "What about him? He's working with a one hundred percent American." She acted until 1925. Then she made costumes in New York and worked for a national day to honor Native Americans (National Women's History Museum). Sources give her birth date as February 14 or February 23, 1884, and her death as March 12 or March 13, 1974.

## 1950 to 2000
<!-- hb-zoom level="era" -->
After 1950 television became the place where most Americans watched actors. In the early 1950s actors performed plays live on television, with no second takes. In 1947 members of a House committee began questioning film workers about communism, and studio executives agreed not to hire people who would not answer. Hundreds of writers, directors and actors lost work, some for more than ten years. White actors still played Asian and Native characters in makeup, while actors of color such as Rita Moreno and Sidney Poitier won Academy Awards and still got a narrow set of parts. From 1975 studios opened big films in hundreds of theatres on the same day. Families could record programs at home from 1975, and in 1984 the Supreme Court justices ruled that recording a broadcast to watch later was legal. Video games began in 1958, and by the 1990s game makers were hiring actors.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="The blacklist" -->
- In September 1947 the House Un-American Activities Committee called 42 people from the film industry to testify about communism. Walt Disney, Jack Warner, Gary Cooper and Ronald Reagan answered the committee's questions, and Disney named former employees he said were Communists (Wikipedia).
- Ten writers and directors, later called the Hollywood Ten, refused to say whether they belonged to the Communist Party. Members of the House voted 346 to 17 to charge them with contempt of Congress on November 24, 1947. The next day about 50 studio executives met at the Waldorf-Astoria hotel in New York and promised not to employ the ten or any Communist "knowingly." This promise started the blacklist, a list of people no studio would hire (Wikipedia).
- The ten were convicted in 1948 and went to prison in 1950. Other people lost their jobs after witnesses named them or after they refused to answer, and the list grew to hundreds of names. Some writers kept working under false names. The Academy Award for the story of *The Brave One*, given in 1957, went to "Robert Rich," a false name for the blacklisted writer Dalton Trumbo. The real Robert Rich was the nephew of the film's producer. Academy officials reissued the award in Trumbo's name in 1975 (Wikipedia).
- Some people named others to keep working. The actor Sterling Hayden said in 1963, "I was a rat, a stoolie, and the names I named of those close friends were blacklisted and deprived of their livelihood." The blacklist began to break in 1960, when the director Otto Preminger and the actor Kirk Douglas gave Trumbo credit on screen for *Exodus* and *Spartacus* (Wikipedia).
- In 1950 State Department officials took away the passport of Paul Robeson, a Black singer and actor who had played Othello on Broadway in 1943. In 1956 he told the committee, "I am being tried for fighting for the rights of my people, who are still second-class citizens in these United States of America" (Britannica).
- On September 19, 1952, Attorney General James McGranery cancelled Charlie Chaplin's permit to return to the United States while Chaplin was at sea (Wikipedia). `government-politics` tells the committee, and `news-communication` tells the broadcast blacklist.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="The Actors Studio, and plays on live television" -->
- In 1947 the directors Elia Kazan, Cheryl Crawford and Robert Lewis opened the Actors Studio in New York. About 700 actors auditioned in the first year, and 50 were chosen, including Marlon Brando. The studio taught "method acting," which grew out of the Russian director Konstantin Stanislavsky's teaching. Lee Strasberg ran it from 1952 until his death in 1982 (Wikipedia).
- On December 3, 1947, Brando played Stanley Kowalski in Tennessee Williams's *A Streetcar Named Desire* on Broadway, directed by Kazan. Kazan directed the film version in 1951 (Wikipedia).
- From 1947 the networks broadcast weekly plays performed live, such as *Kraft Television Theatre* (from May 7, 1947) and *Studio One* (from November 7, 1948). The actors had no second takes. After 1957 networks could record shows on videotape, and by 1961 about 80 percent of evening programs were filmed ahead of time (Wikipedia).
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="I Love Lucy: a stage comedy on film" -->
- *I Love Lucy* ran on CBS from October 15, 1951, to May 6, 1957. Lucille Ball and Desi Arnaz performed each episode in front of an audience of about 300 people, in order, like a play, while three film cameras recorded it at once (Wikipedia).
- The three-camera method was not new. Jerry Fairbanks had used it before on other shows. The *Lucy* crew, led by the cinematographer Karl Freund, lit the set so all three cameras got the same picture (Wikipedia).
- The sponsor wanted the show performed live in New York. Ball and Arnaz wanted to stay in Los Angeles and film it. They took a $1,000-a-week pay cut to cover the cost of film, and in return they got 80 percent ownership of the films. Because they owned the films, their company could sell the episodes again as reruns (Wikipedia).
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Who was allowed to play whom" -->
- White actors went on playing Asian characters in makeup. In *Breakfast at Tiffany's* (1961), Mickey Rooney, a white actor, played a Japanese landlord, I. Y. Yunioshi. Critics later called the part racist (Wikipedia).
- Espera de Corti, the son of Italian immigrants in Louisiana, played Native Americans in more than 200 films under the name Iron Eyes Cody and said his parents were Cherokee and Cree. In 1971 he appeared in a famous television advertisement against litter, crying a tear. In 1996 a New Orleans newspaper reported, from his half-sister and his baptism record, that he was Italian American. He denied it (Wikipedia).
- On April 9, 1962, Rita Moreno became the first Hispanic woman to win an Academy Award. On April 13, 1964, Sidney Poitier became the first Black man to win the Academy Award for Best Actor (Wikipedia). Both of their stories follow.
- On March 27, 1973, Marlon Brando won Best Actor for *The Godfather* and stayed away from the ceremony. He sent Sacheen Littlefeather, who said she was Apache, to refuse the award. In about 60 seconds she said he was protesting "the treatment of American Indians today by the film industry" and the armed standoff then going on at Wounded Knee, South Dakota. Some of the audience booed and some applauded. In June 2022 the leaders of the Academy apologized to her in a letter (Wikipedia).
- Littlefeather died on October 2, 2022. After her death, her two sisters told the Navajo writer Jacqueline Keeler that the family was not Native American and that their father was of Spanish and Mexican descent (Wikipedia). `native-nations` and `rights-movements` tell the 1973 occupation of Wounded Knee.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Big openings and home video" -->
- Universal's executives opened *Jaws* on June 20, 1975, on 409 screens in the United States at once, with advertisements on national television. Columbia had done something similar a month earlier with *Breakout*. *Jaws* earned more than any film before it (Wikipedia).
- *Star Wars* opened in only a small number of theatres on May 25, 1977, because few theatre owners wanted it. It spread as crowds came, and it passed *Jaws* in earnings (Wikipedia).
- In 1968 the studio heads replaced the Production Code with movie ratings (Wikipedia).
- Sony's Betamax video recorder went on sale in the United States in November 1975. The first VHS recorder, made by RCA, followed on August 23, 1977 (Wikipedia).
- In 1976 Universal and Disney sued Sony to stop home taping. On January 17, 1984, the Supreme Court justices ruled 5 to 4 that recording a broadcast at home to watch later was legal (*Sony v. Universal*, Wikipedia). Families could now keep and rent copies of films and shows. `marketplace` tells the rental stores.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Games, and the first actors in them" -->
- In 1958 the physicist William Higinbotham built *Tennis for Two* for a public open house at Brookhaven National Laboratory in New York. Visitors lined up to play it on a small round screen (Wikipedia). In 1972 Atari sold *Pong*, the first video game that made money, to arcades (Wikipedia). `sports-play` tells games as play, and `technology` tells the machines.
- Early games had no actors. From 1983 some arcade games played scenes stored on laser discs. In the early 1990s, games on compact discs used filmed actors, often from low-budget films and television, in games such as *Night Trap* (1992) and *The 7th Guest* (1993). In 1994 the makers of *Wing Commander III*, published by Electronic Arts, cast Hollywood actors including Mark Hamill (Wikipedia).

### Lucille Ball
> **Who:** A film actress who became television's biggest star and later the first woman to run a major television studio · **When and where:** born in Jamestown, New York, on August 6, 1911, worked in Los Angeles from the 1930s, died on April 26, 1989 · **Movie:** *Being the Ricardos* (2021), a feature film about Ball and Arnaz
Ball worked in films through the 1930s as a contract player at RKO, where people called her "Queen of the B's" for her many low-budget films. In 1948 she starred on the radio comedy *My Favorite Husband* for CBS. When CBS executives asked her to move it to television, she insisted that her husband, the Cuban bandleader Desi Arnaz, play her husband. The executives did not think viewers would accept the pair, so Ball and Arnaz toured a stage act to prove them wrong (Wikipedia). *I Love Lucy* began on October 15, 1951. The episode in which Lucy has a baby, shown on January 19, 1953, drew about 44 million viewers (Wikipedia). In 1957 their company, Desilu, bought the RKO studios where Ball had once worked, for more than $6 million. Ball and Arnaz divorced in 1960. In November 1962 she bought out his share and became the first woman to run a major television studio. Her company developed *Star Trek* and *Mission: Impossible*, and she agreed in 1967 to sell it. The sale, for $17 million, closed in 1968 (Wikipedia).

### Lee Grant
> **Who:** An actress who was blacklisted for twelve years at the start of her film career and later won an Academy Award for acting and one for a documentary she directed · **When and where:** born Lyova Haskell Rosenthal in New York City on October 31 in the mid-1920s (the year is disputed), acted in New York and Los Angeles from 1948
Grant played a shoplifter in the play *Detective Story* on Broadway in 1949 and in the 1951 film. The part earned her an Academy Award nomination and the Best Actress prize at the Cannes Film Festival (Wikipedia). In 1951 the actor J. Edward Bromberg died soon after the House committee questioned him. At his memorial service Grant gave a speech that suggested the committee's pressure had caused his death. The director Edward Dmytryk had already named her future husband, the writer Arnold Manoff, as a Communist, and she married Manoff in 1952 (Wikipedia). She was blacklisted. Sources give two reasons: the speech for Bromberg, and her refusal to testify about her husband, as the actor Kirk Douglas remembered it. For twelve years her film and television work was limited. She taught acting and took small parts under other names. In her 2014 memoir she wrote of "my rage at those who had taken twelve working, acting years from my life." She came back in the television series *Peyton Place* and won an Emmy in 1966. She won the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress for *Shampoo* (1975). Her documentary *Down and Out in America* (1986) won the Academy Award for Documentary Feature (Wikipedia).

### Lorraine Hansberry
> **Who:** The first Black woman to have a play produced on Broadway · **When and where:** born in Chicago on May 19, 1930, *A Raisin in the Sun* opened in New York on March 11, 1959, died in New York City on January 12, 1965, at 34 · **Movie:** *Lorraine Hansberry: Sighted Eyes/Feeling Heart* (2017), a documentary about her
Hansberry's father, Carl Hansberry, sold real estate on Chicago's South Side. When she was about seven, the family bought a house in Woodlawn, a white neighborhood where owners had signed an agreement not to sell to Black people. Anna M. Lee, a homeowner there, sued to cancel the sale, and a violent mob harassed the family. The judges of the Illinois Supreme Court ruled against the Hansberrys, and they had to leave. In 1940, in *Hansberry v. Lee*, the US Supreme Court justices reversed that ruling on a legal point (Britannica, Wikipedia). Her play *A Raisin in the Sun* tells of a Black family in Chicago who plan to move into a white neighborhood, and of a white man from that neighborhood who offers to pay them to stay away. The producer, Philip Rose, took 18 months to raise money for a play with an almost all-Black cast. Lloyd Richards directed it, the first Black director of a Broadway play, and Sidney Poitier and Ruby Dee starred. It ran for 530 performances. Richards said it was the first Broadway play that large numbers of Black people came to see (Wikipedia). Hansberry died of pancreatic cancer in 1965 (Britannica). `art` tells her as a writer, and `rights-movements` tells the housing case.

### Sidney Poitier
> **Who:** The first Black man to win the Academy Award for Best Actor · **When and where:** born in Miami on February 20, 1927, raised in the Bahamas, acted in New York and Los Angeles from the 1940s, won the award on April 13, 1964, died on January 6, 2022 · **Movie:** *Sidney* (2022), a documentary about him
Poitier's parents were tomato farmers on Cat Island in the Bahamas. He was born two months early in Miami while they were there to sell their crop, which made him a US citizen. He grew up on Cat Island and in Nassau, went to Miami at 15, and moved to New York at 16, where he washed dishes (Wikipedia). He failed his first audition at the American Negro Theatre, a Black theatre company in Harlem, because he could not read the script well. For several months an older waiter sat with him each night and helped him read the newspaper. After a short time in the Army, he tried again and won a part (Wikipedia). In 1958 he became the first Black actor nominated for Best Actor, for *The Defiant Ones*. In 1959 he starred on Broadway in *A Raisin in the Sun*. He won Best Actor for *Lilies of the Field* on April 13, 1964. In 1967 his films drew more ticket buyers than any other star's. Critics complained that his characters were too perfect. He wanted more varied parts but felt he had to set an example, because he was then the only Black actor getting leading parts in Hollywood films (Wikipedia).

### Rita Moreno
> **Who:** A Puerto Rican actress, dancer and singer who became the first Hispanic woman to win an Academy Award · **When and where:** born Rosa Dolores Alverío Marcano in Humacao, Puerto Rico, on December 11, 1931, moved to New York in 1936, acted in Hollywood from 1950, won the award on April 9, 1962 · **Movie:** *Rita Moreno: Just a Girl Who Decided to Go for It* (2021), a documentary about her
Moreno's mother brought her from Puerto Rico to New York City in 1936 and left her younger brother behind. She was on Broadway at 13 and signed with MGM as a teenager (Wikipedia). Studios cast her again and again in the same kind of part. She later said, "I was always offered the stereotypical Latina roles. The Conchitas and Lolitas in westerns. I was always barefoot. It was humiliating, embarrassing stuff. But I did it because there was nothing else." She played Anita, a Puerto Rican woman in New York, in the film *West Side Story* (1961) and won the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress on April 9, 1962. She said the offers afterwards were "pretty much the same thing. A lot of gang stories," and she did not make another film for seven years (Wikipedia). She went on to win a Grammy in 1972, a Tony in 1975, and Emmys in 1977 and 1978 (Wikipedia).

## 2000 to Today
<!-- hb-zoom level="era" -->
Since 2000 actors have performed in suits covered in markers, and computer artists have turned those movements into creatures and game characters. Game makers now hire actors the way film studios do. Netflix alone had 325 million paying members by 2026, and in 2025 people in the United States and Canada bought about 780 million movie tickets, while ticket sales in dollars were still 22 percent below 2019. In 2023 film and television actors and writers went on strike together for the first time since 1960, and video game performers struck from July 2024 to July 2025. Both strikes were partly about who controls a digital copy of a performer's face, body and voice. In 2023 Michelle Yeoh and Ke Huy Quan won Academy Awards for acting, and in 2024 Lily Gladstone became the first Native American nominated for Best Actress. In the 100 most popular films of 2024, fewer than 1 in 100 speaking characters was Native American.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Acting in a marker suit" -->
- In the *Lord of the Rings* films (2001 to 2003), the English actor Andy Serkis played Gollum on set beside the other actors. Artists at Weta Digital used the recorded movements of his body and face, painted him out of each shot frame by frame, and put the computer-made Gollum in his place (Wikipedia). This method is called motion capture, or performance capture when it records the face as well.
- For *The Two Towers* (2002) the crew streamed Serkis's movements into the Gollum model as he performed, the first time a feature film used capture in real time (Wikipedia). Other actors followed: Tom Hanks in *The Polar Express* (2004), the actors playing the Na'vi in *Avatar* (2009), and Mark Ruffalo as the Hulk in *The Avengers* (2012) (Wikipedia).
- Producers and studios campaigned for Serkis to be nominated for acting Academy Awards, first for Gollum and in 2011 for the ape Caesar. He was never nominated. *War for the Planet of the Apes* (2017) was nominated for its visual effects instead (Wikipedia). Animators objected in 2014 when Serkis described their work as "painting digital makeup" onto actors' performances, because they build much of what appears on screen (/Film, 2014). His story follows.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Games as acted stories" -->
- Naughty Dog's game *The Last of Us* came out on June 14, 2013. Troy Baker played Joel and Ashley Johnson played Ellie. About 85 percent of the game's movement was recorded on a motion-capture stage, and some lines were recorded later in a sound studio (Wikipedia). The two actors changed the story: Johnson persuaded the director, Neil Druckmann, to make Ellie tougher and more independent (Wikipedia). By April 2018 buyers had bought 17 million copies (Wikipedia).
- HBO's television version began on January 15, 2023, with Pedro Pascal as Joel and Bella Ramsey as Ellie. Almost 40 million viewers watched the first episode within two months (Wikipedia). Ramsey was told not to play the game so as not to copy Johnson's performance, and Johnson played Ellie's mother in the series (Wikipedia).
- Game actors struck for the first time from October 2016 to September 2017, 340 days. They asked for residuals, payments that grow when a game sells well, and did not get them (Wikipedia). In 2016 the voice actor Jennifer Hale told NPR that game actors were paid $825 for a four-hour session, with nothing more however many copies sold (NPR, 2016). `sports-play` tells games as play, and `technology` tells the machines.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Streaming, and fewer people in movie theatres" -->
- Netflix's managers, who ran a business mailing DVDs to renters, started a service that streamed films over the internet on January 16, 2007, with 1,000 titles. On February 8, 2012, they released every episode of *Lilyhammer*, a Norwegian series they had bought, on the same day. On February 1, 2013, they released *House of Cards*, the first series Netflix had ordered for itself (Wikipedia). By 2026 it had 325 million paying members in more than 190 countries (Wikipedia).
- Researchers at the FX television network counted 182 scripted shows on American television in 2002 and a record of about 600 in 2022. The count fell to 516 in 2023, the year of the writers' and actors' strikes (FX counts, as reported in 2023 and 2024).
- In March 2020 the COVID-19 pandemic closed movie theatres. The weekend of March 13 to 15, 2020, was the lowest for North American ticket sales since 1998. Studio executives sent new films straight to home viewing, and on July 28, 2020, the managers of the AMC theatre chain agreed that Universal could sell a film for home viewing after only 17 days in AMC theatres (Wikipedia).
- In 2025 moviegoers in the United States and Canada spent $8.87 billion on tickets, 22 percent less than the $11.4 billion of 2019. They bought about 780 million tickets, 5 percent fewer than in 2024 (Comscore, reported by *Screen Daily*, January 2026). `big-business` tells the streaming companies, and `marketplace` tells the rental stores.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Broadway dark for eighteen months" -->
- On March 12, 2020, New York Governor Andrew Cuomo banned gatherings of 500 people or more. His order closed the Broadway theatres at 5 p.m. that day, a day earlier than other large events (Governor's press release, March 12, 2020). All 41 Broadway theatres stayed dark for 18 months (*Playbill*).
- The first play back on Broadway was *Pass Over*, by the Black playwright Antoinette Nwandu, which began performances at the August Wilson Theatre on August 4, 2021 (Wikipedia and theatre news). Nwandu started writing it in 2012, after the killing of the Black teenager Trayvon Martin (Wikipedia). The large musicals reopened in September 2021.
- In the season that ended on May 24, 2026, Broadway audiences bought 14,577,322 tickets and paid $1.91 billion, the most money in any season on record (Broadway League, May 27, 2026). `health` tells the pandemic.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Anyone can perform" -->
- Three former PayPal workers started YouTube in February 2005, and the first video went up on April 23, 2005. From May 2007 YouTube's managers shared the advertising money from some videos with the people who made them, usually 55 percent to the maker and 45 percent to YouTube. YouTube's managers set the rules for who is paid and have changed them several times. Since November 2020 they have been able to put advertisements on any video in the United States and keep all of that money when the maker is not in the program (Wikipedia).
- On August 2, 2018, the managers of the Chinese company ByteDance merged the lip-sync app Musical.ly into their app TikTok, and that October TikTok was the most downloaded app in the United States (Wikipedia). One person with a phone can now write, act, film, edit and publish a performance with no studio and no audition. `news-communication` tells the platforms and the 2024 to 2026 fight over TikTok.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Who is on screen, and who votes on the awards" -->
- On January 15, 2015, all 20 actors nominated for Academy Awards were white. That day April Reign started the Twitter hashtag #OscarsSoWhite. On January 14, 2016, all 20 were white again, and the directors Spike Lee and the actress Jada Pinkett Smith said they would stay away from the ceremony (Wikipedia).
- In 2015 the members of the Academy, the people who vote on the Oscars, were 92 percent white and 75 percent men. Its president, Cheryl Boone Isaacs, promised in 2016 to double the number of women and people of color by 2020 (Wikipedia). The Academy had about 6,000 members in 2016. After invitations in June 2026 it could have 11,319, and it will be 75 percent white and 64 percent men (AP, June 25, 2026).
- In the 100 most popular films of 2024, 13.5 percent of speaking characters were Asian, 3.1 percent were Hispanic or Latino, and fewer than 1 percent were American Indian or Alaska Native. Twenty-nine of the 100 films had no Asian speaking characters at all, and 98 had no Native American girls or women (USC Annenberg Inclusion Initiative, August 2025).
- *Reservation Dogs* ran on the Hulu streaming service from August 9, 2021, to September 27, 2023. Sterlin Harjo, a Native filmmaker from Oklahoma, created it with Taika Waititi. It is about four Native teenagers in a small town in the Muscogee Nation, and it was the first American series with all Native writers and directors and an almost all Native cast (Wikipedia).
- For *Killers of the Flower Moon* (October 20, 2023), about the murders of Osage people for their oil money in the 1920s, the director Martin Scorsese met Osage Principal Chief Geoffrey Standing Bear. The film crew held a meeting with about 200 Osage people, cast Osage actors in at least 40 parts, and hired Christopher Côté to teach the actors the Osage language (Wikipedia). Côté said at the premiere that the film told the story mostly through the white husband who helped kill his wife's family: "when somebody conspires to murder your entire family, that's not love." In 2024 he said, "I still stand by what I said" (HuffPost, 2024).
- Lily Gladstone, who grew up on the Blackfeet Reservation in Montana, played Mollie Kyle, the Osage wife. In January 2024 she became the first Native American to win a Golden Globe for acting and the first Native American nominated for the Academy Award for Best Actress (Wikipedia). `native-nations` and `crime-justice` tell the Osage murders.
- *Everything Everywhere All at Once*, about a Chinese immigrant woman who runs a laundromat, won seven Academy Awards on March 12, 2023. Michelle Yeoh won Best Actress and Ke Huy Quan won Best Supporting Actor (Wikipedia). His story follows.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="#MeToo in Hollywood: Harvey Weinstein" -->
- In October 2017 reporters at *The New York Times* and *The New Yorker* published accounts from more than a dozen women who said the film producer Harvey Weinstein had sexually harassed them, sexually assaulted them or raped them (forced them into sex). By October 31, 2017, more than 80 women had made such accusations. He was fired from his own company, and the leaders of the Academy expelled him. Weinstein said he had never had sex with anyone against her will (Wikipedia).
- In November 2017 the reporter Ronan Farrow showed that Weinstein had hired the private spy firm Black Cube. Its investigators used false names to follow reporters and actresses, especially the actress Rose McGowan, who had said he raped her (Wikipedia). `rights-movements` tells #MeToo as a movement.
- On February 24, 2020, a New York jury convicted Weinstein of a criminal sexual act and of rape, and on March 11, 2020, a judge sentenced him to 23 years in prison. A Los Angeles jury convicted him on December 19, 2022, of rape and other sexual assaults committed in 2013, and a judge sentenced him to 16 years (Wikipedia).
- On April 25, 2024, the judges of New York's highest court, the Court of Appeals, threw out the New York convictions because the trial judge had made what they called "egregious errors," and they ordered a new trial. Weinstein stayed in prison because of the California conviction (Wikipedia).
- At a new trial, on June 11, 2025, a jury convicted him of forcing oral sex on Miriam Haley, a former production assistant, in his Manhattan apartment in 2006. The jury found him not guilty on the charge involving the model Kaja Sokola. Two juries in a row could not agree on the charge that he raped the actress Jessica Mann. On June 25, 2026, Manhattan District Attorney Alvin Bragg announced that there would be no further trial on that charge (NBC News, 2026, Wikipedia).
- On September 23, 2026, Justice Curtis Farber sentenced Weinstein to 15 years in prison. He told him, "You took what you wanted by force, and you have never accepted responsibility." Haley told the court that the assault was "a life sentence for me" (NBC News, September 23, 2026). In June 2026 California judges had upheld his Los Angeles conviction but ordered a new sentence, which had not been set by late September 2026 (MyNewsLA, 2026).
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="The 2023 strikes, and scanning" -->
- Members of the Writers Guild of America, the union of 11,500 screenwriters, went on strike against the studios from May 2 to September 27, 2023, 148 days. Their new contract forbids the studios from using writers' work to train computer programs that write, and from using those programs to cut writers' pay (Wikipedia).
- Members of SAG-AFTRA, the actors' union of about 160,000 members, went on strike from July 14 to November 9, 2023, the longest actors' strike against the film and television studios. It was the first time actors and writers had struck at the same time since 1960 (Wikipedia).
- On July 13, 2023, the union's chief negotiator, Duncan Crabtree-Ireland, said the studios wanted to scan background actors (extras with no lines) for one day's pay and own the scans. The studios' negotiators said a scan would be used only in the film the actor was hired for (Wikipedia). NPR interviewed five background actors who had been scanned. Most had signed agreements not to talk about their work, and they said they were afraid of being called difficult and not hired again if they refused (NPR, August 2, 2023). No public count exists of how many were scanned.
- The contract that ended the strike, approved on December 5, 2023, requires clear consent before a studio makes or uses a digital replica, a computer copy of an actor's face, body or voice. A studio that wants to use a replica in another production must ask again and pay again. The consent lasts after the actor dies unless the contract says otherwise, and studios may not use replicas to avoid hiring background actors (Authors Guild summary of the contract).
- Writers approved a new four-year contract in April 2026. SAG-AFTRA's members approved theirs on June 4, 2026, with 91 percent voting yes. It permits producers to use a performer made entirely by computer only when it adds "significant additional value" over a live actor (*Los Angeles Magazine*, June 2026).
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="The video game strike, 2024 to 2025" -->
- About 2,600 game performers in voice and motion-capture work struck from July 26, 2024, to July 9, 2025, against companies including Activision, Electronic Arts, Epic Games, Take-Two and Warner Bros. Games. Union negotiators had bargained with the companies for about a year and a half after the old contract ran out in November 2022. The one issue left was consent and pay for AI copies of their voices and bodies (Wikipedia).
- On May 16, 2025, Epic Games staff added a Darth Vader to its game *Fortnite* that talked to players in a computer-made copy of the voice of James Earl Jones, who had died on September 9, 2024. Jones had signed a deal in 2022 allowing his recorded voice to be used this way, and his family worked with Epic and Disney on it (Disney, May 16, 2025, Wikipedia). On May 19, 2025, union officials filed a charge with the National Labor Relations Board against Llama Productions, a company owned by Epic Games, saying its staff had replaced actors' work with AI without notice (Wikipedia).
- The strike ended when 95 percent of voting members approved a new contract on July 9, 2025. It requires written, specific consent before a company makes a digital replica, pay for its use, and disclosure of how it is used. During a future strike, performers can stop companies from using their replicas to make new material (Wikipedia, legal summaries, 2025).
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Faces and voices nobody performed" -->
- In *Rogue One* (December 16, 2016), the actor Guy Henry played the part of Grand Moff Tarkin, and effects artists put a digital copy of the face of Peter Cushing, who had died in 1994, over Henry's face. Lucasfilm staff got permission from Cushing's estate (the people in charge of his property) first (Wikipedia).
- On March 21, 2024, Tennessee Governor Bill Lee signed the ELVIS Act. Under it, copying a person's voice with AI without permission is against the law in Tennessee (Governor's office). On September 17, 2024, California Governor Gavin Newsom signed two laws at the actors' union headquarters. AB 2602 requires a contract to describe how a digital replica of a living performer will be used. AB 1836 forbids using a digital replica of a dead performer in films, games and recordings without the estate's consent (Governor's office). A federal bill, the NO FAKES Act, had not passed as of September 2026 (Wikipedia).
- In September 2025 Eline Van der Velden, whose company made a computer-generated "actress" called Tilly Norwood, said talent agents wanted to represent it. The actors' union's leaders answered in a statement that Tilly Norwood "is not an actor" and was made by a computer program "trained on the work of countless professional performers," without their permission or pay (FOXBusiness, October 1, 2025).
- OpenAI's Sora 2 video app came out on September 30, 2025, and users made clips with the face and voice of the actor Bryan Cranston without his consent. After Cranston and the union complained, OpenAI's managers agreed on October 20, 2025, to require a person's permission before the app copies them. Cranston said he was "deeply concerned not just for myself, but for all performers whose work and identity can be misused in this way" (NBC News, October 20, 2025).
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-story:start slug="andy-serkis" name="Andy Serkis" movie="" kind="famous" status="verified" -->
### Andy Serkis
> **Who:** An English actor whose performance-capture work for American studios started the argument over whether a performance turned into a computer-made body is still acting · **When and where:** born April 20, 1964, played Gollum in films released from 2001 to 2003, played Caesar in the *Planet of the Apes* films from 2011 to 2017
Serkis played Gollum in *The Lord of the Rings* on set with the other actors and supplied Gollum's voice as well (Wikipedia). Artists at Weta Digital used his recorded movements and face, and they painted his body out of each shot and put the computer-made Gollum in its place. For the second and third films they redesigned Gollum's face to look more like Serkis's (Wikipedia). The film's producer, Barrie M. Osborne, argued that Serkis should be nominated for an Academy Award as an actor, because his voice, body movements and facial expressions were on screen (Wikipedia). In 2011 staff at 20th Century Fox campaigned for a nomination for his work as the ape Caesar in *Rise of the Planet of the Apes*, and the actor James Franco called him "the undisputed master of the newest kind of acting called 'motion capture'" (Wikipedia). Serkis said the apes were "infused with the heart and soul of an actor" (Leaver, 2011). He has never been nominated for an acting Oscar. In 2014 animators objected when he described their work as "painting digital makeup" onto an actor's performance, and the director Matt Reeves said the phrase had come from Weta's own artists (/Film, 2014). In 2011 Serkis opened a capture studio in London, the Imaginarium (Wikipedia).
<!-- hb-story:end slug="andy-serkis" -->
<!-- hb-story:start slug="ashley-johnson" name="Ashley Johnson" movie="" kind="famous" status="verified" -->
### Ashley Johnson
> **Who:** A child television actor who later played Ellie in the video game *The Last of Us* with her voice and her body, and won more British Academy game awards for performance than anyone else · **When and where:** born in Camarillo, California, on August 9, 1983, acting in Los Angeles from 1990
Johnson began acting at six, as Chrissy Seaver on the comedy *Growing Pains* from 1990 to 1992. By the time she was 12 she had been in the casts of eight television series (Wikipedia). She was cast as Ellie in *The Last of Us* and acted most of the part on a motion-capture stage opposite Troy Baker, who played Joel. She persuaded the director, Neil Druckmann, to rewrite Ellie as tougher and more independent. She said, "There were some days when we would shoot things that even at my age made me feel a little uncomfortable" (Wikipedia). Her own face shaped the character. After the actor Elliot Page complained that the first design of Ellie looked like Page, the studio's artists redrew Ellie to look younger and more like Johnson (Wikipedia). Johnson won the British Academy Games Award for Performer for the 2013 game and again on March 12, 2015, for its added chapter *Left Behind*, the only person to win it twice (Wikipedia). She played Ellie again in *The Last of Us Part II* (2020), and in the 2023 television series she played Ellie's mother, Anna (Wikipedia).

### Alexandria Rubalcaba
> **Who:** A background actor whose face and body were scanned into a digital copy that she was never told how anyone would use · **When and where:** Los Angeles area, on the Disney+ series *WandaVision* during the COVID-19 pandemic, told to NPR in 2023
Rubalcaba worked for four weeks as a background actor, an extra with no lines, on *WandaVision*. Then the crew sent her to a truck trailer, where dozens of background actors stepped one at a time in front of cameras on metal frames. She remembered the instructions: "Have your hands out. Have your hands in. Look this way. Look that way. Let us see your scared face. Let us see your surprised face." Each scan took about 15 minutes, and a digital copy of each actor was made from it (NPR, August 2, 2023). Nobody told her how, or whether, her copy would be used, and she would not be paid for any use of it. She earned the union rate for background actors, $187 a day, and she did not give permission for the copy to appear in any scene. "What if I don't want to be on MarioVision, or SarahVision?" she asked. "I fear that AI is eventually going to weed out background actors. They won't have any use for us anymore." Disney did not answer NPR's questions, and no record shows whether her scan was ever used (NPR). Her account came out during the 2023 actors' strike. The contract that ended it requires consent before a studio makes or uses a replica and forbids studios to use replicas in place of hiring background actors (Authors Guild summary).

### Ke Huy Quan
> **Who:** A child actor from a family of refugees who quit acting when he could not get parts as an adult Asian American actor, and came back decades later to win an Academy Award · **When and where:** born in Saigon, South Vietnam, in 1970, came to the United States in 1979, acted in Los Angeles from 1984, won the award on March 12, 2023
Quan's family was ethnic Chinese and left Vietnam three years after the war ended in 1975. Quan, his father and five of his brothers and sisters went to a refugee camp in Hong Kong, and his mother and three others went to Malaysia. In 1979 the whole family came to the United States as refugees and settled in Los Angeles (Wikipedia). At 13 he played Short Round, Harrison Ford's young sidekick, in *Indiana Jones and the Temple of Doom* (1984), and in 1985 he played Data in *The Goonies* (Wikipedia). As he grew up, "the opportunities got fewer, and the wait time longer," he said. "And the roles got smaller." In 1993 he did not hear back after an audition for a small part. "I remember sitting at the edge of my bed, completely frozen," he said. He blamed himself: "I thought I wasn't tall enough, I wasn't good-looking enough, I was not a good enough actor" (*Screen Daily*, February 27, 2023). He quit acting, studied film at the University of Southern California, and stayed away from acting for 19 years, arranging fights for *X-Men* (2000) and working as an assistant director (Wikipedia). In 2018 he watched *Crazy Rich Asians*, a hit film with an almost all-Asian cast: "I was sitting in the theatre, fantasising, wishing I was up there with them" (*Screen Daily*). He went back to acting and played Waymond Wang in *Everything Everywhere All at Once*. On March 12, 2023, he won the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor, the first actor born in Vietnam to win an Oscar (Wikipedia). He began his speech, "My journey started on a boat" (NBC News, March 15, 2023).
