<!-- hb-time:start id="before-1500" order="01" chapter="health" label="Before 1500" state="thin" progress="seed" -->
## Before 1500
<!-- hb-zoom level="era" -->
Native medicine was a body of practical knowledge — plants, setting bones, wound care, midwifery — and populations here were free of the crowd diseases that had swept Europe and Asia.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Plant medicine and no crowd diseases" -->
Plant medicine and healers; sweat lodges and other treatments; illness and injury that did exist; no smallpox, measles, or influenza.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-time:end id="before-1500" -->

<!-- hb-time:start id="1500s" order="02" chapter="health" label="The 1500s" state="full" progress="seed" -->
## The 1500s
<!-- hb-zoom level="era" -->
The deadliest event in this land's history is a wave of disease that arrives before most Europeans do.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="The Great Dying" -->
Smallpox, measles, and influenza spreading ahead of the newcomers along trade routes; the population collapse and how badly the size of it is disputed; whole towns emptied before any European saw them.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-time:end id="1500s" -->

<!-- hb-time:start id="1600s" order="03" chapter="health" label="The 1600s" state="full" progress="seed" -->
## The 1600s
<!-- hb-zoom level="era" -->
Colonists brought their diseases with them and had almost no medicine to answer them.
<!-- /hb-zoom -->
<!-- hb-zoom level="span" label="Colonial epidemics and folk medicine" -->
Smallpox epidemics in the colonies; the Starving Time and malnutrition; folk medicine, midwives, and household remedies; almost no trained doctors.

## 1700 to 1750
